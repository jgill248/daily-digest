## 1. Frontier Model News

### Anthropic Says Claude Autonomously Designed Protein Binders That Worked in the Lab for 14 of 15 Disease Targets

Anthropic published results Wednesday showing that Claude — using Opus 4.8 and an unreleased Mythos Preview model — designed functional protein binders against 14 of 15 disease targets in blind laboratory tests, per Anthropic's own research announcement, corroborated by Storyboard18, NewsBytesApp, TechBriefly, BigGo Finance, and MarketScreener. External evaluators Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience independently produced and tested Claude's designs — Claude generated 30 candidate binders per target across the 15 targets, and 22% to 35% of individual designs bound successfully depending on method, against the 10% to 15% success rate typical of protein-design campaigns today. Results included high-affinity binders against at least six targets and binders matching or exceeding the best previously reported affinity against at least four; in a separate Adaptyv Bio competition, the unreleased Mythos Preview model hit 40% against the target RBX1, versus a 3.7% hit rate among human competition participants. The same announcement disclosed that Claude Opus 5 processed raw NMR and LC-MS instrument data — used to confirm compound identity and purity — in 23 and 19 minutes respectively, work Anthropic said normally consumes the slower, more tedious part of a chemist's day even though the instrument run itself takes only minutes. Anthropic said life-science tasks remain restricted in its most capable model on safety grounds and that launching a vetted access program for scientists is a current priority, rather than opening the model broadly. *(Sources: Anthropic, Storyboard18, NewsBytesApp, TechBriefly, BigGo Finance, MarketScreener, August 19-20)*

### OpenAI Sets August 24 Launch for ChatGPT Ads Across 31 European Markets as Astra Safety Monitoring Adds Roughly 20% Compute Overhead

OpenAI said Wednesday it will begin serving ads in ChatGPT across 31 European markets — including Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, the Netherlands, and Austria — starting August 24, per OpenAI's own announcement, corroborated by Dataconomy, The Tech Outlook, Digiday, NL Times, TechXplore, and Neowin. This digest's August 17 edition already reported that OpenAI had notified EEA and Switzerland users on August 15 that ads were coming "this month"; the new disclosure fixes the exact date and market list six months after OpenAI began testing ads with US free-tier users. Ads will show only to ChatGPT's Free and Go tiers, with Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Education accounts staying ad-free; because EU law requires explicit consent for personalized targeting, OpenAI will ask users to opt in, initially routing advertisers through its Ads Solutions team and partners before a self-service Ads Manager follows later this year. Separately, and reported the same day, new figures obtained by The Register put a number on the safety-monitoring costs OpenAI outlined in the blog post covered in this digest's August 19 edition: the expanded chain-of-thought monitoring and workload isolation triggered by Astra's preliminary "Critical" cybersecurity capability finding add roughly 20% to the inference compute of affected workloads, covering all reinforcement-learning training and tool-use evaluations for GPT-5.6 Sol-class models and higher, plus all inference on Astra itself. OpenAI told The Register the added cost is internal research spend it will not pass to customers, though the outlet noted that position may be harder to sustain if OpenAI goes public as planned. *(Sources: OpenAI, Dataconomy, The Tech Outlook, Digiday, NL Times, TechXplore, Neowin, The Register, August 19)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### CISA Orders Federal Agencies to Patch a Critical, Actively Exploited Flaw in the Ray AI Framework by Today

The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency added a critical remote-code-execution vulnerability in Ray, the open-source framework Amazon, Apple, and OpenAI use to scale machine-learning workloads, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 17, giving federal civilian agencies until today, August 20, to patch, per CyberPress and GBHackers, corroborated by TheHackerNews, SecurityAffairs, and CyberUpdates365. The flaw, tracked as CVE-2025-62593 with a CVSS score of 9.4, stems from insufficient authentication on Ray's HTTP API and job-submission endpoints, which can be made to accept requests that launch arbitrary code on vulnerable Ray development environments; CISA confirmed the vulnerability is under active exploitation. The fix is to upgrade to Ray 2.52.0 or later and rebuild any container images or dependency trees that pinned an older release, since package caches can silently reintroduce the vulnerable version. The three-day remediation window is unusually tight even by CISA's standards and reflects the confirmed in-the-wild exploitation rather than a routine advisory. *(Sources: CyberPress, GBHackers, TheHackerNews, SecurityAffairs, CyberUpdates365, CISA, August 17-20)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Insurance Brokerage IMA Financial Group Says It Has Reached Enterprise-Wide AI Adoption Across Its 3,000-Plus Associates

IMA Financial Group, a North American insurance brokerage and risk-management firm, announced Tuesday that it has moved beyond pilots to enterprise-scale AI adoption, with more than 95% of its 3,000-plus associates using AI daily across thousands of agentic workflows, per the company's own announcement on GlobeNewswire, corroborated by Yahoo Finance, The Manila Times, Coverager, and The Insurer. The agents support research, analytics, document comparison, and workflow automation, freeing associates to spend less time gathering information and more time on client-facing advisory work, according to the company. IMA said the rollout is deliberately platform-agnostic rather than built around a single vendor, and is powered internally by a homegrown "AI Studio" that turns employee-proposed use cases and pilots into tools deployed firm-wide, paired with role-specific training and governance. The company did not name the underlying model providers, and the daily-usage and workflow-count figures are self-reported rather than independently audited. *(Sources: IMA Financial Group/GlobeNewswire, Yahoo Finance, The Manila Times, Coverager, The Insurer, August 18-19)*

## Key Themes

**AI moved from language into wet labs today.** Anthropic's protein-binder results are a rare case of a frontier lab publishing capability claims that were independently produced and tested by outside companies (Adaptyv Bio, Twist Bioscience) rather than benchmarked in-house — a higher evidentiary bar than most model announcements clear, even as Anthropic itself keeps its most capable model's life-science abilities gated pending a formal access program.

**Safety compliance is now a visible line item, not just a policy statement.** OpenAI's ~20% compute-overhead disclosure puts a number on what "monitoring at the frontier" costs in practice, arriving the same week the company is racing to launch monetization (ChatGPT Ads in Europe) — a company absorbing rising safety costs on one product line while opening a new revenue line on another.

**Open-source AI infrastructure is now squarely inside federal patch-management deadlines.** CISA's three-day window for the Ray vulnerability — a framework literally embedded in how Amazon, Apple, and OpenAI scale ML workloads — is a reminder that "AI security" increasingly means ordinary infrastructure security for the plumbing underneath models, not just model-level safeguards.
