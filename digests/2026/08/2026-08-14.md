## 1. Frontier Model News

### OpenAI Names Ex-Wiz Executive Dali Rajic as Chief Revenue Officer, Its Second CRO in Under a Year, as Departures Keep Climbing

OpenAI announced August 13 that Chief Revenue Officer Denise Dresser is stepping down after less than a year in the role, replaced by Dali Rajic, the former president and COO of Wiz, per Axios's original reporting, corroborated by CNBC, TechCrunch, Bloomberg, and Business Today. Rajic previously served as president and COO of Zscaler and chief customer and revenue officer at AppDynamics before joining Wiz, which Google acquired earlier this year; OpenAI co-founder and president Greg Brockman announced the hire in a company blog post. The move lands one day after this digest covered COO Brad Lightcap's exit, and follows Fidji Simo's July departure from her "CEO of AGI deployment" role — the third senior executive change in as many weeks. Fortune's August 13 reporting frames the pattern starkly: of the wave of senior women OpenAI hired into top roles over the past two years, almost all are now gone. Brockman has taken on a larger day-to-day management role since Simo's exit. Coverage across outlets ties the timing to OpenAI's intensifying preparations for an expected 2027 IPO, though no outlet has reported the departures themselves are IPO-related beyond that contextual framing. *(Sources: Axios, CNBC, TechCrunch, Bloomberg, Fortune, Business Today, August 13)*

### OpenAI Previews "Ultrafast": GPT-5.6 Sol Now Runs at Up to 750 Tokens Per Second via a New Cerebras Partnership

OpenAI previewed a new "Ultrafast" processing mode for GPT-5.6 Sol on August 13, running the model on Cerebras hardware at up to 750 output tokens per second — as much as 14 times the speed of standard processing — per OpenAI's own announcement, corroborated by Cerebras's technical blog, 9to5Mac, and TechCrunch. The mode is in limited preview to a select group of customers, with OpenAI saying access will expand as Cerebras capacity grows; pricing has not yet been disclosed, though standard GPT-5.6 Sol runs $5/$30 per million input/output tokens for comparison. Cerebras frames the partnership as proof its wafer-scale chips can serve a frontier lab's flagship model at production scale, not just smaller open-weight models as in prior Cerebras inference deals. The move continues a pattern this month of frontier labs competing on raw speed and latency for agentic workloads, not just benchmark scores — a category Nvidia is separately targeting with its own cost-focused routing tools (below). *(Sources: OpenAI, Cerebras, 9to5Mac, TechCrunch, August 13)*

### Google Ships Gemini 3.7 Flash, a Coding-Focused Update That Beats Rival Models on Benchmarks Just Three Weeks After 3.6 Flash

Google released Gemini 3.7 Flash on August 13, its most capable entry-level model yet, pitched at coding, agentic workflows, and knowledge work, per Google's own release notes, corroborated by SiliconANGLE, Artificial Analysis, and officechai. Google attributes the gains to algorithmic changes in the model's reasoning foundation rather than a larger parameter count; the model handles up to 1 million tokens of combined text, image, and video input per prompt with responses up to 64,000 tokens. Google says it beats comparable Anthropic and OpenAI models across nine benchmarks, including 43.6% on FrontierCode 1.1 Main and 65.3% on DeepSWE v1.1, up from 49.0% for the prior Flash version — those figures are Google's own benchmark claims and have not been independently reproduced here. Introductory pricing is $0.75/$3.75 per million input/output tokens, rising to $1.50/$7.50 on January 1, 2027. Landing three weeks after 3.6 Flash and one day after xAI's Grok 4.6, it's the latest entry in this month's rapid-fire Flash-tier release cadence among frontier labs. *(Sources: Google, SiliconANGLE, Artificial Analysis, officechai, August 13)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Nvidia's NeMo Switchyard Router Cuts Agentic AI Costs by Up to 74% — and Cognition Is Already Using It in Production

Nvidia launched NeMo Switchyard on August 11 alongside its new Nemotron 3.5 Lightning model, an open-source router that dynamically shifts an agent's workload between cheaper and more powerful models mid-task based on complexity, error rates, and stagnation signals, per Nvidia's own developer blog, corroborated by VentureBeat, LangChain's benchmark writeup, and XenoSpectrum. In Nvidia's internal tests, the router held near-frontier accuracy while cutting task-completion cost to roughly a third of running everything through Claude Opus 4.8; a separate LangChain multi-turn agent benchmark found only 7% of requests needed a top-tier model, reducing cost by about 74% compared to an all-frontier baseline. Cognition has already integrated Switchyard's staged routing into Devin Desktop, reporting near-frontier results on its FrontierCode Main benchmark while cutting mean cost 28% versus routing everything to a single frontier model — a company-reported figure from Nvidia's own materials, not independently verified here. It's a direct answer to the token-cost anxiety this digest has tracked all month, arriving as tooling rather than a policy response. *(Sources: Nvidia, VentureBeat, LangChain, XenoSpectrum, August 11)*

### xAI Launches Grok Bot, Always-On AI Agents That Get Their Own Cloud Computer

xAI launched Grok Bot in beta on August 11, a product built around assigning each AI agent its own persistent cloud computer that can sign into a customer's existing tools, navigate software and websites, and complete multi-step jobs unsupervised, per xAI's own materials, corroborated by Unite.AI, eWeek, and ithinkdiff. Users assign each bot a specific role — xAI's internal examples include a sales-outbound bot that researches accounts and drafts emails overnight and a demo-readiness bot that fixes broken environments while the team sleeps — and can close their laptop mid-task without interrupting the agent. Access is bundled into existing SuperGrok Heavy, Cursor Ultra, and Cursor Teams Premium subscriptions rather than sold separately, with apps live on macOS, Windows, Linux, and iOS and Android listed as coming soon. It follows the same distribution-bundling playbook this digest flagged in Grok 4.6's same-day Cursor placement last week — folding new capability into subscriptions customers already pay for rather than launching a standalone product. *(Sources: xAI, Unite.AI, eWeek, ithinkdiff, August 11)*

### Anthropic Flips Claude Code to Autonomous "Auto Mode" by Default Today, Citing a 97% Rubber-Stamp Rate on Manual Approvals

Auto mode became the default permission setting for new Claude Code sessions on Pro, Max, and Team plans today, August 14, as Anthropic previously announced, per Anthropic's own documentation, corroborated by DEV Community, The Register, and Hacker News discussion threads. Rather than prompting for approval before each action, a classifier now screens every tool call and blocks only those judged irreversible, destructive, or aimed outside the user's own environment; Anthropic says the change is grounded in internal data showing 97% of permission prompts get approved regardless of content, suggesting most users click through reflexively rather than reviewing each command. In Anthropic's controlled study, the classifier caught roughly 89% of planted dangerous commands versus 13.6% for human reviewers — figures this digest first reported August 12 — and independent red-team testing cited today found a 7–11% miss rate on adversarial attempts, the first outside validation of that number reported. Enterprise accounts, the API, and cloud-partner deployments remain opt-in for now, and existing users who haven't touched their permission settings get a one-time prompt before the switch takes effect. *(Sources: Anthropic, DEV Community, The Register, Hacker News, August 14)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Google's Gemini App Crosses 1 Billion Monthly Active Users, the Fastest-Growing Product in the Company's 28-Year History

Google CEO Sundar Pichai announced August 11 that the Gemini app has surpassed 1 billion monthly active users, calling it the fastest-growing product in Google's history and the 14th Google service to reach that scale, per Google's own blog post, corroborated by TechCrunch, 9to5Google, and The Information. The app grew from 400 million MAU in May 2025 to 650 million by October, 900 million in May 2026, and 950 million by late July, before crossing 1 billion in the roughly three weeks since — a growth curve Google contrasts with Gmail's roughly 14 years and YouTube's roughly eight years to reach comparable scale. The milestone puts Gemini alongside ChatGPT, which crossed 1 billion MAU in June 2026, meaning two frontier labs now claim billion-user consumer AI products within two months of each other. Google's announcement did not break out paid-subscriber counts separately from the total, a detail TechTimes flagged as a notable omission given the company's emphasis on the headline number. *(Sources: Google, TechCrunch, 9to5Google, The Information, TechTimes, August 11)*

### Salesforce's Agentic Enterprise Index Finds AI Agent Deployments Nearly Tripled Year-Over-Year, With Retail Online Sales Growing 4x Faster

Salesforce published the second edition of its Agentic Enterprise Index on August 10, analyzing Agentforce usage among businesses consistently running agents from February 2025 through April 2026, per Salesforce's own report, corroborated by CXToday, Futurum Group, and Enterprise Times. The company-reported figures show activated agents nearly tripled year-over-year, with average deployment time down 53% and businesses now running about 13 agents on average; retailers using agents saw 4x higher online sales growth than non-adopters, and agent skill sets expanded up to 350% during peak shopping periods to handle more complex, multi-step requests. Salesforce also reported that "agentic work units" — its internal measure of discrete tasks completed by an agent — grew at a 15% compound monthly rate as of April 2026. As a vendor-commissioned report measuring usage of Salesforce's own product, these figures should be read as company-reported rather than independently audited, but they add hard usage data to a month of AI-ROI claims that have often relied on surveys instead. *(Sources: Salesforce, CXToday, Futurum Group, Enterprise Times, August 10)*

### Cloudflare Discloses Machine-to-Machine AI Traffic Topped 50% of Its Network for the First Time, Fueling an Enterprise Software Stock Rally

Cloudflare's CEO disclosed on the company's August 13 earnings call that non-human, machine-to-machine AI traffic accounted for more than half of Cloudflare's total network volume for the first time in the company's history, driven by enterprise customers consolidating legacy point solutions into its unified network architecture, per StockStory's earnings coverage, corroborated by StartupHub.ai's markets desk. The disclosure landed alongside broader enterprise-software strength that day: monday.com gained 9.7%, MongoDB climbed 7.9%, and Cloudflare itself added 6.2%, as earnings beats across the sector and soft producer-price data pushed the S&P 500 to a record close. StartupHub.ai's markets coverage frames the rally as agentic AI adoption reshuffling sector leadership, with enterprise SaaS names outpacing semiconductor hardware stocks in the short run. It's a market-level echo of the usage data in Salesforce's index above — investors rewarding evidence of actual agent traffic and deployment, not just announced capability. *(Sources: StockStory, StartupHub.ai, August 13)*

## Key Themes

**Two billion-user milestones inside two months mean consumer AI adoption has stopped being a story about growth rates and become a story about which apps have already won.** Gemini crossing 1 billion MAU, alongside ChatGPT's June milestone, suggests the leading consumer AI products are now converging on comparable scale even as the labs behind them remain locked in a tight capability race — distribution and raw usage numbers are becoming as important a competitive signal as benchmark scores.

**OpenAI's executive churn is no longer an isolated event but a pattern with its own throughline.** Denise Dresser's exit after less than a year, following Brad Lightcap's departure last week and Fidji Simo's in July, is compelling enough that Fortune is now writing about it as a trend — nearly all of the senior women OpenAI hired into top roles over the past two years are gone — rather than covering each departure as a standalone story.

**The agentic-cost-anxiety story this digest has tracked all month is starting to produce tooling answers, not just policy ones.** Where earlier coverage showed CIOs building usage caps and per-engineer budgets, Nvidia's NeMo Switchyard router and its early adoption by Cognition point to a technical fix — routing work to cheaper models automatically — arriving in the same weeks as the budget-cap trend, addressing the same underlying problem from a different direction.

**Hard usage and traffic data continues to be more persuasive than survey data on the "is agentic AI real" question.** Salesforce's tripled-deployment figures and Cloudflare's disclosure that machine-to-machine traffic now exceeds half its network volume are both company-reported, but they describe actual measured activity rather than sentiment — and markets responded to Cloudflare's number with a same-day rally, a more concrete reaction than survey-driven coverage has generally produced this month.
