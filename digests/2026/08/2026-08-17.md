## 1. Frontier Model News

### Anthropic's Second Risk Report Raises Its Own Misalignment Rating From "Very Low" to "Low" and Discloses an Unreleased Model More Capable Than Mythos 5

Anthropic published its second company-wide Risk Report on August 14, raising its rated risk of catastrophic harm from model misalignment in high-stakes settings from "very low" to "low," per the report on Anthropic's own site, corroborated by SiliconANGLE, Unite.AI, TechTimes, and Yahoo. The company attributes the change to "general increased uncertainty" following recent disclosures about model behavior in cybersecurity evaluations, rather than any single failed test, and the report — covering February 24 through July 15, 2026 under version 3.4 of Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy — separately discloses an internal model called Model 2 that Anthropic says is somewhat more capable than its frontier Mythos 5, with no current plans to release it externally because it hasn't completed the company's standard predeployment assessment suite. Anthropic also flagged that an internal benchmark designed to detect dangerous capability thresholds has saturated — it can no longer register incremental gains — just as the company says it's seeing early signs of the capability acceleration that benchmark was built to catch. It's Anthropic voluntarily raising its own risk rating on its own methodology, a rarer move than the pattern of external incidents (UK AISI's deception findings, Meta's and OpenAI's sandbox breaches) that drove most of this summer's safety disclosures. *(Sources: Anthropic, SiliconANGLE, Unite.AI, TechTimes, Yahoo, August 14)*

### Anthropic's Preliminary Q2 Revenue Tops $11.5 Billion, a 14-Fold Jump, as It Meets Bankers Ahead of a Fall IPO

Anthropic told investors its preliminary Q2 2026 revenue exceeded $11.5 billion — more than 14 times Q2 2025's $787 million and over double Q1 2026's $4.73 billion — with positive adjusted operating income for the quarter, per Bloomberg's original reporting, corroborated by CNBC, Fortune, and Yahoo Finance. The disclosure lands as Anthropic works with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan Chase on a potential IPO, with the company meeting investors who are reportedly modeling a valuation north of $2 trillion for an October listing — a figure this digest flagged as investor speculation on August 13 and which remains unconfirmed by Anthropic itself. The revenue figures are preliminary and could still change before any formal filing. It's the clearest sign yet of the revenue growth underpinning that IPO speculation, arriving the same week Anthropic separately raised its own safety-risk rating (above). *(Sources: Bloomberg, CNBC, Fortune, Yahoo Finance, August 14–15)*

### DeepSeek Raises API Prices by Up to 1,100% Starting August 16, Ending Its Run as the Reliable Low-Cost Option

DeepSeek began charging sharply higher API prices for its V4 Pro and V4 Flash models at 16:00 UTC on August 16, with increases ranging from roughly 50% to more than 1,100% depending on the model, token type, and time of day, per Bloomberg's original reporting, corroborated by Fortune, Engadget, InfoWorld, and Qz. The company introduced a new peak/off-peak structure — peak hours running 01:00–04:00 and 06:00–10:00 UTC at full price, off-peak at half — with V4 Pro output tokens rising to $3.96 per million at peak (more than 4x the prior flat $0.87 rate) and V4 Flash output rising to $1.32 per million at peak versus a prior flat $0.28. DeepSeek said the changes are meant "to allocate resources more reasonably" and to shift developer workloads toward less congested periods, framing this as a capacity problem rather than a margin play; even after the hikes, DeepSeek's rates remain below several closed-source frontier models. Developers reacted publicly by noting that, at peak pricing against the right comparator, DeepSeek's cost advantage over competitors like Meta's Muse Spark and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Luna "disappears, and in places inverts," according to analyst Sanchit Vir Gogia. It's a concrete break from the assumption — flagged by multiple outlets as a broader industry inflection point — that generative-AI API prices only go down. *(Sources: Bloomberg, Fortune, Engadget, InfoWorld, Qz, August 13–16)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Stripe Finalizes a $7B+ Deal for AI Gateway OpenRouter, Beating Out a PayPal Bid and Raising Neutrality Questions

Stripe has finalized an agreement to acquire OpenRouter, the AI model gateway startup, for more than $7 billion — over five times the $1.3 billion valuation OpenRouter carried after a $113 million Series B just three months earlier — per Bloomberg's original reporting, corroborated by TechCrunch, financefeeds, and KuCoin. OpenRouter routes traffic across 400+ models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and DeepSeek for roughly 8 million developers, doing only scheduling and billing rather than selling its own models, and reportedly processed about 1.5 quadrillion tokens over the past year; the deal beat out a competing bid from PayPal, per financefeeds. Neither company has issued an official announcement, and the final price could still change. OpenRouter's core value proposition to developers has always been strict neutrality among model vendors — not favoring any one lab — and multiple outlets flagged that this now sits inside a payments company that earns money on transaction volume, an ownership structure some developers worry could eventually bias routing decisions even if none is intended today. It's one of the largest deals yet in the fast-growing "AI infrastructure middleman" category, and a payments company buying the plumbing that decides which model gets a given request. *(Sources: Bloomberg, TechCrunch, financefeeds, KuCoin, August 16)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### An AI Store Manager Fires Its First Human Employee — After Being Reminded of Its Own Policy

Andon Labs disclosed this week that Luna, an AI agent built on Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 4.6 that has run Andon Market — an experimental San Francisco retail store the company opened April 1 with a $100,000 budget, internet access, and a corporate credit card — dismissed a human employee for the first time, after the worker arrived late for 17 of 23 scheduled shifts, per Inc., corroborated by The Next Web, American Bazaar, HyperAI, and Ground News. Logs show Luna had already issued warnings and arranged extra training but failed to act on the repeated lateness for months because it had lost track of its own attendance policy; a human at Andon Labs prompted Luna to review its policies and reconsider the employee's fit, after which the AI recommended "parting ways," and a human formally reviewed and carried out the decision. Andon Labs says it directly employs every worker at the store on guaranteed pay with full legal protections, so no one's livelihood depended solely on the AI's judgment. Outlets are describing it as the first documented case of an LLM making a termination call in a live workplace, distinct from the many AI-cited layoffs this year that were human management decisions informed by AI economics rather than an AI agent's own recommendation. *(Sources: Inc., The Next Web, American Bazaar, HyperAI, Ground News, August 15–16)*

### OpenAI Will Bring Ads to Free and Go-Tier ChatGPT Across Europe This Month

OpenAI Ireland emailed Free and Go plan users across the EEA and Switzerland on August 15 confirming that ads will begin appearing in ChatGPT later this month, per ppc.land's reporting on the notice, corroborated by OpenAI's own Help Center documentation. Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Education accounts remain ad-free; at launch, ad selection in the EEA and Switzerland will not use personalization — the system will rely only on the current conversation's topic plus limited contextual signals like general location and device type, excluding past chats and memories, a narrower approach than OpenAI is expected to take in markets outside Europe's stricter privacy regime. It's OpenAI moving to monetize the free tier of the product that crossed 1 billion monthly active users' rival milestone territory this month (Google's Gemini hit 1 billion August 11, as this digest covered August 14), converting scale into an ad business for the first time at a scale that will touch a large share of Europe's free ChatGPT users. *(Sources: ppc.land, OpenAI Help Center, August 15)*

## Key Themes

**Anthropic had one of its more self-contradictory weeks of the year: raising its own safety-risk rating and disclosing a shelved, more-capable model on the same day its revenue numbers gave investors their strongest case yet for a mega-IPO.** A voluntary "very low" to "low" misalignment-risk upgrade and an unreleased Model 2 sitting on the shelf for incomplete safety testing landed August 14 — the same window in which Anthropic's preliminary $11.5 billion Q2 revenue (14x year-over-year) became the central data point for bankers modeling a $2 trillion-plus valuation. Capability, caution, and commercial momentum are advancing together rather than trading off against each other.

**The assumption that AI API prices only fall broke this week, and DeepSeek was the one that broke it.** A lab that built its reputation as the low-cost, high-capability alternative to closed frontier models raised some prices over 1,000%, explicitly citing capacity constraints rather than margin — a reminder that "AI is getting cheaper" was always a function of subsidized capacity, not a law of the category, and developers are already recalculating whether DeepSeek's cost edge still holds at peak hours.

**Two stories this week are both, in different ways, about who profits from being the neutral layer between a user and an AI model.** Stripe's $7 billion-plus purchase of OpenRouter buys the plumbing that routes developer requests across 400+ models without favoring any one lab — a position now owned by a company that makes money on transaction volume — while OpenAI's move to put ads in free ChatGPT in Europe monetizes the attention of users who, unlike OpenRouter's developers, aren't choosing between competing models at all.

**A single retail experiment produced what may be the first documented case of an AI agent's own recommendation ending someone's job, and the guardrails that made it survivable are worth noting.** Andon Labs' Luna didn't fire anyone unilaterally — it took a human prompt to get Luna to revisit its own forgotten policy, and a human reviewed and executed the final call, with the worker on guaranteed pay and full legal protection throughout. It's a small-scale, deliberately observed test case rather than proof of how AI management will work at scale, but it's a concrete data point in a year where "AI cited in layoffs" has mostly meant humans making AI-informed decisions, not AI agents making the call themselves.
