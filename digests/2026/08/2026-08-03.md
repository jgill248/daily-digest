## 1. Frontier Model News

### OpenAI's Unreleased Astra Model Solves Ten Open Problems in Math and Theoretical Computer Science

OpenAI announced August 1 that an internal, unreleased version of its next model, Astra, independently solved ten previously open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science, publishing machine-checkable Lean 4 proofs on GitHub for roughly $2,000 in total compute, per OpenAI's own research post, corroborated by TechTimes, SiliconANGLE, TheNextWeb, and DataCamp. The headline result is the first explicit construction of a non-sofic group, resolving a question that had stood open since Mikhail Gromov defined soficity in 1999; the other nine include a counterexample to Connes's rigidity conjecture, improved sphere-packing bounds reaching the Cohn–Elkies threshold, exponentially stronger binary and spherical code bounds, an n⁴/log n lower bound for the permanent, and a superexponential lower bound for multicolor Ramsey numbers that resolves Erdős Problem 183. Every proof ships with a Lean certificate and a "sorry" count of zero, meaning no step was left unproven, and Fields Medal winner Timothy Gowers said he would recommend one of the proofs for a top journal without hesitation. It's the clearest evidence yet that frontier labs are now producing genuinely novel research results, not just passing benchmarks — and it lands the same week a second Fields medalist, Jacob Tsimerman, joined OpenAI specifically to work on AI safety (below). *(Sources: OpenAI, TechTimes, SiliconANGLE, TheNextWeb, DataCamp, August 1–2)*

### Amazon Scraps Most of Its Nova Model Lineup, Bets Everything on One New Frontier Model

Amazon is winding down active development on Nova Premier, Nova Omni, Nova Reel, and Nova Canvas — moving them into "keep the lights on" maintenance mode — and redirecting compute and talent to a single new effort called Frontier Model Research (FMR), led by Pieter Abbeel (who joined Amazon via its 2024 acquisition of Covariant), per TheStreet, corroborated by TheNextWeb, the-decoder.com, Technology.org, and Futurism. Peter DeSantis, who took over Amazon's consolidated AI group after Rohit Prasad's late-2025 departure, has been concentrating scarce compute on fewer, higher-stakes bets rather than the broad Nova family Amazon launched at re:Invent 2024; Nova 2 Lite, Nova 2 Sonic, and the customer-facing Nova Forge platform remain active, and a new flagship model — possibly still under the Nova name — is expected to debut at re:Invent this fall. The reset is a striking admission, arriving in the same week Amazon posted a $53.4 billion paper gain from its Anthropic stake and its best AWS growth in 18 quarters (covered in this digest July 31): Amazon's own model-building effort hasn't kept pace with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, even as its infrastructure and investment bets in those same labs pay off handsomely. *(Sources: TheStreet, TheNextWeb, the-decoder.com, Technology.org, Futurism, July 29–30)*

### UPDATE: White House's August 1 Deadline for a Frontier AI Review Framework Passes With No Public Deliverables

The 60-day clock set by President Trump's June 2 executive order (EO 14409) — tracked in this digest July 30 as "nearing finalization" — expired August 1 with none of its three required deliverables published: no classified benchmarking process from NSA/CISA/NIST, no voluntary frontier-AI disclosure framework from Treasury/NSA/CISA/NIST, and no statement from the Office of Science and Technology Policy, per Yahoo Finance, corroborated by TechTimes. The silence is notable given how much motion surrounded the deadline all week: OpenAI and Anthropic formally endorsed a "pacing" framework as company policy (covered July 30), and a separate, longer-running program called TRAINS — under which OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, and xAI already give the Commerce Department's Center for AI Standards and Innovation early access to new models for security testing — continues to operate independently of the stalled executive-order deadline. The gap between labs publicly asking for government pacing infrastructure and the government actually delivering it on its own deadline is now the story. *(Sources: Yahoo Finance, TechTimes, July 29–August 1)*

### Fields Medalist Jacob Tsimerman's Move From a University of Toronto Professorship to OpenAI Draws Wider Coverage

A Wall Street Journal profile by Ben Cohen, published August 2 and flagged widely via Techmeme, detailed mathematician Jacob Tsimerman's decision to take leave from the University of Toronto — where he has taught since completing a PhD at Princeton and postdoctoral work at Harvard — to work on AI safety at OpenAI in San Francisco, corroborated by BetaKit and Yellow. Tsimerman, who won the 2026 Fields Medal at ICM Philadelphia on July 23 for his role in proving the André-Oort conjecture, says he believes AI will soon outperform human mathematicians and could accelerate the field roughly 100-fold; he keeps his U of T faculty post and says he has no firm plan past the next year. His move — coming the same week OpenAI published Astra's ten machine-verified proofs (above) — is a concrete data point in a pattern this digest has tracked repeatedly: top research talent moving toward frontier labs specifically to work on safety, not capabilities, even as those same labs' capabilities keep advancing. *(Sources: Wall Street Journal via Techmeme, BetaKit, Yellow, July 23–August 2)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Y Combinator Open-Sources QM, an MIT-Licensed Multiplayer Agent Harness It Runs Its Own Company On

Y Combinator released QM on July 31 — a cloud-first, MIT-licensed "multiplayer agent harness for work," built for whole-company use rather than individual coding tasks, per YC's own announcement on X, corroborated by Dealroom.co and Startup Fortune. YC says it already runs QM across its accounting, legal, events, and engineering functions, including using it to build QM itself; the tool gives each employee and each "room" (a shared project space) scoped memory, files, a keychain, permissions, scheduled crons, shareable web-app artifacts, and a durable sandbox, natively integrated with Slack and a web UI. Notably, QM is model-agnostic — employees can swap between Pi, OpenCode, Codex, and Claude Code without vendor lock-in — positioning it as infrastructure for the kind of company-wide, non-coding agent use that AWS's AgentCore and Microsoft's coming Copilot "super app" (both covered in this digest July 30–31) are chasing from the enterprise-platform side; QM approaches the same problem as an open-source, single-company-tested harness instead. *(Sources: Y Combinator (X), Dealroom.co, Startup Fortune, July 31)*

### Thinking Machines Lab Ships Inkling-Small, a Distilled Open-Weight Model That Beats Its Own Teacher

Thinking Machines Lab — Mira Murati's startup — released Inkling-Small on August 2, an open-weights Mixture-of-Experts model with 276 billion total parameters and 12 billion active (about a quarter the size of its July 15 parent model, Inkling, which carries 975 billion total and 41 billion active parameters), per MarkTechPost, corroborated by the company's own model card. Despite being distilled from Inkling, Inkling-Small surpasses its teacher model on reasoning and agentic coding benchmarks. It continues the company's stated bet against one-size-fits-all frontier chatbots: both Inkling models are explicitly not positioned as the strongest models available, but as bases meant to be fine-tuned on a customer's own data via Thinking Machines' commercial Tinker platform to outperform generic frontier models on specific work — a smaller-model, specialization-first counterpoint to the same week's giant-model headlines from OpenAI and Amazon (above). *(Sources: MarkTechPost, Thinking Machines Lab, August 2)*

### DeepSeek Moves V4-Flash Out of Preview and Into Public API Beta

DeepSeek published DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 to Hugging Face and moved the official V4-Flash API into public beta on July 31, with the model card confirming this release supersedes the earlier preview on an unchanged architecture and parameter count, per MarkTechPost, corroborated by Artificial Analysis and BenchLM.ai. The upgraded release brings notable agentic and coding gains, scores 50 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (against a same-size open-weight median of 25), and generates at 122.7 tokens per second — nearly double the open-weight median of 62.7 t/s — while staying priced at $0.14 per million input tokens on a cache miss and $0.28 per million output tokens. It's the second consecutive week a major Chinese open-weight lab has shipped a formal, competitively priced release (following prior digests' coverage of DeepSeek and Qwen updates), keeping pressure on OpenAI's own price cuts — GPT-5.6 Luna dropped 80% in the same window, per multiple outlets — as open-weight models close the gap on cost and throughput. *(Sources: MarkTechPost, Artificial Analysis, BenchLM.ai, July 31)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### A Field Report From OpenAI and Academic Partners Shows Coding Agents Can Modernize Neglected Research Software — With a Catch

OpenAI, alongside academic research groups, published a field report (first surfaced around July 27–28 and still generating fresh coverage as of August 3) documenting eight case studies — mostly in genomics, immunology, and RNA sequencing — where scientists used coding agents like Codex and Claude Code to modernize old, neglected research software, per OpenAI's own post, corroborated by The Decoder, TechTimes, and Artificial Intelligence News. Results include a 60x speedup in RNA-sequencing quality control, a from-scratch Rust rewrite of a 20,000-line C/C++ genome aligner achieving 99.8% parity with the original, and a GPU-native redesign that cut synthetic genome generation from 1,610 seconds to 27 seconds per run. The catch, emphasized throughout: agents executed well-defined engineering tasks reliably but could not judge whether their own output was scientifically correct, sometimes presenting flawed code with full confidence. Philip Ewels, who led one of the rewrites, described the agents as "eloquent, convincing, and confidently wrong in ways that are easy to miss" — meaning human scientists remained essential for verification and long-term ownership even as the agents did the bulk of the engineering labor. *(Sources: OpenAI, The Decoder, TechTimes, Artificial Intelligence News, July 27–August 3)*

### Verizon's AI Automation Stack Nears Completion — 85% of Network Issues Now Resolve Autonomously, Alongside 16,600+ Job Cuts

Verizon CEO Dan Schulman said the company's AI tech stack, built with Google and Anthropic, is on track to be substantially complete by this fall, and that AI-driven automation now autonomously resolves 85% of network issues before customers notice them, delivering more than $200 million in energy savings, per TheStreet, corroborated by Data Center Dynamics and Yahoo Finance. The same initiative Schulman calls "the most transformative in Verizon's recent history" has coincided with more than 16,600 positions cut or moved off payroll since he took over in October 2025, with a new WARN filing disclosing 121 additional layoffs at Verizon's Basking Ridge, New Jersey headquarters effective August 7; Schulman maintains the cuts reflect a broader $5 billion cost-savings target rather than a direct AI substitution, though analysts covering the story question whether the two are really separable. It's a concrete, named-company counterpart to this week's OpenAI research-software story (above): one shows AI amplifying human scientists who remain firmly in the loop, the other shows AI automation directly correlated with a shrinking human workforce at enterprise scale. *(Sources: TheStreet, Data Center Dynamics, Yahoo Finance, July 19–August 1)*

## Key Themes

**Frontier labs are now producing verifiable research output, not just benchmark scores — and recruiting top human researchers specifically to keep watch on it.** OpenAI's Astra independently solved ten machine-verified open math problems the same week a second Fields medalist, Jacob Tsimerman, left a tenured professorship to work on AI safety at OpenAI — capability and safety talent both accelerating in the same lab, in the same week.

**Government AI oversight is falling behind the pace labs themselves are setting.** The White House's own 60-day deadline for a frontier-model review framework lapsed August 1 with zero public deliverables, even as OpenAI and Anthropic have already made "pacing" a formal corporate policy (covered July 30) and a separate pre-existing program, TRAINS, quietly continues doing early-access security testing without the stalled executive order's involvement — three different tracks of "government oversight of AI" moving at three different speeds.

**Even Amazon is admitting its own model-building hasn't kept pace with the labs it invests in.** Scrapping most of the Nova lineup to make one concentrated bet under Pieter Abbeel, in the same week Amazon posted a $53.4 billion paper gain from its Anthropic stake, is Amazon choosing to keep winning through infrastructure and investment while conceding it needs a reset on frontier models built in-house.

**Real-world AI deployment this week shows two very different relationships between humans and agents.** OpenAI's research-software field report found agents that execute reliably but can't judge their own scientific correctness — keeping human scientists essential — while Verizon's network-automation stack autonomously resolves 85% of issues and has coincided with over 16,600 job cuts, a reminder that "AI augments humans" and "AI replaces humans" are both playing out simultaneously, just in different workflows.
