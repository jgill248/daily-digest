## 1. Frontier Model News

### OpenAI Shuts Down Sora and Atlas, Pares Back Disney Partnership to Refocus Compute on Next-Generation Models

Sam Altman told TIME, in an interview published today, that OpenAI has spent recent months winding down projects it now sees as distractions from its core mission — including the Sora video-generation app, the standalone Atlas web browser, and a scaled-back version of its partnership with Disney — per TIME's original reporting, corroborated by Yahoo News Canada's republication and Dealroom.co's summary of the interview. Altman said the moves, driven under co-founder and president Greg Brockman, reflect a decision to "concentrate our compute and our product capacity" on OpenAI's next generation of models and automated-research systems, a family the company has previewed internally as Astra. On Sora and Disney specifically, Altman told Variety and The Hollywood Reporter in earlier interviews this year that he personally called new Disney CEO Josh D'Amaro to explain the Sora shutdown and felt "terrible" doing so, but that the two companies are still working to find a different way to collaborate. Separately, OpenAI completed the previously announced retirement of the o3 model family from ChatGPT today, ending a 90-day sunset period that began in May; the change affects only ChatGPT's model picker, not the API, and o3-pro remains available to paying subscribers. Taken together, the moves mark one of the clearest public acknowledgments yet from OpenAI leadership that the company overextended into consumer products and is now prioritizing the compute-intensive frontier race over product breadth. *(Sources: TIME, Yahoo News Canada, Dealroom.co, Variety, The Hollywood Reporter, August 26)*

### OpenAI Disrupts Russian Covert Influence Campaign That Used ChatGPT to Mask Its Origin

OpenAI disclosed it banned a cluster of ChatGPT accounts behind a Russian-run covert influence operation that used the model to generate social media content for a fake Israeli-branded think tank, per OpenAI's own report, corroborated by CNBC, Tom's Hardware, and IBTimes. The operators accessed ChatGPT from Russia through VPNs — circumventing OpenAI's restriction on access from that country — and prompted the model in Russian to produce English-language posts that avoided linguistic markers of Russian origin, which were then spread across Substack, Telegram, X, Facebook, and LinkedIn. The campaign's central vehicle, a group calling itself the "International Burke Institute," published a "sovereignty index" ranking Russia favorably against Western countries and hosted articles that copied real academic writing, sometimes with false attribution. OpenAI said the operation's actual reach stayed small but that its VPN-based, multi-platform infrastructure could have scaled significantly, and it described the campaign as the most elaborate Russia-linked influence operation it has disrupted to date. *(Sources: OpenAI, CNBC, Tom's Hardware, IBTimes, August 25)*

### Bain & Company Becomes First Consultancy to Reach Anthropic's Top Partner Tier

Bain & Company and Anthropic announced a global partnership placing Bain in the Claude Partner Network at Global Premier, the program's highest tier, per Bain's own announcement, corroborated by MarketScreener, Unite.AI, and Artiverse. Bain has rolled Claude out across its entire 19,000-person workforce, with consultants using Claude.ai, Claude Cowork, Claude Code, Claude for Excel, and Claude for Microsoft 365 for both client engagements and internal research and modeling work. Under the partnership, Bain will work with Anthropic on client engagements spanning AI strategy, technology modernization, and AI-enabled operations, aimed at moving enterprise clients from pilot-stage experimentation to scaled deployment. Reaching Global Premier status requires at least 1,000 active certified individuals, 100 deployed joint customers across three or more regions, 15 public customer case studies, and a joint business plan with named executive sponsors — thresholds Anthropic has been building out since opening the Claude Partner Network's services track earlier this year with an initial $100 million commitment. *(Sources: Bain & Company, MarketScreener, Unite.AI, Artiverse, August 25)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Thomson Reuters Launches Thomson, Its First Proprietary Large Language Model

Thomson Reuters launched Thomson, a proprietary large language model trained on decades of Westlaw, Practical Law, Checkpoint, and Reuters content, per the company's own announcement, corroborated by SiliconANGLE, LawSites, Law.com, and Artificial Lawyer. The company said it spent roughly $40 million developing the model — covering talent and compute — starting from an open-source base model, then realigning it with Thomson Reuters' values, pretraining it on the company's proprietary content, and post-training it with guidance from legal and tax professionals plus reinforcement learning that taught it to operate Thomson Reuters' own tools like Westlaw and Practical Law. The final training run itself cost about $450,000, a small fraction of the total build cost. Thomson's first deployment is inside Tabular Analysis, a high-volume document-review feature of the company's CoCounsel Legal AI assistant that lets attorneys review up to 10,000 documents and ask up to 100 questions at once, returning results in a filterable table — a use case Thomson Reuters says benefits from a model trained specifically on legal-domain data rather than a general-purpose frontier model. *(Sources: Thomson Reuters, SiliconANGLE, LawSites, Law.com, Artificial Lawyer, August 24)*

### Google Rolls Out "Ask Gemini" as a Unified AI Command Line Inside Google Chat

Google began rolling out Ask Gemini in Chat today, a feature that embeds a Gemini-powered command line directly into Google Chat, per Google's own Workspace Updates announcement, corroborated by Neowin and TechRadar. Built on Google's Workspace Intelligence layer — which grounds Gemini's answers in a user's Gmail, Drive, Calendar, and Chat data — the feature lets users search across their Workspace footprint, draft updates or generate images without leaving a conversation, catch up on missed threads, and manage tasks and events from within Chat, replacing the product's previous side panel. The rollout covers Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus, and Google AI Pro for Education tiers, phasing in over as long as 15 days per Google's standard visibility ramp. Workspace customers get promotional access to higher usage limits through October 1, after which standard usage limits apply — a signal Google expects the feature's usage-based costs to matter once the introductory period ends. *(Sources: Google Workspace Updates, Neowin, TechRadar, August 26)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Starling Bank Launches the UK's First Agentic AI Assistant for Business Banking Customers

Starling Bank rolled out Starling Assistant, an agentic AI tool, to all of its business-banking customers, per the bank's own announcement, corroborated by FStech, Finextra, Banking Exchange, and Financial IT. Rather than answering questions, the free in-app assistant executes banking commands directly: a business owner can tell it to "put 20% of last month's earnings aside for VAT," and it calculates the figure from account history and transfers the funds into a dedicated savings Space automatically. It also reviews supplier invoices for signs of fraud and advises customers on what to do if it flags one as suspicious. Starling said the assistant will gain a new tool roughly every week for the rest of the year, including deeper integration with its accounting software that will let it draft and send invoices on a business's behalf, and that customers can request specific capabilities for its AI team to build. The launch moves agentic banking assistants from pilot programs into a mainstream product for one of the UK's largest digital-only banks, covering its full small-business customer base rather than a limited test group. *(Sources: Starling Bank, FStech, Finextra, Banking Exchange, Financial IT, August 25)*

### Aviation Records Platform Bluetail Says It Has Digitized More Than 30 Million Aircraft Records With AI

Bluetail, a company that builds AI-powered aircraft maintenance-records software, said it has now digitized more than 30 million aircraft records, which it describes as one of the largest digital datasets of its kind in aviation, per the company's own announcement. The milestone, disclosed on the company's sixth anniversary and alongside its second consecutive appearance on the Inc. 5000 list of fastest-growing private companies, spans records from individual owners of small aircraft like Cessnas up to large commercial fleets operating in the US and internationally. The company has built out a suite of AI products this year — including Ask Bluetail for querying records in natural language, Autonomous Logbooks, a Lifecycle Timeline view, and a Compliance Check tool — aimed at replacing the paper-based and PDF logbooks that have historically made aircraft maintenance history difficult to search or verify during sales, inspections, and regulatory audits. This item is sourced solely from Bluetail's own announcement; no independent aviation trade outlet has yet corroborated the specific 30-million-record figure. *(Sources: Bluetail, August 25)*

## Key Themes

**OpenAI and Anthropic are converging on the same strategic bet: narrow the product surface, deepen the enterprise commitment.** OpenAI's decision to kill Sora and Atlas to free up compute for its next model generation, and Anthropic's push to institutionalize Bain as a Global Premier partner delivering Claude at 19,000-person scale, both point toward frontier labs treating consumer breadth as a distraction from the compute-intensive work of winning enterprise deployment and staying ahead on model capability.

**Trust and safety incidents are shaping how labs talk about their own products, not just how they're covered.** OpenAI's disclosure of the Russian influence campaign — and its choice to detail the operators' VPN workaround and multi-platform reach rather than downplay it — continues a pattern this digest has tracked through August of labs publishing their own security and misuse findings before outside reporting forces the issue.

**Today's real-world implementation stories share a common shape with prior weeks: narrow domain-trained tools embedded directly into existing workflows.** Thomson Reuters trained Thomson specifically on its own legal and tax content rather than relying on a general frontier model, and Starling Assistant only executes pre-defined categories of banking commands — both reflect a preference for scoped, auditable AI deployment over open-ended agents, even as adoption itself (a full business-customer base at Starling, a whole legal-review workflow at Thomson Reuters) becomes more ambitious.
