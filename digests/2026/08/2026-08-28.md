## 1. Frontier Model News

### Nvidia Agrees to Buy Hugging Face for Roughly $12.9 Billion

The acquisition talks this digest flagged on August 25 have turned into an agreement: Nvidia has agreed to buy Hugging Face for about $12.9 billion, per The Information's original reporting, corroborated by CNBC, Fortune, TechCrunch, and Tom's Hardware. The price lands just below the $13 billion-plus figure first reported last week and is nearly triple Hugging Face's 2023 valuation. Neither company has confirmed the deal publicly, and no terms have been disclosed beyond the price; CNBC reports Nvidia and Hugging Face did not respond to requests for comment. The fit tracks Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue's public alignment with Nvidia's open-model strategy this year — he's argued that "open models will be kings" in AI cybersecurity and has used an Nvidia-modified open model to help defend Hugging Face's own infrastructure after a cyberattack. If finalized, the deal would put the most widely used hub for sharing and running open-source AI models under the ownership of the industry's dominant chipmaker, deepening Nvidia's reach from silicon into the software layer developers use to deploy on it. *(Sources: The Information, CNBC, Fortune, TechCrunch, Tom's Hardware, August 27)*

### Federal Judge Rules Pentagon's "Supply Chain Risk" Designation of Anthropic Was Illegal

U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled Thursday night that the Pentagon acted illegally when it designated Anthropic a national-security "supply chain risk" in February, per NPR's original reporting, corroborated by CNN and Forbes. In a 59-page order, Lin — a Biden appointee — wrote that neither the Constitution nor the federal statute the government invoked permits Washington to "impose sweeping penalties based principally on Anthropic's critique of the Administration's views." The dispute began in February when President Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth accused Anthropic of endangering national security after CEO Dario Amodei declined to soften the company's public concerns about AI being used for mass surveillance or autonomous weapons; OpenAI struck its own Pentagon deal within hours of Anthropic being punished. An Anthropic spokesperson welcomed the ruling, saying the company "remain[s] focused on working productively with the government to harness AI for our national security." The government is expected to appeal. *(Sources: NPR, CNN, Forbes, August 27–28)*

### Anthropic Signs $45 Billion Compute Deal With Nscale as IPO Plans Firm Up

Anthropic has committed $45 billion over six years to rent AI computing capacity from British infrastructure firm Nscale's West Virginia data center, per Bloomberg's original reporting, corroborated by CNBC, TechCrunch, and Yahoo Finance. The deal covers roughly 460 megawatts of power — enough for about 345,000 US homes — running on Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chips once they come online next year, and follows Microsoft's earlier exit from a planned Nscale partnership. The commitment lands as Anthropic's IPO preparations advance: the company is reportedly targeting an October public offering at a valuation of $2 trillion or more, which would make it the largest IPO in history, after posting Q2 revenue of $11.5 billion and what Bloomberg described as its first adjusted operating profit. Critics have noted the valuation still implies profits far beyond what Anthropic currently generates, but investors appear to be betting on the growth trajectory rather than current earnings. *(Sources: Bloomberg, CNBC, TechCrunch, Yahoo Finance, August 26)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Anthropic Previews a Standard Letting Claude Operate Lab and Factory Hardware

Anthropic introduced the Model Hardware Standard (MHS), a research-preview protocol that lets Claude interface directly with physical equipment — microscopes, liquid handlers, robotic arms, and manufacturing tools — per Anthropic's own announcement, corroborated by Fortune and TechNext. MHS stores structured information about how a given piece of hardware works, letting an AI agent operate multiple instruments in parallel for tasks like routine drug-discovery experiments or laser calibration on a quantum computer, without relying on paper manuals or knowledge held by a handful of specialists. Early testers include AWS, Danaher, Hugging Face, and Raspberry Pi, and Anthropic says it plans to open-source the standard after the preview period so any hardware maker can build to the spec — a bid to make MHS to physical equipment roughly what Anthropic's Model Context Protocol became for software tools. It extends Claude Science, the AI research workbench Anthropic launched in June, into direct control of physical lab and factory equipment for the first time. *(Sources: Anthropic, Fortune, TechNext, August 27)*

### OpenAI Ships an Admin Plugin That Lets IT Managers Run ChatGPT Work and Codex From One Conversation

OpenAI released an Admin plugin for ChatGPT Work and Codex, per OpenAI's own announcement, corroborated by 9to5Mac and Investing.com. The plugin lets workspace administrators review activity and credit usage, add or remove members, manage groups and access permissions, adjust usage limits, and approve or deny spending requests — all inside a single chat rather than switching between separate admin consoles. It also supports automating recurring checks and high-volume requests without custom engineering, which OpenAI says reduces manual monitoring and lets admins focus on judgment calls rather than routine account maintenance. The plugin is available now from the Plugins directory in ChatGPT Work, and follows a broader pattern this year of AI labs turning their own chat interfaces into control surfaces for enterprise IT rather than building separate dashboards. *(Sources: OpenAI, 9to5Mac, Investing.com, August 25)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Salesforce's Agentforce Crosses $1.5 Billion in Annual Recurring Revenue

Salesforce reported that Agentforce annual recurring revenue exceeded $1.5 billion, up more than 240% year over year, in its fiscal Q2 2027 results, per the company's own earnings release, corroborated by CNBC and Yahoo Finance. Combined with Data 360, Agentforce-related ARR reached nearly $3.9 billion, up over 210% year over year, and customers generated 3.2 billion Agentforce usage units in the quarter, up 97% from the prior quarter. Total revenue came in at $11.35 billion, above the high end of guidance, and Salesforce raised its full-year revenue outlook by $300 million; shares jumped as much as 14% on the results. The numbers offer one of the clearer public data points yet on enterprise agentic-AI adoption converting into recurring software revenue rather than one-off pilots, at a scale large enough to move a major software vendor's overall growth rate. *(Sources: Salesforce, CNBC, Yahoo Finance, August 26)*

### CrowdStrike Posts Its Best Trading Day Ever as AI Agents Become Both a Threat and a Customer

CrowdStrike reported record fiscal Q2 revenue of $1.47 billion, up 26% year over year and above Wall Street's $1.44 billion estimate, and raised its full-year revenue guidance to $5.99–$6.01 billion, per Bloomberg's and CNBC's reporting on the earnings release, corroborated by the Motley Fool and Benzinga. CEO George Kurtz called it the best quarter in company history; shares jumped roughly 20% the next day, their largest one-day gain on record, in what CNBC framed as a broader "AI cyber rally" that also lifted Okta. The growth driver, according to CNBC's reporting, is twofold: enterprises are buying more cybersecurity tooling specifically to defend against AI-driven threats, and autonomous AI agents are themselves increasingly showing up as customers of CrowdStrike's identity and access products — a shift the company frames as turning AI from a security risk into a new demand source rather than purely a threat vector. *(Sources: Bloomberg, CNBC, The Motley Fool, Benzinga, August 26–27)*

## Key Themes

**Anthropic had one of its strongest 48-hour stretches of the year.** A federal court struck down the Pentagon's "supply chain risk" designation as illegal, the company locked in $45 billion of compute capacity ahead of a targeted October IPO at a $2 trillion-plus valuation, and it opened a new physical-AI frontier with the Model Hardware Standard — three storylines that, taken together, show a company simultaneously fighting a political battle, financing a scaling bet, and expanding its product surface.

**AI infrastructure ownership keeps consolidating around a few large players.** Nvidia's agreement to buy Hugging Face — the default distribution point for open-weight models — would put chip supply, cloud partnerships (via deals like Anthropic's with Nscale), and now model distribution under an increasingly small set of interlocking companies, extending the concentration this digest has tracked through Microsoft's, Nvidia's, and Anthropic's compute dealmaking this month.

**Enterprise AI spending is now visible in hard earnings numbers, not just pilot anecdotes.** Salesforce's Agentforce ARR and CrowdStrike's AI-driven security demand both landed in quarterly results with double-digit stock moves attached — a more concrete signal of paying enterprise adoption than the case-study-style rollouts this digest has covered in healthcare and retail in recent weeks.
