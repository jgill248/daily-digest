## 1. Frontier Model News

### Anthropic open-sources FlashProve, a zero-knowledge prover that shows which model wrote a reply at about 110 tokens a second on one H100
Anthropic's Frontier Red Team, with collaborators at EPFL, MIT and StarkWare, has published FlashProve, a research prototype for "provable inference": a provider publishes a short cryptographic commitment to a model's weights, then attaches to each reply a proof that the committed model produced those tokens from that prompt. Anyone holding the commitment, prompt and reply can check it on a CPU in seconds without seeing the weights or running the model. The system is a zkSNARK built from an interactive oracle proof and hash functions, which Anthropic says gives post-quantum security, and its top-level soundness claims are proved in Lean. On Llama 4 Maverick, a 400-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with about 17 billion active parameters, the prover reaches roughly 110 tokens a second on a single H100 with 24 CPU cores, produces proofs of about 19 MB per 1,024-token request, and costs about 72 times the GPU time of vLLM at a 24-request batch, rising to about 94 times at the highest load tested. Prior academic work managed about 0.02 tokens a second on the same model. The code, a C++ verifier, a Lean verifier and the proofs are on GitHub under Apache 2.0, where the repository is marked unmaintained and shows about 670 stars. The limits are stated plainly: proofs cover only greedy decoding of an integer-quantised model, contexts of at most 1,024 tokens, and the architecture is not hidden. The post is dated November 2; trade coverage landed November 4. *(Sources: Anthropic research post, GitHub, Help Net Security, November 4)*

### OpenAI says ChatGPT now handles about one-fifth of Google's search volume, counts 250 million weekly voice users and has passed $1 billion of annualised ad revenue
At an OpenAI-hosted dinner in New York on November 2, reported November 3, ChatGPT consumer product head Adam Fry said the number of prompts that send ChatGPT to the web is "roughly one-fifth" of Google's query volume. Big Technology's Alex Kantrowitz, who moderated, carried the remark first; 9to5Google, Search Engine Journal and others corroborate it. Fry defined a search as any prompt that triggers a web lookup, excluding definitions and simple instructions, and did not say which Google figure, period or regions he was comparing against. Ahrefs estimated the ratio at 12% in February, and no outside data verifies the new number. The other figures from the evening are company-reported: weekly active users rose from 1 billion to 1.2 billion in about 60 days, about 250 million people a week use voice for dictation or conversation, roughly 1.5 billion images are generated weekly, and the ads business passed $1 billion of annualised revenue within 200 days of launch with more than 100,000 advertisers, which Fry said has not dented usage. He also said OpenAI plans to merge the Chat and Work modes and described the Dots assistants as "compute heavy," which Fortune paired with Musk's Grok Bot admission from this digest's previous issue as a sign that personal agents are running into serving costs. *(Sources: Big Technology, 9to5Google, Search Engine Journal, Fortune, November 3)*

### OpenAI and Anthropic ran election "war rooms" through the midterms, and Anthropic says asking who to vote for is 0.01% of Claude conversations
Semafor reported on November 2 that both labs would staff dedicated rooms through Election Day on November 3, a first for each, and The Hill confirmed the details with both companies on the day. OpenAI's Election Operations Center, first described to Politico on October 28, is led by Josh Lawson, head of civic and strategic risk, draws on hundreds of staff across teams, investigates accounts suspected of breaking its rules, routes voting questions to Democracy Works and uses the Associated Press for race calls; NBC News filmed inside it. Anthropic's spokesperson said the company has "a dedicated war room monitoring our platform for misuse and ready to quickly respond," defines misuse as deceiving voters or disrupting elections, such as impersonating election officials, and keeps a TurboVote banner on registration, polling-place and date queries. The company added that requests for voting recommendations are about 0.01% of Claude conversations, a figure it did not source further. Former Meta policy director Katie Harbath, who ran the first such room in 2018, told Semafor they exist for problems nobody can predict. The New York Times reports that Google and Meta expanded their own efforts and that the threat surface is broader than in 2024. Neither lab has yet published what, if anything, the rooms caught. *(Sources: Semafor, The Hill, Politico, NBC News, The New York Times, November 3)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Apple opens Siri AI to every third-party app in the iOS 27.3 developer beta, with "app skills" written as SKILL.md files
Apple released the first iOS 27.3, iPadOS 27.3 and macOS 27.3 developer betas on November 3 alongside a Siri AI Developer Preview that any developer can join. Apps expose content and actions through the App Intents framework, and users no longer need to name the app in a request. The new layer is "app skills": a developer ships a Skills.skillsbundle inside the app target, each skill is a folder holding a SKILL.md file with a name and description in the front matter and a body that tells Siri how to chain the app's intents for a multi-step task. Apple's own example is a ride-hailing app whose Live Activity lets Siri handle "change the pickup address" mid-trip. Xcode gains a Siri Integration Advisor and a beta Siri Evaluations framework, and Apple is extending the same hooks to Calendar, Mail, Maps, Messages, Notes, Photos, Safari and other first-party apps. Testing is confined to development-signed builds and internal TestFlight; App Store submissions open in the spring, and Apple says the APIs can still change. The Verge and 9to5Mac carry Apple's developer post; the final 27.3 release is expected in December, and the Chinese Qwen-backed variant this digest covered on November 2 is on the same beta track. *(Sources: The Verge, 9to5Mac, Apple Developer, November 3)*

### CrowdStrike will pay about $495 million for Fiddler AI and Dreadnode and ships an "AI Kill Switch" for runaway agents
At Fal.Con Europe in Barcelona on November 3, CrowdStrike announced two acquisitions and a new control. Per the Wall Street Journal's original reporting, corroborated by CyberScoop and Seeking Alpha, Fiddler AI costs about $400 million and Dreadnode about $95 million, mostly cash with some vesting stock; CrowdStrike itself disclosed no prices and expects both to close in its fiscal fourth quarter. Fiddler monitors deployed models and agents and will become Falcon Observe, which CEO George Kurtz called "the flight recorder for every agent"; Dreadnode runs automated adversarial testing, attacking an agent with more than 100 strategies before it ships. TechBuzz cites sources splitting the money differently, about $300 million and $200 million, so the per-company figures are unsettled. The Kill Switch, part of Falcon Guardian, lets administrators halt an agent that leaves its assigned behaviour, block it from relaunching and cut its network access. Kurtz said every agent will need one "just as every employee needs an identity," and the chief product officer said it came up in "every single conversation" about Guardian. Kurtz also said enterprise customers asked for CrowdStrike's integration before deploying Anthropic's products. Together with Goldman Sachs pulling coding agents from its front office, reported yesterday, the deal prices agent oversight as a product category. *(Sources: Wall Street Journal, CyberScoop, Seeking Alpha, CNBC, TechBuzz, November 3)*

### Snowflake makes Cortex AI Gateway generally available and adds a Business Ontology so agents share one set of approved definitions
Snowflake used its Expedition 2026 event on November 3 to ship the governance layer it previewed in July. Cortex AI Gateway, now generally available, sits between agents and everything they touch: it authenticates each tool call with the end user's identity, checks it against policy before it runs, keeps an activity record, and attributes spend to teams or agents with near-real-time budget limits. A Tools extension in public preview governs MCP connections, built on the Natoma acquisition, and Cortex Sandboxes, Agent Abuse Detection, Agent Trace Intelligence and Dynamic Model Routing are in private preview. Business Ontology, living in Horizon Catalog, lets a company approve definitions of terms such as "active customer" and binds them to tables and views so Snowflake's CoCo and CoWork agents ground answers in them; the press release says public preview is coming soon, while SiliconANGLE reports it is in private preview today. Glean and Kargo are named gateway users, dentsu and Merkle ontology users, and Shelter Mutual's Vibhor Gupta is quoted on live data flowing into CoWork with eight agents in production. Availability labels differ across the press release and trade coverage. *(Sources: Snowflake press release, SiliconANGLE, Enterprise Times, BigDATAwire, November 3)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Oracle cancels a $7 billion lease of 100,000 AI chips to Tencent after US officials object, the FT reports, as Bloomberg says China's Nvidia orders will be 1% filled
The Financial Times reported on November 4, citing two people familiar, that Oracle has scrapped a five-year deal, worth about $7 billion, under which Tencent would have rented as many as 100,000 advanced AI chips across Oracle data centres in Southeast Asia. US officials raised concerns directly with Oracle; separately, senior officials from Guangdong visited Tencent to push it toward domestic chips. Reuters, Bloomberg and Business Standard carried the FT account and said Oracle, Tencent and the White House did not respond; Reuters could not independently verify it. Overseas leasing of this kind is still legal, and the cancellation comes a day after the FT's report, covered in yesterday's digest, that Nvidia told cloud partners to cap Chinese business at 49% of capacity. Bloomberg added a second data point the same morning: Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and other Chinese firms have ordered about 6 million RTX Pro 5500 workstation cards, worth roughly $70 billion, but expect about 60,000, or 1%, within four months. Reuters had reported on October 20 that the three largest buyers alone ordered 3 million cards at about $11,400 each and that Nvidia warned of 5% to 10% fulfilment; the card is not export-controlled, and Nvidia is reportedly prioritising higher-margin parts for OpenAI and Anthropic. Nvidia has not commented on either report. *(Sources: Financial Times, Reuters, Bloomberg, Business Standard, November 4)*

### AMD posts a record $13.4 billion quarter with data centre revenue up 97% and guides to $17 billion as MI450 and Helios racks ship, while Fal raises $1.3 billion at $16 billion after rate-limiting customers
AMD's third-quarter results on November 3 showed revenue of $13.4 billion, up 45% year on year, a 54% gross margin, $2.4 billion of net income and $1.46 of diluted EPS, which the company called its largest and most profitable quarter. Data centre revenue was a record $8.5 billion, up 97% and about 27% sequentially, on EPYC demand and the first revenue shipments of MI455 GPUs and Helios racks; Lisa Su said Helios "ramped very nicely" and that growth should accelerate as Helios and "Venice" production scale. Fourth-quarter guidance of about $17 billion, plus or minus 3%, beat the $16.3 billion consensus Bloomberg cites, and shares rose about 7% after hours. Client and gaming revenue fell 8% to $3.7 billion, and free cash flow dropped to $554 million from $1.53 billion a year earlier. The same day, inference provider Fal announced a $1.3 billion Series F led by Bessemer with Nvidia, Sound Ventures and T. Rowe Price, at a $16 billion valuation, double April's. CEO Burkay Gur told TechCrunch the company had to rate-limit customers over the summer because it could not serve them all; Bloomberg's sourcing puts annualised revenue past $800 million, which Fal has not published. *(Sources: AMD press release, Bloomberg, TechCrunch, SiliconANGLE, November 3)*

### Netflix begins testing an AI Discover tab on TVs that picks titles by theme rather than viewing history
Netflix announced AI Discover on November 3 as a new tab on supported TV apps, rolling out to a small group of US members who use the service in English and widening over the coming weeks. Rather than a search box, the tab presents themes such as "Something Light," "Scare Me," "Family Movie Night" and "Quick Watches," some introduced with a clip; choosing one returns a slate of films and series, more specific sub-themes narrow it, and a "Something Different" option deals a fresh set. Every title inside the tab carries a "Tune Your Recommendations" button beside Play and My List that lets a member keep or exclude it, though Netflix's help page says the exclusion applies only inside AI Discover. Netflix calls the tab its "test kitchen" for discovery and says member usage and feedback will decide which experiments it keeps. Members cannot opt in or remove the tab, Netflix did not name the model or vendor behind it, and Tom's Guide could not find the tab on two test TVs, so coverage is thin on how the recommendations are generated. It follows Netflix's earlier conversational search test on mobile and is its first generative-AI surface on the television home screen, where most viewing happens. *(Sources: Netflix blog, TechCrunch, Variety, Deadline, Tom's Guide, November 3)*

## Key Themes

**Verification is becoming a product.** FlashProve lets a customer prove which model answered, CrowdStrike is paying $495 million for tools that record and attack agents, Snowflake's gateway checks every tool call against policy, and Apple will only let Siri act inside apps whose developers declared what it may do. The common move is to make an AI system's behaviour auditable from outside rather than trusted on the vendor's word.

**Access to compute is being decided by governments and allocation, not price.** Oracle walked away from $7 billion of Tencent revenue after a call from Washington, Chinese buyers will get 1% of the Nvidia cards they ordered, and AMD's record quarter and Fal's doubled valuation both rest on a supply that is sold out and rationed. The constraint OpenAI's Fry named for its own agents, serving cost, is the same one.

**Distribution claims outrun measurement.** OpenAI's one-fifth-of-Google figure, its $1 billion of ad revenue and Netflix's theme-based tab are all company-reported and none comes with a methodology. The election war rooms are the inverse: real staffing against a threat whose size nobody has published.
