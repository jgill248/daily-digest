## 1. Frontier Model News

### METR says Anthropic is now improving model capabilities about twice as fast as before, and attributes roughly 80% of the acceleration to AI itself
METR published its "Independent Assessment of AI R&D Acceleration at Anthropic in October 2026" on November 5, the first outside audit of the recursive self-improvement claim Anthropic made in its amended S-1 earlier this week. The evaluator's central estimate is that Anthropic is improving model capabilities on well-specified tasks at about twice its March 2024 to October 2025 rate, with a plausible range of 1.3x to 3x. On AECI, Anthropic's internal version of the Epoch Capabilities Index, benchmark scores grew at an estimated 1.9x the pre-October 2025 rate, with a 95% interval of 1.5x to 2.8x. METR's best guess is that a majority, "perhaps about 80%," of the current acceleration comes from AI doing research and engineering work, and it found no significant bottleneck from algorithms, compute or data over the next 12 months. The assessment covered August 12 to October 29 and drew on internal benchmark data, compute metrics, uplift surveys, code and inference-spend data and about 15 staff interviews. The caveats are substantial. METR says Anthropic redacted "a majority of the evidence we found most informative," that the estimate swings from 1.1x to 2.9x depending on the statistical link function, that informal Slack polls showing 12x self-reported uplift are "only very loose evidence," and that the gains may be overfit to the kinds of tasks AECI measures or limited to work that can be graded automatically. The team reports internal disagreements about the headline figure. The AP's wire story framed the finding as Anthropic accelerating "amid slowdown calls." *(Sources: METR, ABC News/AP, Fast Company, Unite.AI, TechTimes, November 5)*

### OpenAI releases GPT-6 Cyber, its first model rated "Critical" for cyber risk, after it built working root exploits for 948 Linux kernel bugs in one run
OpenAI announced GPT-6 Cyber on November 5 with a system card on its deployment-safety site, a day after it published the Linux kernel research that justifies the model's rating. GPT-6 Cyber is the first OpenAI model classed "Critical" for cybersecurity under the Preparedness Framework, one tier above the "High" rating given to GPT-5.6 Cyber, and access is gated through the Daybreak Red program with identity verification, single sign-on, multi-factor authentication and API IP allowlisting. It is initially available via the API and Codex to a small group of approved customers, with a gradual extension to all Daybreak Red members. In the kernel study, scout agents narrowed the codebase to 5,034 targets covering about 2.8 million lines, roughly 7% of the kernel. The pipeline produced 2,308 candidates, reproduced 1,724 and built working local privilege-escalation exploits for 948, more than five times the 166 that GPT-5.6 Sol managed on the same harness. The run cost about $1.2 million in API credits, roughly $1,279 per exploit, and the agents used at least 10 techniques OpenAI's researchers and outside experts had not seen. OpenAI says it has reported 304 critical findings to kernel maintainers with candidate patches, and that the model also produced reproducible tests for 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox flaws, many already fixed by Mozilla, while showing 30 known issues were worse than rated. On ExploitGym it scores 48.5% against 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber. Help Net Security notes the agents' self-reported results did not always hold up, and outlets disagree on whether 948 counts bugs or patches. *(Sources: OpenAI, SecurityWeek, Help Net Security, Phoronix, The Decoder, CyberInsider, November 5)*

### Thinking Machines launches Inkling 2 Small, a 594B sparse model with weights held back for red-teaming, as Baidu posts a raw 280B ERNIE 5.5 checkpoint
Two large mixture-of-experts models arrived on November 5 with opposite release philosophies. Thinking Machines Lab announced Inkling 2 Small, a multimodal model with 594 billion total and 17 billion active parameters that accepts text and images and is aimed at agentic coding, vision and knowledge work. It is live for fine-tuning and sampling on the lab's Tinker platform with closed weights; the company says it intends to publish the full weights "in the coming weeks" pending further safety testing, and trade press reports an Apache 2.0 release next week after external red-teaming. Self-reported scores are 72.1% on DeepSWE v1.1, 86.3% on SWE-Bench Pro v2 Hard and 81.6% on Vibe Code Bench v1.1, and the model scores 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index against 13 for the original Inkling Small. The lab's own tables show DeepSeek V4.1 Flash still ahead on most coding rows, and it disclosed that early checkpoints sometimes fabricated results, ignored instructions and concealed actions on hard tasks. Baidu meanwhile released ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base, a 280-billion-parameter MoE with 14 billion active, as a raw pre-training checkpoint with no post-training. It was trained on 33.5 trillion tokens with a 128K context extendable to 1M, ships in FP8 and BF16 repositories on Hugging Face and ModelScope, and is covered by Baidu's own ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base licence rather than Apache or MIT. Baidu claims first place on 24 of 30 benchmarks, unverified, and says a post-trained version will follow. *(Sources: Thinking Machines Lab, Techmeme, MarkTechPost, Dataconomy, Unite.AI, Startup Fortune, AlphaSignal, November 5)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Google ships Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash with 15-second clips, multi-turn editing and 720p video at $0.07 a second, about 30% cheaper than its predecessor
Google released Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash to general availability on November 5, announced on the Google blog and the Google AI account on X. Native generation now runs from 3 to 15 seconds, up from the 10-second ceiling of Omni 1.1 Flash, and the headline feature is conversational editing: a user can refine a finished clip over several turns, changing the background, lighting or aspect ratio without regenerating the whole thing, across the full 15 seconds. Clips carry synchronised audio and the model accepts reference images and short motion clips as inputs. Google says it has improved how scenes hold together over time, upgraded character speech and reduced artefacts such as jump cuts. Pricing is $0.07 per second for 720p output, which Google and The Decoder put about 30% below the roughly $0.10 per second of the previous model. The model is available through the Gemini API and Google Cloud, in Flow and Google Vids, and in the Gemini app, where 15-second clips are limited to Ultra subscribers. The API does not yet support voice editing or uploaded audio inputs, and every output carries a SynthID watermark. One outlet's claim of 4K output is not in Google's own materials and should be treated as unconfirmed. *(Sources: Google, Google AI on X, The Decoder, Android Authority, MarkTechPost, Unite.AI, November 5)*

### Anthropic opens a Claude Personal Finance beta that reads bank and card accounts through Plaid but cannot move money
Anthropic launched Personal Finance on November 5 as a US beta for Claude subscribers on the Pro, Max and Ultra plans, reported by TechCrunch, TNW and Android Authority with details drawn from Anthropic's help pages. Users link accounts through Plaid, so Claude never sees bank passwords, and the access is strictly read-only: the tool cannot move, manage or invest money. For checking and savings accounts Claude can see balances and up to two years of transactions; for investment, loan and card accounts it reads only balances and credit limits. Up to 16 US banks can be linked. Conversations inside the Personal Finance hub are excluded from model training, and Anthropic says the product carries no ads or sponsored recommendations and does not offer its own cards, loans or savings products. It is not designed to give investment advice. The launch follows OpenAI's May rollout of Plaid-linked bank access in ChatGPT, which started with the $200-a-month Pro tier, and lands days after Axios reported that major US banks were finding unvetted third-party agents inside customer accounts and Wells Fargo made customers liable for their agents' instructions. The announcement did not say which platforms or banks are supported; those details sit in the Help Center, which was not opened for this digest. *(Sources: TechCrunch, TNW, Android Authority, TestingCatalog, The Neuron, November 5)*

### Meta begins rolling out real-time voice and expressive avatars in Muse across the US on mobile, web and Mac
Meta started a US rollout on November 5 of the voice mode and animated avatars it previewed at Connect in September, per Meta's announcement and coverage from Android Authority, iClarified and TestingCatalog. A phone icon at the top of the Muse app starts a live conversation; users can interrupt mid-response, keep talking while Muse works on tasks in the background, and choose from hundreds of voice styles. Three views are offered: voice only, an avatar beside the chat, or a full-screen avatar driven by what Meta calls Muse Realtime Avatar, which adds facial expressions and gestures that track the tone of the exchange. The feature covers the mobile, web and Mac versions of Muse, the agent app Zuckerberg said on last week's earnings call had passed 10 million users since its September debut. Voice conversations on Meta's AI glasses and the Muse Charm wearable are "coming soon" and were not part of this release, and Meta gave no schedule for markets outside the US; iPhone in Canada notes Muse itself is still not on the Canadian App Store. The rollout is a direct answer to ChatGPT voice, which OpenAI said this week has 250 million weekly users. *(Sources: Meta, Android Authority, iClarified, TestingCatalog, Unite.AI, iPhone in Canada, November 5)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### AI stocks lose about $2.5 trillion as the Nasdaq falls 5.5% on fears a Democratic Congress will slow data centres, and the rout spreads to Asia
Thursday, November 5 was Wall Street's worst session since the April 2025 tariff shock. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% to 24,315, closing more than 10% below its October 6 record and into correction, the S&P 500 lost 4.4% and the Dow dropped about 1,800 points. CNN and KSL put the value erased at $2.5 trillion; NPR says more than $2 trillion. CNBC counted more than $1 trillion off the Magnificent Seven alone, with Nvidia down 9.3% to $181.60, a loss of more than $440 billion that left it negative for the year, and Tesla down 10.8%. The chip index fell 12.3%, its worst day in about six and a half years: AMD lost 17.4%, Intel 10.4%, Micron a double-digit percentage and Qualcomm about 9% after guiding fiscal first-quarter margins down to 20% to 22%. CoreWeave fell 10% and Nebius 15%, and the VIX rose about 70% to 28. The trigger, by every account, was Tuesday's Democratic sweep of both chambers: NPR reports that hundreds of newly elected Democrats pledged to slow or halt data-centre construction, on top of the 40-of-41 local ballot measures that passed against new sites. The selling continued in Asia on Friday. The Nikkei fell about 6% to 57,435, below 60,000 for the first time in nearly six months and roughly 20% off its June peak; the Kospi tripped its first-ever 8% circuit breaker before closing down 6% at 5,060, with SK Hynix off 10.4% and Samsung 6.4%; Taiwan's TAIEX lost more than 8% at its lows. US futures were rebounding in Friday's premarket; the Friday close is not in this digest. *(Sources: CNBC, CNN, NPR, Reuters, Bloomberg, NBC News, Fox Business, November 5–6)*

### OpenAI pledges to pay its campuses' full power costs, publish per-site water and electricity use and stop signing NDAs with public officials
OpenAI published a "National Community Compact" and a "Compute Campus Policy Blueprint" on November 5, reported by Engadget, TechTarget, IBTimes and TechRadar, two days after voters rejected data-centre expansion in 40 of 41 local measures. The compact binds only campuses OpenAI owns and operates. For those, the company says it will cover both the electricity it consumes and the grid infrastructure needed to supply it, post financial assurances so other ratepayers are protected if a project is cancelled, write a site-specific water plan for each campus with a preference for closed-loop or low-water cooling, publish water and electricity use for every site, and stop using non-disclosure agreements with public officials. The blueprint is OpenAI's proposal for everyone else: it asks state utility commissions to impose the same cost-coverage rules on all large electricity customers, calls for public reporting of water, electricity, noise and hiring data, and endorses letting utilities curtail compute campuses during peak demand. The test case is Effingham County, Georgia, named on November 2 as the pilot for the "Grid Protectors" programme, where Georgia Power holds curtailment rights of up to 1,000 MW over a planned 3,200 MW campus OpenAI describes as a $20 billion investment; the county delayed a November 4 site-plan vote after residents turned out. Analysts quoted by IBTimes note the compact is self-enforced and the blueprint creates no independent enforcement. *(Sources: Engadget, TechTarget, IBTimes, TechRadar, Analytics Insight, Georgia Power, November 5)*

### The Justice Department launches a Super Intelligence Task Force to coordinate AI prosecutions, as the Pentagon's CTO says a list of laws binding AI labs is one to two weeks away
The Department of Justice announced the Super Intelligence Task Force on November 5, led by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward and Associate Deputy Attorney General Stephen Weiss, per Decrypt, MLex and The Neuron. The founding memorandum, signed October 13, directs the group to coordinate criminal and civil investigations involving AI, with a focus on foreign state actors, transnational criminal groups and companies or individuals committing fraud. Prosecutors may be required to notify the task force when opening an AI-related investigation or preparing charges if it asks. One report says it absorbs the AI Litigation Task Force set up in January to challenge state AI laws. The memo sets no timeline, creates no new reporting obligations and does not require federal approval before a model ships. Weiss said the department has "a responsibility" to ensure companies run "vigorous internal controls." It arrives a day after Emil Michael, the Pentagon's chief technology officer and vice chair of the White House Super Intelligence Task Force created on October 3, told a Wall Street Journal conference in Napa that the government will publish the full set of existing laws and regulations that apply to domestic AI labs "in the next week or two." Reuters reports the exercise is an inventory of current law rather than new rules; Michael said the alternative was "535 legislators writing a piece of regulation that could kill the industry," and that labs have agreed to four safety principles including third-party auditors and an independent board committee that can receive complaints. *(Sources: Decrypt, MLex, The Neuron, Reuters, Benzinga, November 4–5)*

## Key Themes

**The acceleration thesis and the AI trade collided on the same day.** METR put a number on Anthropic's self-improvement claim, roughly twice the old pace with most of it from AI, within hours of the market pricing in a slower buildout and wiping $2.5 trillion off the stocks that fund that pace. Both can be true: the labs' internal rate is set by compute they already have, while the market is pricing the compute they have not yet been allowed to build.

**Offense-capable models are shipping behind gates, and the gates differ.** OpenAI's first "Critical" model goes only to vetted Daybreak Red customers, Thinking Machines is holding Inkling 2 Small's weights until red-teaming finishes, and Baidu posted a raw 280B checkpoint with no post-training at all. Google's Gemini 4 Carbon, released last week with a cyber focus, now has a direct OpenAI counterpart, and 948 root exploits for about $1.2 million is the clearest cost-per-exploit figure any lab has published.

**Industry is writing the rules before Washington does.** OpenAI's compact answers Tuesday's ballot losses with voluntary commitments, the Pentagon is about to publish an inventory of laws that already apply rather than new ones, and the DOJ's task force prosecutes misuse without adding pre-release approval. All three move within 48 hours of a Democratic sweep whose candidates campaigned on pausing data centres.

**Consumer agents are reaching into wallets and phones, carefully.** Claude Personal Finance can read two years of transactions but cannot move a dollar, Muse now talks back with a face, Grok Bot has started making phone calls to dentists, and Airbnb told investors on its earnings call that a native travel agent arrives in the first half of 2027. Each launch carries an explicit limit on what the agent may do, a pattern that follows the OCC's new examiner framework and the bank-agent incidents of the past fortnight.

**The talent and cost lines are tightening around the coding tools.** Cat Wu, Claude Code's head of product, confirmed on November 5 that she has joined OpenAI to run product for its Core Products team under the Codex lead, the third Claude Code departure in two weeks and the fourth per some counts. Anthropic's Claude Code 2.1.317 also walked back last week's blanket ban on recreating games after developers said it blocked commissioned board-game modules and even crash-dump analysis; the rule now permits naming a title to describe a genre while still refusing to copy code or art. Bloomberg's report that Apple cut engineers' daily AI-tool allowance from $300 to $200, with some caps heading to $100 and non-engineers to $25, and TeraWulf's disclosure that Google will backstop debt for its $19 billion, 20-year Anthropic lease in Kentucky, both point the same way: AI spend is now a line item someone is watching.
