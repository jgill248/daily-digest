## 1. Frontier Model News

### Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 and raised a record $81.3 billion, with ANT due to start trading on Nasdaq today
Anthropic's newsroom confirmed the pricing late Wednesday: 980,000,000 Class A shares at $83.00, above the $70 to $78 range it marketed and up from the 825 million shares first offered. The company sells 956,351,354 of those shares and selling stockholders 23,648,646, with a 30-day option for underwriters to take up to 147,000,000 more; Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead the book and the deal closes November 20. The roughly $81.3 billion raised tops SpaceX's $75 billion in June as the largest IPO on record, and CNBC says the $26 billion placement of non-voting Class N stock that seven companies agreed to buy at the IPO price takes the total to about $107 billion. Valuation figures diverge: most outlets put it near $1.5 trillion, while Renaissance Capital calculates $1.38 trillion fully diluted. Bloomberg's read of grey-market contracts, relayed by Forbes, pointed to an opening 20% to 26% above $83, Nasdaq 100 futures rose more than 2% premarket, and Vanda Research estimates about $250 million of net retail buying on day one. The first print depends on an opening auction that CNBC warns can take well past 9:30 a.m. ET; one analyst note flags that up to 316 million shares can be sold by insiders from the first day. *(Sources: Anthropic newsroom, CNBC, CNN, CBS News, Bloomberg via Forbes, Renaissance Capital, Quartz, November 18–19)*

### Google launched Gemini 4 Neon at $3/$15 per million tokens, and Artificial Analysis scores it 61, third behind Fable 5.2 and GPT-6.2 Astra
Google's fourth Gemini 4 model in about seven weeks slots between Hydrogen and Carbon and, per 9to5Google, replaces Argon in the lineup while Gemini 3.1 Pro is retired from the Gemini app. Pricing is $3 per million input tokens and $15 output, with thinking billed as output, cached input at $0.15, and batch and Flex at half price; the context window is 1,048,576 tokens shared across input, thinking and output, or 512K inside Antigravity. Google's own table claims best-in-class results on Terminal-Bench Science at 70.5%, RiemannBench at 84.0% and HLE-Diamond at 71.3%, and says Neon matched Carbon on Terminal-Bench 4.0 with no detected cheating; on Terminal-Bench 4.1 it posts 65.2% against 65.8% for Claude Opus 5.5. Artificial Analysis puts Neon at 61 on its Intelligence Index at the xhigh thinking level, behind Fable 5.2 at 65 and GPT-6.2 Astra at 62 and a point ahead of Carbon at high, but rates it "notably slow" and "very verbose" at $6.44 per index task; at the default setting one tracker has it at 59.6, below Carbon. It is live in the Gemini API, AI Studio, Gemini for business, Antigravity for paid subscribers and the Gemini app for Pro and Ultra. *(Sources: Google blog via 9to5Google and Analytics Vidhya, Artificial Analysis, OrcaRouter, MindStudio, November 18)*

### Nvidia moved staff pay reviews to December and halted Hopper sales to China, as the WSJ says the $500 billion lending pledge has funded nothing
Three threads on Nvidia's first days without Huang. At the first all-hands since his exit, interim CEO Colette Kress told employees "let's move forward" and pulled the annual review, which normally delivers raises and stock grants in March, forward to December, per CNBC; Wired reports compensation was the top theme among roughly 700 submitted questions and that chair Harvey Jones said the next CEO "won't be a Jensen clone" and the board won't "compromise our requirements for the sake of expediency." On Tuesday's call Kress said "we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China," a line that brought in $2.4 billion in the quarter to October 25, with no reason given; Reuters reporting relayed by several outlets puts about 600,000 H200s sitting in Hong Kong awaiting Beijing's import approval. And The Wall Street Journal reports that the $500 billion of chip financing Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR pledged in August was a nonbinding memorandum that has deployed no capital, with Nvidia offering to cover only up to 25% of project costs against Broadcom's roughly 85% residual-value guarantee on Anthropic's $35 billion Project Big Sky and just under 75% on the $63 billion North Star; no deal is expected to close before 2027. *(Sources: CNBC, Wired via Tom's Hardware and Gizmodo, Bloomberg via Gizmodo and Silicon UK, The Wall Street Journal via Investing.com and FourWeekMBA, November 18–19)*

### The Trump administration is weighing a "name and shame" rule forcing US firms to disclose Chinese AI model use, Reuters reports, as India's Sarvam resells DeepSeek
Reuters, citing three people, says officials at Treasury and the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy have discussed requiring companies to post on their own websites which Chinese models they rely on, naming Alibaba's Qwen, Moonshot's Kimi and DeepSeek. The idea is to discourage dependence without a ban that could provoke industry or Beijing; there is no timeline and the report says it may never become a rule, though Treasury Secretary Bessent said in July that sanctions were possible if distillation theft were proven. Reuters notes Airbnb's customer-service agent runs on Qwen, and Airbnb said it uses "only a limited number of China-origin models, all of which are open-source," while a DoorDash co-founder has said an internal system routes some tasks to Kimi, which drew a congressional letter in July. The mirror-image row is playing out in India: Bloomberg reports that Sarvam, picked by the government in 2025 to build a sovereign model, launched a Token Factory inference platform on November 16 whose eight models include DeepSeek, Kimi and Z.ai's GLM alongside its own 105B model. Co-founder Pratyush Kumar called it a commercial decision to fund Sarvam's own research, and co-founder Vivek Raghavan said the infrastructure is privately funded. *(Sources: Reuters via Yahoo Finance, Stocktwits and MLQ, Bloomberg, Analytics India Magazine, BW Businessworld, November 16–19)*

### A Muslim author sued xAI in federal court, alleging Grok was used to strip her hijab from a selfie and generate sexualised images
Amani Al-Khatahtbeh, founder of the media company Muslim Girl, filed in the Northern District of California on November 18, according to WIRED's reporting as relayed by several outlets. The complaint says users prompted Grok to remove her hijab and put her in revealing clothing in images derived from a selfie she posted at Zohran Mamdani's inauguration as New York City mayor on January 1, and it pleads intentional distribution of sexual images without consent, design defect, negligence and infliction of emotional distress. It argues xAI was on notice and should have built safeguards against removing religious clothing from real people's photos, and seeks up to $250,000 per violation under the California Civil Code. xAI did not respond to requests for comment. The suit lands in a week when a security firm's report of Grok Bot probing corporate accounts prompted xAI to add an admin toggle, and a Center for Countering Digital Hate estimate of about 3 million sexualised images produced on X in 11 days is being cited alongside the case, though that figure is separate from her claims. *(Sources: WIRED via ThePrint, Neoteo and AI Weekly, Cybernews, November 18–19)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Palo Alto Networks is buying Applied Compute for $4.5 billion in stock, two weeks after the startup raised at $3.25 billion
The all-stock deal, announced alongside fiscal first-quarter results, values the San Francisco company about 38% above the $350 million round led by Elad Gil with Nvidia, Shopify Ventures and HubSpot Ventures that closed in early November. Applied Compute was founded by three former OpenAI researchers: CEO Yash Patil, a core member of the Codex effort, CTO Rhythm Garg, a contributor to the o1 reasoning model, and Linden Li on RL training infrastructure. Its platform lets enterprises adapt open-weight models to their own data and run them in production; Palo Alto says it wants to secure AI adoption whether customers use open or closed frontier models, and Patil becomes Co-Chief AI Officer at close, expected in the fiscal third quarter subject to regulatory clearance. Management targets roughly $1 billion of incremental next-generation security ARR from the deal by fiscal 2029, a forward-looking goal analysts note comes with stock dilution. The quarter itself beat: revenue of $3.44 billion rose 39%, and full-year guidance moved to $14.52 billion to $14.57 billion. *(Sources: Bloomberg, SecurityWeek, CSO Online, SiliconANGLE, Dealroom, Help Net Security, November 18–19)*

### Cloudflare open-weighted its Clairic extraction models, a 27B update and a new 9B Clairic-flash, under Apache 2.0
Cloudflare's post, credited to a new Workers AI Superintelligence team, puts both models on Hugging Face and on Workers AI endpoints for pulling structured values out of text, images and PDFs such as invoices and receipts. The model cards list Qwen3.8-27B as the base for Clairic and Qwen3.5-9B for Clairic-flash; the 9B model takes the new clairic-flash endpoint and the 27B replaces the original clairic endpoint. Workers AI bills $0.24 per million input tokens for Clairic and $0.10 for Clairic-flash with no separate output charge, though some coverage says the 27B rate stays at $0.10 until December 1. The weights run locally with PyTorch and Transformers; a community MLX port puts them at roughly 20 GB and 56 GB and notes it downloads from Cloudflare at runtime rather than re-hosting. Training data is not released. The move puts a CDN company alongside the week's other open-weight drops, and makes Alibaba's Qwen the base of yet another US-vendor model on the day Reuters reported Washington wants such dependencies disclosed. *(Sources: Cloudflare blog via Runtime Wire, Unite.AI and DEV Community, Hugging Face, tbreak, November 18–19)*

### Agent-skills tooling leads GitHub trending, as NVIDIA's SkillEvaluator passes 4,000 stars and LangSmith ties coding-agent spend to pull requests
Four of the top five trending AI repositories on Thursday's ai-radar snapshot are skills frameworks or harness optimisers. NVIDIA's SkillEvaluator, the open-source implementation of the live-evaluation tier in its August ACES paper, runs three tiers: static quality and security checks with Gitleaks, Bandit and optional Semgrep plus a harness-load test, embedding-based deduplication across a skill catalogue, and a Docker-sandboxed paired trial that reports "Skill Lift" with and without the skill; it is Apache 2.0, labelled experimental, shipped 0.9.0 on November 13 and has gone from 2,453 stars on November 16 to about 4,000. Google Research's PaperOrchestra, a multi-agent pipeline that turns ideas and experiment logs into LaTeX manuscripts with outline, literature review, writing, refinement and plotting agents, added 1,524 stars in a day to reach 4.8k; its README reports no evaluation figures and says the dataset comes later. LangChain, meanwhile, put LangSmith for Coding Agents into public beta: an "outcomes" concept links GitHub pull requests or GitLab merge requests to traced threads from Claude Code and Codex, so teams can see what a merged PR cost and how much spend never produced one, a day before its November 20 release event. *(Sources: GitHub READMEs, ai-radar, Tokenpost, AlphaSignal, LangChain blog via search excerpts, November 13–19)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Citi, PNC and Citizens made customers liable for anything a third-party AI agent does on their accounts, including after a hack
TechCrunch reports Citi added a new section to its Online User Agreement this week stating that customers who give outside AI agents access are responsible for the agents' actions, and that the liability extends to breaches: if a tool leaks credentials or is compromised, the customer bears whatever follows. Citi's own first-party assistants are carved out. PNC revised its terms on November 18, Citizens sent customers a notice this week, and JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America adopted similar language last month, which one tally makes Citi the fifth major US bank to shift the risk. The timing follows OpenAI's ChatGPT Wallet launch for Pro subscribers and United Airlines' agent block, and sits oddly with Regulation E's consumer protections for unauthorised transfers, a tension the coverage raises without any bank addressing it. One report contrasts the banks' stance with Anthropic's personal-finance tool, launched earlier this month, which is read-only and cannot move or invest money. *(Sources: TechCrunch, Crypto Briefing, Tokenpost, Coinotag, KuCoin News, November 18–19)*

### KPMG ran more than 100 AI agents across a full FTSE 250 audit in parallel with its human team, and says the bots sometimes asked better questions
The Financial Times reports that "Project Exponential" applied agents to every phase of the audit of an unnamed UK-listed company: collecting information, analysing and documenting data, drafting conclusions and observations, and suggesting next steps. UK head of audit Cath Burnet said the agents went deeper on several individual questions and in some cases asked better ones than KPMG's own auditors. Humans set the agents' goals, supplied context, reviewed the output and challenged conclusions, and the firm is explicit that the exercise ran alongside, not instead of, the statutory audit it signed. KPMG plans to extend the approach to as many as 20 larger audits and eventually to sell AI-based audits to clients. The FT pairs the pilot with a Centre for Public Interest Audit study in which 23% of audit committee chairs reported "limited" confidence in AI; a single secondary summary adds that the Financial Reporting Council has warned AI-derived insights can threaten auditor independence, which this digest could not confirm. *(Sources: Financial Times via The Decoder, European Business Magazine and Daily Pioneer, November 18)*

### AWS made four patient-facing agents generally available in Amazon Connect Health, as OpenAI and Scale push agents into hospital workflows
At HLTH in Las Vegas, AWS healthcare AI director Naji Shafi said appointment management has left preview and prescription management, patient-portal password resets and information assistance are now generally available, with agents verifying patients against EHR data, finishing routine tasks in one conversation and handing context to staff when a human is needed; a Redox integration is meant to reach multiple EHRs without a connection per system. Temple Health was the design partner after going live with patient verification in June, and AWS says the agents give no clinical advice. Two days earlier OpenAI added computer-use agents and more than 20 plugins to ChatGPT for Healthcare: agents that read screens, click and move data across EHRs and payer portals, with CVS Health using them for referrals and document sending and open-sourcing its Agent Kit, Cigna pulling benefits and provider data from more than 10 systems, and Abridge and Ambience reported as early builders. OpenAI named no models, error rates or prices. Scale AI launched five hospital-operations applications the same day, covering scheduling, order guidance, record review, safety investigations and documentation, with no outcome data. *(Sources: HIT Consultant, Newsweek, CX Today, OpenAI via Healthcare Innovation and Digital Health News, Unite.AI, November 16–18)*

## Key Themes

**The biggest IPO ever, priced before anyone knows how it trades.** Anthropic raised more than SpaceX did in June and more than the next several US tech listings combined, with a $26 billion strategic placement on top. The number investors will care about by tonight is the first print against $83, and the $1.38 trillion versus $1.5 trillion gap shows valuation already depends on who is counting the Class N shares.

**Nvidia's post-Huang discipline is showing up as retreat.** Pay reviews moved earlier to keep staff, Hopper sales to China stopped without explanation, and the Journal's reporting that the $500 billion financing pledge was a nonbinding memo with no money moved reframes an August headline as a negotiation Nvidia was losing on guarantee size. Broadcom's 85% cover on Anthropic's Big Sky is now the benchmark Nvidia's customers will cite.

**Qwen is everywhere, which is exactly Washington's problem.** Cloudflare's new open-weight extractors are Qwen-based, Airbnb's support agent runs on Qwen, Sarvam is reselling DeepSeek and Kimi under a sovereign-AI banner, and Reuters says the administration's answer is disclosure rather than a ban. A name-and-shame rule would land on US vendors as much as on their customers.

**Agents are getting identities, liabilities and auditors.** Banks moved the risk of agent misuse onto customers in writing, KPMG let agents work a statutory audit under human sign-off, hospitals are getting agents that log into EHRs, and the trending GitHub repos are all about measuring whether a skill actually helps. Microsoft's day-two Ignite items fit the same pattern: Defender now inspects Claude Code and Copilot CLI at runtime on Windows and assigns discovered local agents an Entra Agent ID. The tooling for accountability is arriving a step behind the deployments.
