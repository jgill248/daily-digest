## 1. Frontier Model News

### Nvidia's board met late into Wednesday night on whether to remove Jensen Huang after a reported second affair, with no decision announced by Thursday morning and shares down about 7% premarket
Four days after the board said it had confidence in Huang, it convened on Wednesday evening to weigh his removal, according to Reuters and CNBC, after The New York Times reported on November 11 that Huang had a relationship with an Nvidia executive who reports to him; SiliconANGLE identifies her as healthcare vice president Kimberly Powell, who did not respond to requests for comment, and Nvidia declined to comment. The Wall Street Journal reported the board was preparing to oust him, Bloomberg reported it has been working with an outside firm on possible successors and is weighing an interim chief, and Seeking Alpha and others name finance chief Colette Kress as the likely interim pick. The meeting stretched into the night, and the Washington Post said no announcement had been made as of Thursday morning. On Tuesday the board retained Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to lead the investigation into whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. Nvidia's bylaws let a majority of the 11 directors, who include Huang and have no chair, remove the chief executive with or without cause, though that would not take him off the board. Shares fell about 7% after hours on Wednesday and were down between 5.5% and 6.9% premarket on Thursday, depending on the time stamp; Bernstein cut its target to $200 from $400 and its rating to market-perform, and Morgan Stanley dropped the stock from its top-picks list. Kalshi traders put the odds of Huang being out by December 1 at 80% late Wednesday, while Fortune cited Polymarket at 16% for year-end, and Fortune's own read is that the board may rein him in rather than fire him. Earnings are due November 17. *(Sources: Reuters via CNBC, The New York Times via SiliconANGLE and Decrypt, Wall Street Journal via Decrypt, Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance, Washington Post, Fortune, Seeking Alpha, Investing.com, CNN, November 11–12)*

### Anthropic's seven founders will each sell 3,378,378 shares, about $250 million apiece, as the Australian retail offer opens and Goldman's analysts model $685 billion of revenue in 2030
The amended S-1 filed November 10 lists Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah as the only selling stockholders, each offering 3,378,378 Class A shares for 23,648,646 in total; at the $74 midpoint that is about $250 million each and roughly $1.75 billion combined, a range of $236 million to $264 million per founder before any above-range pricing, and about 1.5% of each founder's stake per Bloomberg. The company receives none of it, and the founders keep a majority of the vote, reported as 50.1% or 51.1% depending on the outlet; one secondary write-up lists only five sellers and omits both Amodeis, which conflicts with the filing-based accounts. A separate Australian prospectus dated and lodged with ASIC on November 10 opened a retail tranche of newly issued Class A shares on Wednesday that closes November 17 per broker Stake, with CommSec as lead retail broker alongside Macquarie, Morgans, Bell Potter, LGT Wealth, Morgan Stanley Wealth Management and Sharesies; there will be no ASX listing, the price is set after applications rather than before, and the document warns that enforcing civil liabilities against Anthropic and its officers is "restricted and difficult for Australian investors." Pricing is expected November 18 US time with trading November 19, which Australian coverage renders as November 20 Sydney time. Bloomberg reported on November 10, citing a person familiar, that Goldman Sachs, the lead underwriter, is modeling about $685 billion of revenue in 2030, roughly $586 billion of it from enterprise, from about $52 billion in 2026, with no positive free cash flow before 2030; one outlet's $520 billion and $6 trillion figures appear to be transcription errors. *(Sources: TechCrunch, Bloomberg via Seeking Alpha and Techstrong.ai, CNBC, Australian Prospectus via SEC Form FWP, ACS Information Age, The Nightly, FourWeekMBA, Entrepreneur, KuCoin News, November 10–12)*

### DeepMind releases AlphaGenome 2, a DNA model conditioned on cell type that it says finds nearly twice as many expression-altering variants, with API access for non-commercial research
Announced November 11 alongside Gemini Robotics 2.5, AlphaGenome 2 extends last year's Nature-published AlphaGenome from fixed bulk-tissue outputs to predictions conditioned on a chosen tissue, cell type or cell state, including states supplied from a researcher's own single-cell experiment, so it can score variants in cell types it was never trained on without retraining. DeepMind says it was trained on more than 100 million single-cell profiles across 68 tissues, combining bulk and single-cell data from human and mouse, and that it identifies nearly twice as many variants that alter gene expression in specific cells as previous state-of-the-art models; on the TenK10K immune-cell dataset the company reports it pinpointed the causal variant 30% of the time against 16% for the prior approach. One summary says it adds DNA methylation to the readouts, which no other source repeats. Access is through the existing AlphaGenome API, free for non-commercial research with outputs barred from training other models, with commercial research in an invitation-only private preview and AlphaGenome 2 Skills for Google Antigravity promised "soon." DeepMind stresses the model is for research only and is not validated for clinical or diagnostic use. All performance figures are DeepMind's own, no third-party replication has appeared, and the DeepMind blog post itself could not be retrieved. *(Sources: SiliconANGLE, Unite.AI, OODAloop, KuCoin News, Cellcog, DigitalToday, DeepMind AlphaGenome API terms, November 11)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Google launches the Gemini Decisions API, a model that writes no text and scores up to 1,000 closed-set questions per request at $0.10 per million input tokens with no output charge
Sundar Pichai announced the Decisions API on November 11, powered by a new narrow model, Gemini Decision 1, available in Google AI Studio and the Gemini Developer API's v1beta surface. Instead of generating tokens it answers yes/no, multiple-choice with 2 to 255 options, and rubric-scoring questions against a shared input of text, images, audio, video or PDFs, returning a probability and calibrated confidence for each; Google quotes 150 to 250 milliseconds end to end, with model compute at a median 50 to 100 milliseconds and latency roughly flat from one to 100 questions. Pricing is $0.10 per million input tokens and zero for output, with a free tier for prototyping, but caching, the Batch API and Flex or Priority tiers do not apply, so repeated inputs are billed in full each call. On its internal suite Google says the model is about five times cheaper than Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite through the Interactions API, $0.88 against $4.14, and reports a top-1 expected calibration error of 0.0250 versus 0.2104 for Flash-Lite, 0.0494 for TypeSafe's Jev 1.14 and 0.0721 for GPT-6 Luna; MarkTechPost notes Jev comes in at $0.68 on the same suite, so Google is not the cheapest option in its own comparison. There are no open weights, no function calling or text generation, and no independent benchmark yet. *(Sources: Google and Sundar Pichai on X, MarkTechPost, Techzine, Interesting Engineering, IT Daily, BigGo Finance, eesel, November 11)*

### xAI takes Grok Bot out of beta with 1.0, adding shared Team Bots in Slack and Microsoft Teams, bots that join Meet, Zoom and Teams calls, and proactive work pickup, with no price in the post
Three months after the August 11 beta, xAI's November 11 post describes three changes: multiplayer teammates, proactive teammates and more capable bots. Team Bots can be published and shared across Slack and Microsoft Teams, restricted to Team and Enterprise plans, and bots can join Google Meet, Zoom or Teams calls from a link, answer questions in the meeting chat and send a summary afterwards, which requires a Team, Enterprise or Ultra plan and an admin switch. Proactive bots spot unassigned work and offer to take it, follow up on unfinished tasks and pull a human in when a decision needs judgement; xAI says they respect each user's permissions, ask approval for sensitive actions and check whether information belongs in a public channel. The beta's habit of silently saving what a Team Bot learned for everyone has been replaced by "reviewed learnings," where the bot proposes the change and an owner or manager approves it, and admins can limit who may share bots. Connectors for Gmail, Calendar and GitHub are listed; how connectors are shared across a team is not described. The post gives no price, quota or performance figure, and third-party pages quote $30, $100 and $200 per seat with Enterprise on request, which could not be verified. The launch follows Elon Musk's November 1 admission that serving Grok Bot cost "unsustainably" more than it earned and his November 10 note that a Grok 4.8 model in reinforcement learning would get a soft rollout to Grok Bot users; one outlet dates the launch to November 2, but the changelog lists 1.0.0 on November 11. *(Sources: xAI, Flavio Copes, AlphaSignal, WebProNews, Crypto Briefing, FourWeekMBA, Cellcog, Techspot, November 11)*

### Google's Gemini app lands on Apple CarPlay worldwide, and OpenAI adds in-country API inference in India for GPT-6 Luna
Google said on November 11 that the Gemini app is rolling out to Apple CarPlay for all Gemini users on iOS, in every language Gemini Live supports; the icon sits on the CarPlay screen or side rail, drivers hold back-and-forth voice conversations, and with Gmail and Calendar linked Gemini can summarise unread mail and long threads or check when an appointment starts, with in-car chats syncing to the phone app afterwards. Setup needs the latest Gemini app, microphone permission and at least one prior Gemini Live session, and 9to5Mac noted the update had not yet reached its devices at publication. It is Google's second route onto CarPlay after Gemini inside Google Maps began reaching US drivers on October 21, and follows Apple quietly dropping its Siri-only restriction earlier this year; Apple's own rebuilt Siri runs on Gemini under the January partnership. Separately, Indian outlets reported on November 12 that OpenAI has launched API inference residency in India, so eligible customers can run GPT-6 Luna requests through the Responses and Chat Completions APIs on an India regional endpoint with both processing and storage of supported content kept in the country; it covers direct API use only, follows similar residency for Australia and Japan, and is aimed at regulated enterprises with localisation requirements. OpenAI's own announcement and the eligibility criteria could not be retrieved. *(Sources: Google, 9to5Mac, AppleInsider, Engadget, Quartz, Analytics India Magazine, The Mobile Indian, FoneArena, November 11–12)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### CoreWeave's revenue rose 168% to $3.66 billion on a $132.5 billion backlog, and it is lining up a $4 billion investment-grade loan as debt reached $46.9 billion
Reporting after the close on November 10, CoreWeave posted third-quarter revenue of $3.66 billion against a $3.55 billion consensus, with backlog of $132.5 billion at September 30, up 138% from $55.6 billion a year earlier. Adjusted operating income was $379 million, a 10.4% margin that management said reached double digits a quarter early, while the GAAP net loss widened to $709 million from $110 million on $846 million of net interest expense. Capital spending was $14.5 billion in the quarter, though the cash-flow statement shows $9.44 billion of equipment purchases, and debt rose to $46.9 billion from $35.1 billion three months earlier. Full-year revenue guidance went to $13 billion to $13.3 billion from a $12.9 billion consensus, capex guidance to $37 billion to $39 billion with the floor lifted from $35 billion, and management said 2027 capex would stay in line with 2026 if it built only for committed backlog; it cited about $6.5 billion of cash and short-term assets, nearly $5 billion of undrawn delayed-draw loans and about $9 billion of customer prepayments and deposits expected over the next three months. Bloomberg reported on November 11 that the company plans to raise at least $4 billion through an investment-grade loan led by Morgan Stanley and MUFG, syndicated in phases from as soon as next week, with price talk of 7% to 7.5% against yields above 14% on its existing high-yield bonds, mirroring an $8.5 billion deal in March; terms are not final. Shares rose 5.7% after hours and one snapshot shows a gain near 25% on Wednesday, which could not be confirmed as a close, while several analysts cut targets and the stock remains 57% below its 52-week high. Sources disagree on adjusted EPS, giving both a loss of $0.95 and $1.27. *(Sources: CNBC, Investing.com, Benzinga, Bloomberg, Motley Fool transcript, TradingKey, Tech Times, Seoul Economic Daily, November 10–12)*

### Mercor, Fractile and Agile Robots raise $1.97 billion in three days at valuations of $20 billion, $7 billion and $8.5 billion
Mercor said on November 10 it raised $750 million in a Series D at a $20 billion valuation, double the $10 billion Series C that Felicis led in October 2025; the round has no named lead, includes an employee tender whose share of the $750 million the company has not disclosed and which its own release does not mention, and brings in Nvidia, Salesforce and Zoom alongside Benchmark, Felicis, General Catalyst, Apollo-affiliated funds, Sapphire and StepStone. Chief executive Brendan Foody said in July that annualised revenue passed $2 billion in June, the company claims more than 5 million domain experts, and the money is meant to take its agent-evaluation business from frontier labs into enterprises. Fractile, the London and Bristol inference-chip designer, announced a $720 million Series C on November 11 led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed and Redpoint with the UK's Sovereign AI Fund and British Business Bank participating; the UK government puts the valuation above £5 billion, about $6.6 billion, while the Financial Times and Reuters report about $7 billion, up from roughly $1 billion at the $220 million Series B in May. Chief executive Walter Goodwin said the funding lets it "ship first chips and systems next year"; Companies House filings already showed £577 million of Series C shares issued between September 9 and October 23, and a Bloomberg-reported $250 million supply agreement with Anthropic remains unconfirmed by either side. On November 12 Reuters reported that SoftBank led a $500 million round in Munich's Agile Robots at an $8.5 billion valuation with Nvidia and Hitachi participating, smaller than the roughly $800 million earlier discussed, for US expansion and more production capacity in Germany; Agile discloses no revenue. *(Sources: SiliconANGLE, Unite.AI, Tech Funding News, MLQ, TechCrunch, Reuters via Yahoo News and Global Banking & Finance, Silicon UK, Dealroom, The Next Web, November 10–12)*

### Google Cloud puts Gemini for Healthcare in preview with HCA, Northwell, Optum and Stanford as launch customers, as Oura pays $625 million for AI primary-care startup Counsel Health
Google Cloud's November 10 release describes Gemini for Healthcare as an agentic layer on its general Gemini agent for health systems, medical groups and health plans: it connects to electronic health records, claims and billing workflows, clinical guidelines and CMS data to surface patient records, prepare paperwork and check coverage, and reads from Epic and Oracle Health without writing back, so a clinician still makes any change. Launch customers are Blue Cross Blue Shield of Massachusetts, Hartford HealthCare, HCA Healthcare, Highmark Health, Northwell Health, Optum and Stanford School of Medicine, with the American Society of Clinical Oncology contributing guidelines; it is in preview for Gemini Enterprise Standard and Plus customers, and one analyst expects production use to be six to twelve months out given compliance reviews. The same day Oura agreed to buy Counsel Health for $625 million in cash, subject to adjustments, six weeks after it shelved an IPO that had targeted up to $2.2 billion at about a $15 billion valuation. Counsel pairs a chat-based medical assistant with licensed physicians and has been inside the Oura app since June; about 70 staff join, chief executive Muthu Alagappan becomes chief clinical AI officer, existing health-plan and employer customers are kept, and Counsel had raised about $36 million. Oura reported preliminary unaudited revenue of $533 million for the September quarter, up 153%, with $87 million of adjusted EBITDA. Counsel's release says the deal closes before the end of 2026; TechCrunch is reported to have said 2027. *(Sources: Google Cloud press release, HIT Consultant, PYMNTS, The Next Web, Fierce Healthcare, STAT News, Counsel Health, Bloomberg, TechCrunch via Yahoo Finance, November 10–12)*

## Key Themes

**Governance has become the frontier risk.** The week's two biggest AI companies are being tested on who controls them rather than what they ship: Nvidia's chairless board is deciding overnight whether a founder with no obvious successor can stay, with a trillion dollars of market value already gone, while Anthropic's founders cash out $1.75 billion and keep a majority of the vote, and tell Australian retail buyers in writing that suing them will be hard. The models themselves barely featured in either story.

**Narrow and calibrated is the new cheap.** A day after Google undercut Fable 5.2 on price with Carbon, it shipped a model that does not generate text at all and charges nothing for output. Decision 1's pitch is a calibration error of 0.025 and a 200-millisecond answer, not a leaderboard score, and its own comparison table shows a smaller rival is cheaper still. xAI, by contrast, moved Grok Bot to 1.0 without naming a price ten days after Musk said serving it lost money.

**Capital is still flowing, but the terms are visible now.** CoreWeave can grow 168% and still need to swap 14% bonds for a 7% loan to keep building, Fractile raised at seven times May's valuation before shipping a chip, and Goldman has to model $685 billion of 2030 revenue to justify Anthropic's range. Mercor's round quietly includes a tender so early employees can sell. Investors are paying up, and they are asking for exits and cheaper debt at the same time.

**Healthcare is where agents are being allowed in, carefully.** Google's hospital agent reads Epic but cannot write to it, DeepMind labels AlphaGenome 2 research-only, and Oura bought licensed physicians along with Counsel's chat model. The pattern matches the UK banks' pre-emptive blocks from last week: the deployments that are happening put a human or a read-only boundary between the model and the record.
