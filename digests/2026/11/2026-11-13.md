## 1. Frontier Model News

### Jensen Huang resigned from Nvidia and CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, ending yesterday's overnight board standoff
Nvidia announced Thursday morning that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, CEO and director with immediate effect, resolving the board meeting that had produced no decision when yesterday's digest went out. The 8-K filing says the board accepted the resignation on November 11; the press release is dated November 12. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and keeps the finance job, and Harvey C. Jones becomes the company's first-ever board chair. The board says it will search for a permanent CEO among internal and external candidates. The release gives no reason for the exit and does not mention the affair reports or the independent probe opened on November 9. Shares opened at $151.01 and touched $148.80, a 14% fall, before closing at $166.19, down 4.3%, per Motley Fool's market wrap. Wedbush's Dan Ives expects Kress to serve about a year with an outsider taking the permanent job. CNN reports three House committees have moved their closed-door interview with Huang to December 1. Bloomberg says investors are now weighing Nvidia's agreement to buy about $10 billion of Anthropic shares in the $26 billion placement alongside next week's IPO; no source reports the pledge changing. Bloomberg separately reported that OpenAI told House offices it backs three chip export bills, the Chip Security, MATCH and AI Overwatch Acts, which Anthropic already supported and Nvidia has resisted, and Senate Majority Leader John Thune said tighter controls are "a high probability." *(Sources: Nvidia 8-K and press release, CNBC, CNN, Bloomberg, Motley Fool, Fox Business, November 12)*

### OpenAI has raised $15 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation, half its original target, per CNBC
CNBC's David Faber reported Thursday, citing people familiar with the deal, that OpenAI has raised about $15 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation. The round has not formally closed and is expected to wrap up within a couple of weeks; OpenAI has not announced it. The change since our November 2 story is the size and price: OpenAI first sought as much as $30 billion at $1.4 trillion, cut the target to about $20 billion, and Bloomberg reported on November 6 that roughly $11 billion had closed. The $15 billion figure is $4 billion above that tranche and about 47% above the $852 billion valuation of the March round. People familiar with the deal described it to CNBC as an opportunistic bridge to an initial public offering now expected in 2027, with the Wall Street Journal's reported $10.9 billion quarterly operating loss as the backdrop. SoftBank and Abu Dhabi's MGX are the only participants named across the coverage, and Faber said Nvidia and SoftBank have put roughly $30 billion and $65 billion into the company so far. One commentary piece notes that if $1.25 trillion is a pre-money figure the post-money valuation would be about $1.265 trillion. The raise lands a week before Anthropic's planned Nasdaq listing at $70 to $78 a share, and FourWeekMBA calculates the valuation at 29% above Anthropic's range midpoint. *(Sources: CNBC via TradingView and mlq.ai, Dataconomy, Dealroom, FourWeekMBA, November 12–13)*

### Google launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a Flash successor priced at $1.50/$7.50 that claims 2.5x the speed of Gemini 3.8 Flash
Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Thursday as the successor to Gemini 3.8 Flash and the first model in the line to drop the Flash name. It is priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, with cached input at $0.075, which VentureBeat notes is twice the current Flash price; several trackers say Flash itself rises to the same rate on January 1, 2027, a claim worth checking against Google's pricing page. Google's own figures, relayed by The Decoder and VentureBeat, say Hydrogen completes tasks 2.5 times as fast as 3.8 Flash at its default reasoning setting and scores 57% on Terminal-Bench 4.1 against 18.2% for Flash. Artificial Analysis's independent index puts Hydrogen at 51 at high effort and 54 at xhigh, up from 31 for Flash and one point above the larger Gemini 4 Argon at high, though still below the 55 that one review lists for Claude Sonnet 5.5. Google positions Hydrogen for everyday and agentic coding rather than frontier reasoning, with Argon and Carbon, which went generally available on Tuesday at $2/$10, remaining the top tier. Hydrogen is rolling out through the Gemini API, AI Studio, Gemini for business, the Gemini app and Google Search, and becomes the headline model of the $4.99 AI Plus plan; free users mostly still get Flash-Lite. *(Sources: VentureBeat, The Decoder, Neurohive, Android Headlines, Artificial Analysis via secondary coverage, November 12–13)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### OpenAI launched Daybreak Red Team and Defense Factory, agents that pen-test web apps and draft the patches
At its "Cyber at the Frontier" event on Thursday, OpenAI announced two Daybreak products. Daybreak Red Team, in early access by request, runs teams of agents against web applications in authenticated and unauthenticated modes, builds working proof-of-concept exploits, and re-runs them after a fix to confirm remediation. Daybreak Defense Factory, a research-preview plugin for workspaces with Daybreak access, takes findings from Red Team and from Wiz, Snyk and Endor Labs, and prepares draft GitHub pull requests that engineers must review before merging; it does not deploy. Outlets differ on whether the plugin lives in Codex or ChatGPT. The Register reports Defense Factory runs on GPT-6 Cyber, the model OpenAI released on November 5, and a keynote slide referenced an unnamed "upcoming cyber model" for which no written material exists. Nancy Wang, who leads cyber-applied AI at OpenAI, described Red Team as pairing deep penetration testing with automated scanning at scale. Neither product page states a price, and OpenAI has not said whether findings from customer code are used for training, a point a Dev Community analysis flags as varying by route. FourWeekMBA links the launch to OpenAI's earlier $1 billion cyber pledge under the Daybreak programme. *(Sources: SecurityWeek, GBHackers, The Deep View, mlq.ai, The Register via secondary coverage, November 12)*

### Anthropic opened self-hosted Claude Managed Agents, letting customers run the whole agent service on their own infrastructure
Anthropic's November 12 release note says customers can now run the entire Managed Agents service, not only the sandboxes, on their own cloud account or on-premise data centre, and store agents, sessions and session history there. Per the documentation, a deployment is a `cma-server` that serves the API and runs the agent loop, a PostgreSQL 16 or later database, and workers that execute the agent's shell and file tools inside the customer's sandboxes; without a database the server keeps state in memory and loses it on restart. The model still runs outside the customer's network: the server sends each model call, including tool inputs and results, to Anthropic by default or to Amazon Bedrock, Claude on Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry or an internal gateway. The docs carry a warning that the agent's bash tool is a real shell with the worker's permissions and that isolation comes only from the container or VM the operator provides. Help Net Security adds that a proxy outside the sandbox attaches credentials to MCP and supported API calls, so agent code never holds secrets. Access is by request through an Anthropic account team under a dedicated server licence; the release note does not label it beta, though most coverage calls it a private beta. No pricing or customers were disclosed. *(Sources: Anthropic release notes and documentation, Help Net Security, VKTR, November 12)*

### AWS released a free local emulator of more than 200 services and says autonomous agents wrote all of its code
AWS published AWS Emulator on November 11, a local offline emulator covering more than 200 AWS services, released on GitHub under a CC0 public-domain dedication except for a vendored framework directory under Apache 2.0. It needs no AWS account, works with the AWS CLI and SDKs, and is pitched at fast local and CI test loops for developers and for coding agents such as Kiro and Claude Code. The notable claim, made by AWS in its own post and repeated by Help Net Security and MarkTechPost, is that the code was written entirely by thousands of autonomous agents organised into three teams that write tests, find issues and fix code, with no human code review. The limitations are material: reviewers note it skips IAM checks and cross-region behaviour, code run through it is not sandboxed and can modify local files with the developer's permissions, and MarkTechPost cites a 56.6% fidelity figure that AWS's own post does not publish. The timing, two and a half weeks before re:Invent, suggests a showcase for Amazon's agentic-coding push as much as a developer tool. *(Sources: About Amazon, Help Net Security, MarkTechPost, Cloud Computing News, November 11–12)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### United Airlines blocked third-party AI agents from booking flights, citing credential risk and FAA check-in rules
United announced on November 11 that unauthorised third-party AI agents may no longer book or change flights on its platforms, directly or through travel agencies and aggregators. The policy names assistants built into social, messaging and search platforms, AI-enabled browsers, standalone agents and any assistant reaching United through an intermediary's tools. Agents can still display schedules and fares, per the Wall Street Journal, but cannot complete a purchase. United's stated reasons are that sharing account credentials with personal assistants can expose data and cause unintended purchases, account changes or MileagePlus redemptions, and that automated check-in could breach FAA and Transportation Department rules that a human complete it. Bookings made by such tools before November 11 will be honoured, and the airline will update its Digital Trust Center with guidance. United has not said how it will detect agents or when enforcement starts, and one outlet notes the policy may be a contractual right to cancel rather than a technical block. CEO Scott Kirby told the Journal, "I don't want to create another middleman." Fast Company reports American and Delta will allow some agents to book, Southwest launched a ChatGPT plugin for bookings last month, and the move follows Bank of America's warning last week that customers should not share passwords with assistants, and the pre-emptive block by five UK banks reported on November 9. *(Sources: TechCrunch, Bloomberg, Wall Street Journal and Fast Company via secondary coverage, PhocusWire, TechRepublic, November 11–12)*

### Naver signed its first AI Factory customer, a 2 trillion won deal with an unnamed US inference company, as Q3 profit fell 21%
Naver said on November 12 that its AI Factory venture with Nvidia and Brookfield has signed a first supply contract worth about 2 trillion won, which outlets convert to $1.4 billion to $1.5 billion, with a US-based AI inference company it did not name. The contract covers roughly 20 megawatts of GPU capacity over four to five years, with supply starting in 2027; Digitimes reports the mix as Nvidia B300 and Vera Rubin. The facility is scheduled to open at 55 MW in the first half of 2027 and reach 200 MW by 2028, and CFO Kim Nam-sun said Naver is negotiating with more than ten prospective customers outside Asia for the remaining 180 MW. The deal arrived with weak earnings: third-quarter revenue hit a record 3.47 trillion won, up 10.7%, but operating profit fell 20.9% to 451.3 billion won on AI infrastructure spending, and shares fell. Nvidia has agreed to invest $1 billion in Naver through a paid-in capital increase and Brookfield has committed up to $9 billion of infrastructure funding, per the companies' earlier announcements. The customer's identity, the GPU mix and the contract term are all Naver's or secondary outlets' accounts; no counterparty has confirmed. *(Sources: Korea Times, Korea Herald, Digitimes, Seoul Economic Daily, KED Global, Korea JoongAng Daily, November 12)*

### Databricks says GM runs more than 1,000 Genie agents as it relaunches Genie as an always-on autonomous coworker
Databricks announced the second generation of Genie on November 11, repositioning the Genie One product it launched in June as an always-on agent that runs long-running enterprise work in the background on its own compute. Users delegate jobs through Genie One, email, Slack or Microsoft Teams, and Genie runs them on a schedule or in response to events. Governance runs through Unity Gateway and Unity Catalog: administrators set which tools Genie may call, approval rules and spending caps, and Genie acts under each user's identity. Genie Agents can now read PDFs and slide decks alongside tables in public preview, Genie Ontology is generally available, and OpenSharing and Genie Clean Rooms are in beta. The implementation claim is General Motors, which Databricks says has deployed more than 1,000 Genie agents across sales, engineering, supply chain and HR; the figure is Databricks' own and GM has not published it. Databricks also says thousands of customers use Genie in production and that Genie One is free through January 31, 2027, which conflicts with its July move to usage-based pricing for the product. Databricks cautions that its benchmark results reflect an established deployment and that gains may be smaller for new ones. *(Sources: Databricks press release and blog, Unite.AI, Techzine, SiliconANGLE, November 11–12)*

## Key Themes

**The Nvidia succession is now a governance and policy story, not just a personnel one.** Huang's exit resolved the board question but opened two others: whether Nvidia's $10 billion Anthropic placement proceeds unchanged under an interim CEO, and whether the scandal hands Congress the votes to attach chip export bills to the defence authorisation. OpenAI's decision to back the same bills Anthropic already supported, within hours of Huang's departure, suggests the labs no longer fear crossing their largest supplier.

**Capital is being raised at whatever price clears.** OpenAI took $15 billion at $1.25 trillion after asking for $30 billion at $1.4 trillion, and the deal is explicitly a bridge to a 2027 listing. Anthropic lists next week at a range whose midpoint OpenAI's round exceeds by less than a third. Naver's record revenue with a 21% profit drop shows the same squeeze one layer down: infrastructure spending is running ahead of the customers to fill it.

**Vendors are moving agents inside the customer's perimeter.** Anthropic's self-hosted Managed Agents, Databricks' Unity Gateway controls and OpenAI's review-before-merge Defense Factory all answer the same enterprise objection, that agents cannot be trusted with credentials and production code. United's booking ban is the consumer-side mirror image: an institution refusing agents it cannot authorise.

**Agents are now writing the tools other agents run on.** AWS's emulator, built with no human code review, and Google's cheaper Hydrogen tier aimed at agentic coding both treat the agent, not the developer, as the primary user.
