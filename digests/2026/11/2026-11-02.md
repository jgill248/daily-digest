# Daily AI Digest — October 30 – November 2, 2026

## 1. Frontier Model News

### OpenAI cuts its funding round to about $20 billion and has roughly $11 billion committed, with a UAE consortium led by MGX as the anchor
The Financial Times reported on October 30 that OpenAI has lowered the target for its current round from about $30 billion to about $20 billion, and that the "first close" is expected to come in below even that figure after some investors trimmed their allocations. The Wall Street Journal, in a follow-up summarized by TechCrunch and CNBC, put commitments at about $11 billion as of the Friday deadline OpenAI had set, and described the response from some large investors as "tepid." The $1.25 trillion valuation is unchanged from the reduction this digest covered on October 23, so what is new is the size of the round, not the price. A consortium of UAE investors including MGX, the Abu Dhabi fund founded by Mubadala and G42, is expected to be the largest participant, and per Forbes the deal gives OpenAI an option to lease newly built compute capacity in the Gulf, a structure that pairs capital with data-centre supply. Wealthy individuals are to get a later retail tranche at the same valuation. The backdrop is the worst month for the AI trade in over a year: the Nasdaq fell 5.3% in October, its worst month since March 2025, and Intel dropped 28.6% on October 30, its worst day since 1974. OpenAI has not commented on the FT or WSJ figures, the round has not closed, and Dealroom reports more commitments are expected in the coming weeks. *(Sources: Financial Times via CNBC and TechCrunch, The Wall Street Journal, Forbes, Benzinga, CNN, October 30 – November 2)*

### Anthropic files to sell up to ¥552.9 billion of IPO shares in Japan, and renames its top consumer plan Ultra with the five-hour limit removed
Reuters reported from Tokyo on November 2 that Anthropic will sell up to 552.9 billion yen, about $3.5 billion at Reuters' 157.82 rate, of shares to Japanese investors as part of its planned Nasdaq listing, according to a filing made in Japan that day. The S-1 filed on October 26 had named Japan among the jurisdictions where Anthropic plans public offerings alongside Australia, Canada, EEA states, Switzerland and the UK, but this is the first sizing of any tranche. The Japanese filing did not state the overall offering size or valuation, and the price range in the S-1 is still blank. For scale, Japanese investors took $2.2 billion of SpaceX's $75 billion June IPO. Reports of a listing in the week of November 16 remain unconfirmed; the earliest a roadshow can start under the 15-day rule is about November 10, and one secondary source naming Mizuho Securities as manager of the Japan portion is not corroborated by Reuters. Separately, on October 30 Anthropic renamed its two highest personal plans: Max 5x becomes Max at $100 a month, and Max 20x becomes Ultra at $200, with existing subscribers moved automatically and prices unchanged. Verified Ultra accounts lose the five-hour session limit and keep a weekly limit of ten times Pro, which Anthropic says has not changed; Max keeps both limits. Mike Overell, who leads subscription product, said on X the aim was to stop users "getting blocked mid-session." *(Sources: Reuters via Investing.com and TradingView, Seeking Alpha, Windows Report, Crypto Briefing, Mezha, October 30 – November 2)*

### OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic agree to honour a single robots.txt token that opts a site out of AI training
The Verge reported on October 30 that the four largest US labs will each honour `__usage_ai-training`, a shared user-agent token in robots.txt that a site can disallow once instead of maintaining separate rules for GPTBot, Google-Extended, Bingbot and ClaudeBot. The token comes out of the IETF's AI Preferences (AIPREF) working group and is specified in an individual Internet-Draft by Kevin Kelley of Anthropic; the IETF notes that individual drafts carry no formal standing, so this is a vendor commitment layered on a draft, not a standard. Per secondary coverage of the announcement, the draft adds no new directives, so existing robots.txt parsers can read it, and its scope is deliberately narrow: it governs use of crawled pages for training only, does not affect search indexing, and does not stop AI products from fetching pages to ground or cite answers. One blog claims that for Google the shared token also removes a site from Gemini answers and that an explicit Google-Extended group overrides it; the other outlets do not address this, so treat that nuance as unconfirmed. Rollout is staggered by company and compliance is voluntary, as with all robots.txt. The move follows years of publishers complaining that per-crawler opt-outs were unworkable, and it lands days before Anthropic's IPO and in the middle of OpenAI's fundraising, when both companies are courting publishers as licensing partners. *(Sources: The Verge via AI Weekly, ShiftDelete, Fireup, Zetik, October 30 – 31)*

### OpenAI will push mandatory "speed limits" on frontier AI after the midterms, as data-centre pauses become a live issue in up to 15 governor races
Axios reported on November 1 that OpenAI plans a policy and public-relations campaign to begin right after Tuesday's midterm elections, timed to the lame-duck congressional session that opens November 9. Chris Lehane, OpenAI's chief global affairs officer, told Axios "you're going to see us talk more and more about the concept of speed limits," meaning mandatory limits on the most powerful systems, and said his team has been stockpiling proposals since August. The second plank is state-level "community compacts" on data-centre siting, and the third is branding: OpenAI wants to call its facilities "AI compute campuses," which a spokesperson told Business Insider "better describes the scale and role of the infrastructure." Axios has a licensing agreement with OpenAI and disclosed it. The rebrand answers a backlash The Information quantified the same day: Shane Burke estimates candidates backing a pause on new data centres could win up to 15 of 36 governor races, with Democratic nominees in Florida, Georgia, Ohio and Texas backing moratoriums and Republicans split, and Fox News counts 34 local data-centre ballot measures in seven states. New York is still the only state with a statewide moratorium. Also on October 30, the Office of the National Cyber Director told Protect Democracy it would not release the seven-page draft of the White House's voluntary AI testing framework, citing national-security and commercial exemptions, despite a September agreement in the group's FOIA suit. *(Sources: Axios via Techmeme and Implicator, Business Insider, The Information, Daily Caller, Fox News, The Hill, October 30 – November 2)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### DeepSeek open-sources dsh-superagent, a research prototype of a proactive coding agent that lives inside one GitHub repository
DeepSeek's Harness team published `deepseek-ai/dsh-superagent-prototype` on October 31 under the MIT licence, and the repository had 11,200 stars and 1,100 forks by November 2. The README describes a proactive coding agent bound to a single GitHub repository, packaged for DeepSeek Harness, that listens to GitHub and optionally to Feishu group chats. When an authorised person asks for work, it opens a work item, runs the task in its own agent session and working directory, and replies in the original thread, typically with a pull request; a web panel lists what still needs a human. The stated design goal is to "take as little human attention as possible, and give as much value as possible," and the agent acts only when asked or when a team-written rule permits it. TechNode and MarkTechPost describe a two-layer split in which one resident router talks to people and dispatches work while isolated workers run tasks in parallel, with proactive behaviour held in an observe mode until a team switches it on; the README itself does not use those terms. The repository is explicit that it is "research prototype for reference only, not an official DeepSeek product," that the code differs from DeepSeek's internal version, and that some designs in the accompanying article, "Exploring Super-agent Design: Towards a More Autonomous Agent System," are future directions not included. It does not name supported models or publish benchmarks. Setup is meant to be driven by a coding agent reading SETUP.md. *(Sources: GitHub, TechNode, MarkTechPost, DataStudios, October 31 – November 2)*

### Google's Computer Use tool reaches general availability in the Gemini API, 388 days after its first preview
Google's Gemini API changelog entry dated October 30 lists Computer Use as generally available across both the Interactions and GenerateContent APIs, per FourWeekMBA and Creators Toolbox, which both read the entry; Google posted no separate blog. The tool lets a model drive a browser, mobile or desktop environment from screenshots and return click, type and scroll actions for the developer's harness to execute. The first preview, built on Gemini 2.5, shipped in October 2025, and in June computer use became a built-in tool in Gemini 3.5 Flash. The current docs name Gemini 3.8 Flash as the recommended model, with Gemini 4 Argon, 3.5 Flash-Lite and 3 Flash Preview also listed. Per FourWeekMBA's reading of the pricing page, Computer Use is billed at ordinary token rates on the paid tier and is unavailable on the free tier; the changelog itself gives no usage limits. This is distinct from the September 9 GA of Computer Use and Shell sandboxes on the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, which covers hosted execution environments rather than the API tool. Google's tools overview page still carried a "Preview" label when checked, so the surfaces may lag the changelog. The GA puts Google level with OpenAI, whose GPT-6.1 Terra launched October 27 with computer use as its headline feature, and with Anthropic's long-standing computer-use tool. *(Sources: Gemini API changelog via FourWeekMBA and Creators Toolbox, Google AI for Developers, October 30 – November 1)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### ADNOC becomes the first energy company to deploy OpenAI Frontier, with field planning and barrel valuation as the first two uses
Abu Dhabi National Oil Company signed a multi-year agreement with OpenAI and G42 at ADIPEC on November 2, witnessed by Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed and Sam Altman, making it the first energy company to deploy OpenAI Frontier, OpenAI's platform for coordinating agents across business operations. The partners will build a bespoke decision-intelligence capability from OpenAI's frontier models and ADNOC's proprietary industrial data, running on G42's sovereign infrastructure in the UAE. The first application is field development planning: ADNOC says reasoning models will process decades of data from thousands of wells, stacked reservoirs and hundreds of surface facilities to optimise a whole field at once, and expects plans that took months to take hours. The second is barrel valuation, in which interconnected agents price oil still in the ground by anticipating supply, demand, prices and margins. ADNOC says human experts keep oversight of major decisions. The deal builds on ADNOC's rollout of ChatGPT Enterprise to more than 40,000 employees. Altman called it a "first-of-its-kind partnership" that would help ADNOC "move faster, operate more efficiently and make better decisions across the energy value chain." The Abu Dhabi Media Office said OpenAI signed three further agreements the same day, with Mubadala, G42's Inception42 and the Abu Dhabi government. No financial terms or deployment timetable were disclosed, and the "hours not months" claim is ADNOC's expectation, not a result. The same UAE investors are anchoring OpenAI's funding round. *(Sources: Gulf News, Zawya, Unite.AI, Energy Connects, ITP.net, Il Sole 24 Ore, November 2)*

### Aramco closes its 49% stake in HUMAIN with about $2.6 billion of cash and AI assets, giving Saudi Arabia's AI champion two state owners
Saudi Aramco and the Public Investment Fund said on November 1 that they have closed the transaction under which Aramco takes 49% of HUMAIN through a wholly owned subsidiary, with PIF keeping 51%. Aramco pays by transferring its key AI assets into HUMAIN and contributing about SAR 9.9 billion, roughly $2.6 billion, in cash during 2026; the release describes the cash as a contribution to HUMAIN rather than a payment to PIF, and no valuation was disclosed. The structure dates to a non-binding term sheet signed October 28, 2025, and one aggregator's claim that the money sits in escrow pending project approvals contradicts the closing announcement and appears nowhere else. HUMAIN, launched by PIF in May 2025, targets three gigawatts of AI data-centre capacity by 2030 and has supply and partnership agreements with Nvidia, AMD, Qualcomm, AWS and others. Aramco says it will use HUMAIN's infrastructure and services to accelerate AI deployment across its own industrial operations, which makes it both shareholder and anchor customer. The deal lands a day before ADNOC's OpenAI agreement across the Gulf, and together they show the two largest national oil companies choosing opposite routes: Aramco is buying into a domestic compute and model vendor, while ADNOC is renting a US lab's platform on a local partner's sovereign cloud. *(Sources: Aramco newsroom via Gulf Business and Unite.AI, Mugglehead, Saudi Gulf Projects, Crypto Briefing, November 1 – 2)*

### Apple confirms Alibaba's Qwen will power Apple Intelligence in China, with a developer beta November 4 and public release in iOS 27.3 in December
Apple and Alibaba announced on October 31 that Qwen models will handle parts of Apple Intelligence for users in mainland China, with testing in the iOS developer beta from November 4 and general release in iOS 27.3 in December. The Qwen-backed features are Siri requests, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence; the next-generation Siri and other features shown at WWDC 2026 are excluded from this first rollout. Eligibility is tied to iPhones bought in China, or to devices whose location and Apple Account region are both set to China. China's cyberspace regulator completed the filing for Apple's on-device generative-AI service in July, which cleared the regulatory path. Baidu told TechCrunch it is also working with Apple, which matches long-running reports that Apple kept two Chinese partners in play. The arrangement ends a gap of more than two years in which Apple Intelligence was unavailable in Apple's second-largest market, where it has lost share to Huawei and Xiaomi, and it means Apple ships a competitor's model to end users in a way it has avoided elsewhere, where it uses its own foundation models with OpenAI and Google as optional tie-ins. Reports differ on whether November 4 is the beta's release date or merely the start of testing, and MacRumors notes the date in China may correspond to November 3 in the US. Neither company disclosed commercial terms. *(Sources: The Next Web, The Apple Post, Technology.org, Türkiye Today, TechCrunch, October 31 – November 2)*

## Key Themes

**The Gulf is the swing capital of this cycle.** Within four days, MGX's consortium became the anchor of OpenAI's shrunken round with a compute-lease option attached, ADNOC became OpenAI Frontier's first energy customer on G42's sovereign cloud, and Aramco closed its stake in HUMAIN. The same sovereign funds are now investor, infrastructure landlord and customer to the frontier labs at once, and the price of their capital is that the compute gets built at home.

**Private markets are repricing with the public ones.** October was the Nasdaq's worst month since March 2025 and the week's earnings sold off on capex, and the private-market echo arrived immediately: OpenAI's round target fell by a third and the first close will be smaller still. Anthropic's Japan tranche is the counter-signal, sizing international demand for an IPO that has not yet set a price range.

**Opt-out is becoming infrastructure.** One robots.txt token backed by four labs is the first interoperable consent mechanism for training data, narrow by design and voluntary by nature. Its arrival in the same week as OpenAI's "speed limits" campaign and the White House's refusal to publish its testing framework shows the labs would rather write the rules themselves than have them written in state capitols, where up to 15 incoming governors may have campaigned on pausing their data centres.

**Agents are moving into the repository and the oilfield.** DeepSeek's prototype treats a single GitHub repo as the agent's whole world and gates proactive work behind team rules; Google's computer-use GA gives any Gemini developer a browser-driving agent at token prices; ADNOC is wiring agents into field planning with humans on the major decisions. The pattern across all three is the same as last week's: the engineering effort is in authorization and scope, not in capability.

**Sovereignty cuts both ways for Apple.** Shipping Qwen inside iOS in China is the price of selling Apple Intelligence there, and it is the clearest case yet of a US platform distributing a Chinese frontier model to consumers, the mirror image of the distillation disputes and chip rules that dominated last week.
