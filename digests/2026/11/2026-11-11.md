## 1. Frontier Model News

### Anthropic's bankers weigh pricing the IPO above the $70 to $78 range after the book was covered on day one, the FT reports, as TSMC commits up to $500 million at the IPO price
A day after the roadshow opened, the Financial Times reported that underwriters led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are considering pricing Anthropic's offering above the $70 to $78 range, citing people familiar with the book. Institutional orders covered the 825 million shares on an allocable basis on the first day, per the report as relayed by Seeking Alpha, TipRanks and MLQ. Pricing above the band would lift the fully diluted valuation past $1.5 trillion and push total proceeds, including the $26 billion private placement, beyond $100 billion; three backers told the FT the company is prioritising cash over valuation given roughly $900 billion of compute commitments. CNBC's sources put pricing on Wednesday, November 18, with trading on Nasdaq as "ANT" the following day. Separately, TSMC's board approved up to $500 million for Anthropic common shares at the IPO price and up to $500 million for OpenAI preferred stock, according to a Form 6-K filed with the SEC alongside a record NT$9 quarterly dividend and a $500 million allocation to a semiconductor-ecosystem fund; the OpenAI line carries no price or timing, and both figures are ceilings rather than commitments. TSMC shares rose more than 4% in Taipei on Wednesday, helped by October revenue up 60% year on year. No final price has been set, and the FT's account of the order book is anonymous-source reporting that Anthropic and its banks have not confirmed. *(Sources: Financial Times via Seeking Alpha and TipRanks, CNBC, TSMC Form 6-K via SEC, Investing.com, Benzinga, BusinessToday, November 10–11)*

### OpenAI told investors Q3 revenue reached about $10 billion while its adjusted operating loss widened to $10.9 billion, as SoftBank booked a $7 billion quarterly loss on a data-centre power writedown
The Wall Street Journal reported, from investor documents it reviewed, that OpenAI's revenue for the three months to September was about $10 billion, the first quarter above that line and roughly 50% more than the second quarter, while the adjusted operating loss grew to $10.9 billion from $10.1 billion. The adjusted operating margin improved to minus 109% from minus 150%. The figures were given on an investor call on Tuesday, November 10; The Information also reported them, OpenAI did not comment, and several outlets quote finance chief Sarah Friar saying enterprise revenue doubled and now matches consumer revenue. Anthropic's amended S-1, by comparison, showed $16.96 billion of Q3 revenue and a $567 million operating loss. The same day OpenAI's largest backer reported its first quarterly loss in two years: SoftBank Group posted a net loss of about ¥1.12 trillion, roughly $7 billion, for July to September, driven by a $3.7 billion writedown on non-refundable advance payments for power equipment for its 8-gigawatt Ohio project and other US data centres, plus higher financing costs and Intel's slide. Revenue of ¥2.22 trillion beat the ¥2.02 trillion estimate. Its $64.6 billion OpenAI stake, about 13%, was not marked down, leaving a $45 billion cumulative gain, and no new round is reflected until formally announced. SoftBank shares fell as much as 11% in Tokyo on Wednesday. One secondary source claims OpenAI's quarter was cash-flow positive on a different measure, which conflicts with the WSJ figures and is unverified. *(Sources: Wall Street Journal via Crypto Briefing and Storyboard18, The Information, DigiTimes, Bloomberg, Investing.com, Benzinga, BigGo Finance, November 10–11)*

### Google makes Gemini 4 Carbon generally available at $2/$10 per million tokens until November 30, undercutting Fable 5.2 by three-quarters while conceding Argon is still better for most tasks
Gemini 4 Carbon, first announced on October 28 with a cyber-defenders-first rollout, opened to everyone on November 10. Google's post puts it on the paid Gemini API and in Gemini for business at the same introductory price as Argon, $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output; third-party trackers say that doubles to $4/$20 on December 1, which Google does not state. AI Ultra subscribers get it in the Gemini app, AI Mode and Antigravity, and the free API tier excludes it. Google frames Carbon narrowly, saying Argon remains the better choice for ordinary and even most software-engineering tasks, with Carbon for long multi-step coding and scientific research; the evidence offered is a customer's 94% resolution rate with zero regressions across 16 real bug fixes in a 14,000-test codebase. VentureBeat's comparison, using Artificial Analysis data, has Fable 5.2 ahead at 65 to 60 on the Intelligence Index at top effort, with Carbon edging Fable 58 to 56 at medium effort but costing more per task, $6.59 against $3.80; at high effort Carbon's $7.76 per task is well under Fable's $26.54 at maximum. Google also disclosed two TPU variants on November 9: TPU 8tx, the 8t training silicon paired with custom AMD EPYC hosts so data stays encrypted in use for governments and regulated customers, and TPU 8i Max, two 8i dies in one package with double the compute, SRAM and HBM bandwidth. The circulating 384 GB and 17.2 TB/s figures are derived by outlets rather than stated by Google, and availability is reported as 2027. *(Sources: Google, VentureBeat, Technology.org, MLQ, TechCrunch, MarkTechPost, Futurum, WebProNews, November 9–11)*

### Alibaba opens a Qwen4-Max preview at 02:01 UTC today, subscription-only, with no weights, benchmarks or per-token price
Five days after the "The becoming begins" teaser and the unlabelled Qoduck model in Qoder, Alibaba's Qwen team said the first preview of Qwen4-Max, the flagship of the Qwen4 family, is available from November 11. Access is only through QwenCloud's flat-rate Token Plan, with promotional pricing from about $3 a month for new users across Essential, Standard and Pro tiers that allow one to eight concurrent agents, plus early access inside QwenWork, Qoder, the Qwen app and QwenStudio; the model ID is qwen4-max-preview and the preview closes on November 25, Beijing time. There is no pay-as-you-go billing, and Alibaba has not published weights, a model card, a licence, a context length or a single benchmark number. Its only claims are that the model beats Qwen3.8 on world knowledge, code generation and sustained performance on long tasks with repeated tool calls, and that it targets complex programming, front-end design and agentic work. The widely quoted 1 million token context comes from a merged Qwen Code pull request that sets that default for Qwen4 identifiers, not from a spec sheet. Token Plan is scoped to interactive use in coding tools including Qwen Code, Cline, Claude Code, Cursor, OpenCode and Codex and excludes batch scripts. Earlier reporting has the family including Flash, Plus and an on-premise 27B model, none of which has shipped. The stable predecessor, Qwen3.8-Max, lists at $2/$6 per million tokens. *(Sources: Qwen on X, KuCoin News, AlphaSignal, Cellcog, ChainCatcher, BigGo Finance, November 11)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Anthropic adds Sheets and Whiteboards in beta, renames Dashboards as Claude Data, and TechCrunch reports Claude Meetings joining Google Meet calls for Team and Enterprise
Anthropic's November 10 post puts Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards in beta on web, desktop and mobile for all paid plans, and makes the Dashboards product generally available as Claude Data. Sheets generates spreadsheets with tabs, formulas, formatting and drop-downs from a prompt, edits cells and lists each change; Anthropic calls it simple for now and points to .xlsx export or Claude for Excel for the full feature set. Whiteboards is a shared canvas where Claude turns a spoken plan into a timeline with owners and dates, groups retro notes by theme, answers questions about any boxed region, and exports to a doc, deck, Lucid or Miro. Claude Data requires a warehouse or CRM connector such as Redshift or BigQuery; certified queries, data stories and live sharing are in beta, and shared viewers see only approved query results. Connectors such as Adobe, Atlassian, Box and Notion can now open as full apps beside the chat. Team plans get all three on by default; Enterprise starts with Sheets and Whiteboards off and turns Data on by default on November 19. TechCrunch reports a fourth product, Claude Meetings, in beta for Team and Enterprise: Claude joins Google Meet as a visible participant, announces itself in chat, drafts plans during the call, leaves if an external guest joins unless an admin allows it, and files notes and action items to claude.ai. Anthropic's own post does not mention Meetings and other write-ups call it "coming soon", so its status is unresolved. *(Sources: Anthropic, TechCrunch, NewsBytes, Online Tech Tips, DataStudios, November 10)*

### OpenAI launches Businesses in ChatGPT with claimable profiles and verified Business Agents, and buys travel agent Odessia, sending Expedia and Booking lower
OpenAI opened Businesses in ChatGPT in beta on November 10. Companies claim their listing in a new Business Hub, prove ownership by email or phone code, DNS record or a connected Shopify store, and then pass identity verification through Persona before they can publish changes, connect a product feed, see aggregate insights on mentions and referred traffic, or create an agent; a blue check appears once Persona approves. Business Agents, which answer customer questions inside ChatGPT, are limited at launch to US e-commerce and online-service businesses, and local stores and restaurants can only claim an existing US listing. Both are free, OpenAI says chats are not shared with businesses, and VP of monetization Benji Shomair told a briefing that verification uses "multiple methods for different types of business in different markets" without detailing them, and that claiming a profile does not change how often ChatGPT recommends a company. The same day Sonder co-founder Francis Davidson said OpenAI had acquired Odessia, his AI travel-planning startup with a $6 million Sequoia-led pre-seed, and that he is joining OpenAI; the standalone service has shut. Terms were not disclosed, TechCrunch describes it as an acqui-hire, and accounts differ on whether OpenAI confirmed the deal. After The Information reported it, Expedia fell as much as 5% and Booking and Airbnb also dropped on fears that assistants will replace search-and-compare travel sites, though OpenAI has announced no travel product. *(Sources: TechCrunch, Adweek, Search Engine Watch, The Next Web, Skift, Investing.com, Yahoo Finance, November 10–11)*

### Adobe puts its Creative Agent inside Premiere, Illustrator, InDesign and Frame.io as public betas at MAX, previews MCP access for outside agents such as Claude, and previews always-on agents
At its MAX keynote in Miami on November 10, incoming chief executive Anil Chakravarthy said the Creative Agent, previously confined to Firefly, now runs as an AI Assistant in public beta across Premiere, Illustrator, InDesign and Frame.io, with Photoshop's version available only in the Photoshop beta app. The assistant gained a planning mode that shows the proposed workflow for review before it runs, and a Premiere mobile agent for iOS and Android entered private preview. Adobe is also previewing local Model Context Protocol integrations so third-party agents can edit native project files in Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere and After Effects; the on-stage demo used Claude to prepare a tiger illustration in Illustrator for screen printing, and Adobe says it is still deciding which commands to expose. Always-on agents that handle recurring work such as organising incoming assets and quality checks were previewed in Firefly, with sources split on whether they are available now or arrive in early 2027. New tools include Relight and Generative Restyle in Photoshop and Generative Remove in beta in Premiere, and Firefly now offers partner models from ByteDance, Google, Kling, OpenAI and ElevenLabs alongside Adobe's own. Pricing and generative-credit rules for the agent features were not announced. This follows Monday's Disney deal to build private Firefly Foundry models on Disney characters. *(Sources: Adobe, Variety, PetaPixel, Digital Trends, heise, Camera Jabber, Startup Fortune, November 10)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Gemini Robotics 2.5 reports 96% success putting Apptronik humanoids on live Mercedes-Benz parts-kitting lines, with no trial count and no public API
Google DeepMind released Gemini Robotics 2.5 to robotics partners on November 10, calling it its most capable vision-language-action model and saying it improved success rates by 30% to 100% and completion speed by 20% to 30% over its predecessor across more than 200 tasks, including serving food with tongs, tying ribbons on gift boxes and pipetting liquids. The real-world claim is a Mercedes-Benz proof of concept in which Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid did automotive kitting on production lines in Kecskemét, Hungary, and Sindelfingen, Germany, at a 96% success rate, with Gemini Robotics ER tracking the workflow and Robotics 2.5 driving the hands and arms. DeepMind published no trial count, duration or definition of success, the figure covers one parts-handling task with one partner, and Mercedes-Benz has not issued its own statement. Partner lists differ across outlets, with Agile Robots and Apptronik consistent and Boston Dynamics in some. Alongside the model, DeepMind open-sourced ASIMOV-Jailbreak, a benchmark of 100 scenarios and 1,500 adversarial prompts designed to push a robot toward unsafe actions; the 99.5% resistance score in several headlines belongs to Gemini 4 Argon, which DeepMind says leads its agentic robotics evaluations, against about 82% for the previous Gemini Robotics ER 2 baseline, and the post gives no ASIMOV-Jailbreak score for Robotics 2.5 itself. There is no general-availability date or pricing. *(Sources: Google DeepMind, The Decoder, MLQ, TechTimes, Digg, Crypto Briefing, IoT Tech News, November 10–11)*

### Reddit turns subreddit threads into AI-written review pages for about 10,000 products, with sentiment counts, links to source comments and retailer links it says it takes no cut from
Reddit introduced Reddit Reviews on November 10, rolling out over the next few weeks to US users on iOS, Android and the web. Each page covers one product and carries an AI summary of what Redditors have said, a count of positive, mixed and negative comments, and links back to the underlying threads; the launch set is about 10,000 frequently discussed products across electronics, health and beauty, home and garden, and fashion. Some pages show prices, images and buy links, but only where a brand or retailer has supplied a catalogue; Reddit told The Verge that merchants cannot pay for inclusion and it takes no cut of sales from the links, though it has not said which catalogues qualify or whether affiliate commissions apply. Reddit's justification is that half of US shoppers already use the site to check recommendations they get from AI tools, and it is launching ahead of holiday shopping. The obvious risk is astroturfing: moderators told The Verge they have already caught brands trying to plant positive sentiment, and Reddit's answer is its site rules, automated spam defences and volunteer moderators, with no new disclosure or detection measures announced. The feature is described as a test by some outlets and a launch by others. *(Sources: The Verge via AI Weekly, The Next Web, PPC Land, Technobezz, NewsBytes, November 10)*

## Key Themes

**The money and the product arrived in the same 24 hours, and they point opposite ways.** Anthropic's order book filled on day one and bankers are talking about pricing above the range, TSMC is putting up to $1 billion into the two labs, and OpenAI crossed $10 billion of quarterly revenue, while the same OpenAI documents show an $11 billion quarterly loss and the lab's biggest backer wrote off $3.7 billion of power equipment it paid for in advance. Investors are buying growth and a story about compute, and the compute is where the losses are.

**Every lab is now shipping a workplace.** Anthropic's Sheets, Whiteboards, Data and reportedly Meetings, OpenAI's Business Hub and verified agents inside ChatGPT, Adobe's agents inside Premiere and Illustrator and Reddit's AI review pages are all the same move: take the model out of the chat box and put it where the work or the purchase already happens. The interesting detail is interoperability, with Adobe letting Claude edit native files over MCP and Anthropic opening Notion and Atlassian as full apps beside the chat.

**Price is the new benchmark.** Google opened Carbon at a quarter of Fable 5.2's rate and immediately told customers to keep using the cheaper Argon for most work, Alibaba put Qwen4-Max behind a $3 subscription with no spec sheet at all, and the Carbon-versus-Fable comparison that circulated was cost per task, not a leaderboard score. With Fable and Carbon within a few index points of each other, the labs are competing on what a completed task costs, and DeepMind's 96% kitting figure is a reminder that in the physical world the trial count still matters more than the headline.
