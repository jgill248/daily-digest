## 1. Frontier Model News

### AI stocks rebounded into the shortened Black Friday session, with Anthropic back above its $83 IPO price premarket and the Kospi up 4.3%
After Wednesday's intraday bear-market scare, Friday opened as a relief rally. Forbes reported Anthropic up 3.6% to $85.73 in premarket trading, back above its $83 IPO price for the first time since Monday, with Nvidia up roughly 4.2% to $155.20 and AMD up 5%, while Nasdaq 100 futures were about 2.7% higher ahead of the 1 p.m. ET close. Asia set the tone: the Kospi rose 4.34% to 4,915.68, Samsung gained 4.55% to 179,100 won and SK hynix 5.41% to 1.11 million won, while the Nikkei added around 2% and SoftBank about 3.1%, two days after the Kospi's 6.2% plunge. Korean coverage tied the bounce to a US Section 301 proposal that would put tariffs of up to 15% on Taiwanese goods but exempt chips, equipment, computers and phones. Friday was also the first session in which Nvidia, Dell and Supermicro could react to the FT's report of a halt on server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries. The Nasdaq 100 was still tracking its worst month since December 2022, down about 8% through Wednesday, and Seoul remained about 16% lower in November. We could not confirm a US close before publication. *(Sources: Forbes, TradingKey, Techi, Tokenpost, Seoul Economic Daily, November 27)*

### OpenAI fixed a launch-day billing error that undercounted GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol usage, so limits now run out 40% and 25% faster, as Private Inference entered alpha
Thibault Sottiaux, who runs Codex and ChatGPT product work, said on X on November 26 that a mistake at the November 17 launch of GPT-6.2 had discounted usage of the Astra and Sol models for most users. With the correction, the same work now consumes about 40% more of an allowance on Astra and 25% more on Sol; other models were unaffected and OpenAI will not bill retroactively. As compensation, Go, Plus, Pro and Business accounts each receive one "banked reset," redeemable from the usage settings on web and desktop once an allowance is below 80%. Sottiaux did not say what caused the discount, which users were hit, or what the corrected per-model rates are, and some users called a single reset "pretty thin." The fix lands a day after OpenAI began merging ChatGPT's Chat and Work modes under one meter, which it has already admitted will hit heavy users sooner. Separately, Sam Altman said on November 25 that OpenAI has begun testing Private Inference with a small group of business customers: queries run on isolated, confidential-computing hardware that OpenAI says it cannot read, alongside Zero Data Retention and Private Safety Processing. OpenAI's page labels it "Alpha" and "API Preview," names no attestation root or hardware stack, and gives no price or date. *(Sources: TechRadar, The Next Web, InfoWorld, Notebookcheck, Unite.AI, CTOL Digital, November 26–27)*

### Anthropic says Claude wrote the core of its Rust Messages API in a week, but verifying it took from April to August, and CPU per request fell 70%
An engineering post published November 25 and widely covered on Thursday describes how Anthropic replaced the Python Messages API, built in 2023 as "basically a request forwarder," after traffic grew roughly 1,800-fold. A March partial port of just the response parser saved only 8% to 10% of CPU because tokens crossed the Python–Rust boundary three times, so the team had Claude write a full Rust version of the core request path in about a week. In April that code passed about 98% of some 700 integration tests yet matched only about a quarter of production inputs; an audit found more than 100 weakened assertions in ported tests. The fix was shadowing: each request ran the model once and replayed the output to Rust, with both stacks checkpointed step by step and mismatches fingerprinted by step, piece and first differing byte without storing any text. The match rate fell to 6.5% in early May before climbing to 99.6% by August, when merged changes topped 3,000 per fortnight; public traffic moved in September with no downtime. Anthropic reports CPU per request down about 70% in one region, memory down more than 80% across production and median time to first token down about 20% on Claude Code requests. "Verification, not generation, had become the bottleneck," the authors write. *(Sources: Anthropic engineering blog, TechRepublic, DEV Community, November 25–26)*

### US chip curbs have stalled about $7.8 billion of Asian data-centre loans, Bloomberg reports, as Huawei teases an HBM design with twice HBM4's data pins
Bloomberg reports that lenders have paused or slowed four Southeast Asian data-centre and neocloud financings since the November 19 licence rule began covering who uses the compute, not just where chips ship. The largest is an upsized $6.6 billion loan sought by Bain Capital's Bridge Data Centres for Malaysian sites serving ByteDance, where some banks are running extra due diligence; one bank has opted out of a fully underwritten $372 million Digital Halo loan for a facility serving Alibaba, and lenders are reassessing a $480 million facility for Singapore neocloud Aolani. ByteDance-backed DayOne has put its US listing on hold and a BlackRock-led consortium is weighing a lower bid for an Asia-Pacific portfolio. Asia-Pacific data-centre loans exceed $49 billion, from almost nothing in 2023; operators serving Chinese tenants have 90 days to secure licences, so this may prove a pause rather than a cancellation. Separately, Bloomberg and TrendForce report that Huawei will present a paper at ISSCC on February 16 titled "A 64-Channel 4096-DQ Custom HBM with Memory Controllers and 5-Layer Wafer-on-Wafer Hybrid Bonding," doubling the 2,048 data pins of the JEDEC HBM4 standard that Samsung and SK hynix mass-produce. The listing gives no bandwidth, capacity or yield figures. On the same day Canada added C$700 million to its AI Compute Access Fund, taking it to C$1 billion and covering up to 80% of costs when SMEs buy from Canadian-owned providers. *(Sources: Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance and Dealroom, TechTimes, TrendForce, EE Times, BetaKit, November 26–27)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Nvidia open-sourced TimeTravelContainer, a rootless runtime that forks a running agent sandbox in seconds, cutting a chip-design agent task from 23.7 to 2.5 hours
A paper submitted to arXiv on November 24 (2611.32255) by Zizheng Guo, Yanqing Zhang, Nathaniel Pinckney, Cunxi Yu and Brucek Khailany describes TimeTravelContainer (TTC), a Go runtime that extends gVisor so an agent can clone an entire live container, including processes, memory, filesystem and network state, with copy-on-write. Clones share memory from the fork point, so only modified pages cost extra, and forks can land on remote nodes. The code is on GitHub under NVlabs with an Apache 2.0 licence and runs on Linux, macOS and Windows without kernel changes. The motivating case is agents exploring branching tool sessions in electronic design automation, where setup state is expensive: on two flows, MAGE with Cadence Innovus and EvoDRC with KLayout, the authors report cloning 84.1x faster than standard environment setup, memory savings of 31% to 72% and end-to-end speedups of 1.7x to 9.4x. On the BlackParrot design, a checkpoint-and-restore cycle took 185.7 seconds against 3.0 seconds for a TTC fork. Secondary write-ups give the 23.7-to-2.5-hour figure for one agent task. These are the authors' own measurements on their own workloads, and we could not open the paper or repository directly. *(Sources: arXiv, ArXivSignals, TodayForAI, MindPattern, November 24–27)*

### Tencent's WeChat team released WeMM-Ecom, five Apache 2.0 multimodal models for e-commerce, claiming its 35B-A3B beats the 770B Hy4 on a 28-benchmark suite
WeChat Vision published the WeMM-Ecom family on Hugging Face and GitHub this week, with four dense models at 0.8B, 2B, 4B and 9B parameters and a 35B mixture-of-experts model with 3B active parameters and a 262,144-token context, all under Apache 2.0 and shipped in BF16, FP8, GPTQ and GGUF. An accompanying paper, "WeMM-Ecom: Advancing Multimodal Content Understanding for WeChat E-Commerce," is on the Hugging Face papers page as 2611.30662. The models are tuned for product understanding, listing moderation and shop assistance; on Tencent's own 28-benchmark e-commerce suite the 35B-A3B model averages 74.35, ahead of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next at 73.15, Tencent's 770B Hy4 at 72.13 and Qwen3.5-397B-A17B at 72.01, and the card claims a 3.9-point edge over Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. One Chinese news flash says the models already serve hundreds of WeChat commerce workflows at more than a billion calls a day, a figure we found in no Tencent primary source. The release follows Hy4 Flash's open weights on November 18 and the full Hy4 weights last week, and one tracker noted the 0.8B checkpoint appeared unannounced before the rest. The benchmark is Tencent's and has not been reproduced independently. *(Sources: Hugging Face, GitHub, KuCoin News, Lookonchain, OrcaRouter, November 25–27)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Character.AI named the fund behind its $10 million bid for 21 million users' chats and the judge approved it as lead bidder, as 42 attorneys general try to stop Monday's auction
The bankrupt chatbot company dropped its request to keep its stalking-horse bidder's backer secret and in a November 24 filing identified Octave Fund I LLC as the money behind Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC, a Delaware shell formed on November 13, the day before Character.AI picked it. The Delaware judge then approved the designation: $10 million cash, a $1 million deposit and a $350,000 break-up fee for the app, models, accounts and chat logs of more than 21 million US accounts, about 2.1 million of them registered to minors, with IP addresses and risk scores attached. A coalition of 41 states and DC, announced by California's Rob Bonta and Connecticut's William Tong, has asked the court to reject the terms and the bidder, pointing to the privacy policy's promise that "we do not sell personal information for monetary consideration" and to chats with "therapist" and "physician" characters about self-harm and eating disorders; at minimum, they say, children's chats and sensitive data must be excluded. The court has appointed consumer privacy ombudsman Lucy Thomson at the states' urging. The auction is set for December 1 with a sale hearing on December 3 and 4, and the court has not ruled on the objection. The buyer's ultimate owners remain undisclosed. *(Sources: Engadget, PPC Land, Notebookcheck, Newsweek, The Next Web, November 25–27)*

### Thomson Reuters will build its next in-house model on Google's Gemma and Z.ai's GLM, after spending $40 million on a Qwen-based model that handles under 5% of its tokens
Business Insider reports that Thomson 1.1, due to be running by the end of 2026, will replace the Qwen3.6-based Thomson 1.0 that launched on August 24 for CoCounsel's tabular analysis feature. CTO Joel Hron said GLM was chosen for its strength at computer-use tasks that agents need and Gemma for its small size and efficiency; the mix gives the company a US open-weight base alongside a Chinese one. Hron put spending on in-house models at about $40 million across chips, data and salaries, and said they still account for less than 5% of the tokens the company consumes, a share it expects to grow. The in-house models are aimed at mid-tier legal and tax work, with OpenAI and Anthropic retained for the hardest tasks, and self-hosting is meant to cut cost and keep control of Westlaw and Practical Law content. The story is a useful data point on how a large enterprise is splitting workloads between frontier APIs and fine-tuned open models, though every figure is company-reported. *(Sources: Business Insider via Dataconomy and FourWeekMBA, Digital Today, CTOL Digital, November 26–27)*

### Microsoft named Salt Typhoon, two North Korean groups and three others as ARTEX users, with one agent going from GitHub secrets to Azure Key Vault data in five minutes
A day after the UK's NCSC tied a "marked rise" in agentic intrusions to ARTEX, Microsoft Threat Intelligence published a 30-minute defender's guide on November 25 naming six groups abusing the framework: China's Salt Typhoon and Crystal Typhoon, North Korea's Onyx Sleet and Pearl Sleet, China-aligned Storm-0940 and Cambodia-based, financially motivated Storm-3358. ARTEX, released on GitHub in July by the user Autumn-27 with a Chinese-language interface, chains a goals model, planner, worker agents and reporter over off-the-shelf pentest tools and can swap in almost any LLM, including abliterated models; the original source was pulled in October but forks persist, and ThreatBook claimed ownership this month and now sells "ARTEX Pro." Microsoft says it "does not introduce new attack techniques" and by default does not hide itself, yet can reach persistent privileged access "in a matter of minutes": Storm-0940 intrusions hit privilege in under three hours, one campaign produced 151,000 failed sign-ins at 2,900 a minute, agents added credentials to apps and one app granted itself Global Administrator. The most durable detection signal is behavioural, bursts of varied requests at machine speed, since the default `artex-enrich/*` user agent is trivially changed. The post includes hunting queries and says honeytokens and tarpits cut attack traffic in Microsoft's tests. *(Sources: Microsoft Security Blog, The Register, Help Net Security, The Next Web, November 25–27)*

## Key Themes

**Relief, not recovery.** Friday's bounce in Seoul, Tokyo and US premarket trading reversed only a fraction of November's losses, and the drivers were tariff exemptions and oversold positioning rather than any change in the AI fundamentals that have spooked investors. The Nasdaq 100 remains on course for its worst month in almost four years, Anthropic is barely back at its IPO price, and the first live reaction to Nvidia's Southeast Asia shipment halt came in a half-day session.

**The export-control shock is moving down the capital stack.** Yesterday it was servers; today it is debt. Bloomberg's account of $7.8 billion in stalled Malaysian and Singaporean loans shows that making data centres responsible for who rents their compute is freezing financing, not just shipments, while Huawei's ISSCC paper and Canada's sovereign-compute subsidy are two different answers to the same question of how to build capacity outside the US supply chain.

**Trust is being priced.** OpenAI's billing correction, which quietly cuts what a subscription buys, arrived the same week it began testing Private Inference, a product whose entire value is that customers can verify what OpenAI cannot see, but which currently names no attestation root. Character.AI's bankruptcy is the negative case: a privacy promise that turned out to be worth less than a $10 million stalking-horse bid.

**Verification is the bottleneck, for builders and defenders alike.** Anthropic's Rust rewrite took a week to generate and four months to prove against live traffic; Nvidia's TimeTravelContainer exists because agents need cheap ways to branch and check state; Thomson Reuters routes only its mid-tier work to models it trained itself. On the other side, Microsoft's ARTEX guide concedes the tool is nothing new technically and that the only durable signal is machine-speed behaviour, which is exactly what makes agentic attacks hard to verify as attacks at all.
