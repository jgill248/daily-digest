## 1. Frontier Model News

### OpenAI will buy Pierre Computer Company and fold its Git-based Code Storage into Codex, in a move Axios frames as a direct challenge to GitHub
OpenAI announced on November 4 that it has agreed to acquire Pierre Computer Company, Inc., a San Francisco developer-tools startup, for undisclosed terms. Axios's Madison Mills carried the news first as an exclusive; OpenAI's own newsroom item and coverage from SiliconANGLE, DevOps.com and Yahoo Finance corroborate it. Pierre's main product, Code Storage, is described in the company's own words as "a distributed storage system built on Git, with API ergonomics designed for machines," and its customers include Lovable, Bolt, Amp, Poke, Anything and Parahelp. OpenAI says it will integrate Code Storage directly into Codex, which it is positioning as a full development platform rather than an agent that lives inside someone else's tooling. Codex already depends on two of Pierre's open-source libraries, Trees and Diffs, for file navigation and rendering code changes; OpenAI says those will stay open source and that existing Code Storage customers can keep using the service. The deal needs customary regulatory approvals and the companies operate independently until it closes. Pierre raised a $22 million Series A led by CRV and 01A in November 2025, per its own announcement; one secondary outlet names the founders as Jacob Thornton and Ian Ownbey, which could not be confirmed elsewhere. Axios tied the purchase to OpenAI's earlier acquisition of the coding-agent startup Ona and to Meta's same-day Conductor deal, covered below. *(Sources: Axios, OpenAI newsroom, SiliconANGLE, DevOps.com, Yahoo Finance, November 4)*

### OpenAI ships Images 2.6 with GIF output, reusable reference images and two API models, Flare and Sunburst, that it says generate 50% faster
OpenAI released Images 2.6 on November 4 across ChatGPT, Codex and the API, per its "Introducing Images 2.6" post and the OpenAI Developers account on X, with MarkTechPost, TestingCatalog and Digit corroborating the details. In ChatGPT and Codex, users can now generate animated GIFs and store reference images to reuse across generations; the GIF feature is not in the API. The API gains two models, GPT-Image-2.6 Flare and GPT-Image-2.6 Sunburst, which OpenAI says produce images about 50% faster than their predecessor. Flare is pitched at rapid iteration and can return a low-quality image in roughly six seconds; Sunburst, at medium or high quality, is said to keep details more consistent from one edit to the next. ChatGPT's image markup, which lets a user circle the exact region to change, is now exposed to developers, and secondary coverage reports SVG export as an API-only option plus a new complexity parameter that trades latency for detail. Pricing is token-based rather than per image: several outlets list $5 per million text input tokens, $8 per million image input tokens and $30 per million image output tokens, with no surcharge for markup, GIF or SVG output, though one outlet cites lower figures it attributes to batch rates and OpenAI's pricing page could not be checked directly. The same day, OpenAI's newsroom listed Daybreak Red as available in AWS GovCloud (US-East). *(Sources: OpenAI, OpenAI Developers on X, MarkTechPost, TestingCatalog, Digit, November 4)*

### Meta buys Conductor, the app for running several coding agents in parallel, and shuts its cloud service on November 20
Axios reported on November 4, again as an exclusive, that Meta has acquired Conductor, a startup whose Mac app lets an engineer run multiple coding agents at once, each in an isolated workspace, then review the results, open pull requests and merge. Conductor does not build agents itself; it orchestrates Claude Code, Codex and Cursor. CEO Charlie Holtz confirmed the deal on X, writing "Conductor is joining Meta!" and that the team has "big, crazy plans for the next interface," and TechCrunch, PYMNTS and Techzine corroborate. Holtz and CTO Jackson de Campos join Meta Superintelligence Labs, where Axios says they will work with Nat Friedman and Alexandr Wang; Holtz said he is "convinced MSL has the highest talent density of all the labs." Terms were not disclosed. The product itself is winding down: Conductor's cloud service, launched only this summer, becomes read-only on November 13 and closes on November 20, after which cloud data is deleted, while the local app's core features keep working into early 2027 before support ends. Holtz promised something new "early next year," and one outlet says it will be aimed at customers in early 2027. TechCrunch notes that Meta already has its own coding agent, Muse Code, and no outlet explains how the two fit together. Alongside the Pierre deal above, it makes two developer-tool acquisitions by frontier labs in one day. *(Sources: Axios, TechCrunch, PYMNTS, Techzine, November 4)*

### Reuters reports about 600,000 Nvidia H200 chips are stuck in Hong Kong because Beijing will not approve bulk imports
In an exclusive published November 5, Reuters reports that roughly 600,000 Nvidia H200 accelerators are sitting in Hong Kong storage awaiting entry to mainland China, because Beijing has not signed off on large-scale imports despite demand from the country's internet companies. The account rests on unnamed sources: two people for the stockpile, three for the detail that small volumes have crossed through intermediaries and indirect routes. Several large Chinese internet firms have each obtained about 10,000 H200s in recent months, and one version of the report says total approvals could end up below 200,000 chips, with Beijing pushing companies to run inference on domestic processors. The squeeze shows in prices: an eight-card H200 server was being offered on the mainland for more than 6 million yuan, about $900,000, by late October, roughly three times a year earlier. Investing.com, Devdiscourse and Global Banking & Finance carry the Reuters text; no Chinese authority or Nvidia comment appears in any version, and the 600,000 figure has no independent confirmation. The report follows the FT's account this week, carried in earlier digests, of Nvidia capping Chinese business at cloud partners and of Oracle cancelling a 100,000-chip lease to Tencent, and an earlier FT report that regulators had cleared ByteDance and Tencent for up to 100,000 H200s each. *(Sources: Reuters, Investing.com, Devdiscourse, Global Banking & Finance, November 5)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Ricursive Intelligence releases Ripple-65B Preview, a dense open-weight coding model it says beats models ten times its size on Terminal-Bench 2.1
Ricursive Intelligence, the chip-design startup founded by AlphaChip creators Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini that raised $300 million at a $4 billion valuation in January, released Ripple-65B Preview on November 4. It is a 65-billion-parameter dense model pretrained from scratch on 12 trillion tokens, published in three checkpoints, an instruction-tuned BF16 build, an NVFP4 build and the base model, under Apache 2.0 on Hugging Face, with hosted access through Together AI, GMI Cloud and OpenRouter. The company reports 80.52 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, which it says tops US open-weight models including Reflection's Beam-501B at 80.1 and Meta's Muse Spark Open-816B at 76.4, plus 79.1 on SWE-bench Pro v2.0 Hard, and says the result took 1.4 million RL rollouts, about 70 times fewer than Beam, on a $32 million compute budget. Mirhoseini says it runs locally on a MacBook Pro or DGX Spark. All of the numbers are self-reported and no independent lab has reproduced them. Community reaction, summarised by MarkTechPost, Crypto Briefing and DEV Community posts, has been pointed: the "US open-weight" framing excludes DeepSeek's V4 Flash, a 284B mixture-of-experts model that scores 2.2 points higher on the same benchmark, Ripple trails Muse Spark on DeepSWE at 44.3 versus 60.0, and Ricursive's own tables show it behind larger models on knowledge and science. The weights claim a 1 million-token context but the first hosted API stops at 16,384 tokens, and one reviewer found the Hugging Face card still listing no inference provider. *(Sources: Ricursive Intelligence on X, MarkTechPost, Crypto Briefing, DEV Community, OfficeChai, November 4)*

### GitHub Copilot arrives inside Xcode's built-in coding assistant in public preview, a day after it became a selectable agent in JetBrains Air
GitHub's changelog entry dated November 4 says Copilot can now be used directly in Xcode's native coding assistant, in public preview starting with the Xcode 27.3 beta. It requires a Mac running macOS Tahoe 26.6 or later, setup happens under Xcode's Intelligence settings, and all Copilot plans are supported. The existing GitHub Copilot for Xcode extension, which has been in public preview since October 2024 and gained agent mode and MCP support earlier, remains available as a separate option, so Apple developers can choose between Apple's assistant surface with Copilot models behind it or GitHub's own extension. A DEV Community post that tracks the changelog notes this is a preview inside a beta IDE; 9to5Mac expects stable Xcode 27.3 in December, which is not an Apple or GitHub date. The same post reports that on November 3 GitHub made Copilot a selectable agent in JetBrains Air, JetBrains' agent-first environment, which could not be confirmed against a primary JetBrains announcement. Taken with the Xcode change, Copilot has in two days become available inside both major IDE vendors' native agent surfaces rather than only through GitHub's own plugins, and the same week's changelog adds a Copilot usage API covering bring-your-own-key and enterprise custom models. *(Sources: GitHub Changelog, DEV Community, Daily.dev, November 4)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Uber says AI agents booked four rides and about 500 Eats orders during its busiest week, against 320 million trips
On Uber's third-quarter earnings call on November 4, the company gave its first numbers for bookings that arrive through third-party AI agents, and they are tiny: during Halloween week, its busiest week in October, agents placed four rides and roughly 500 Uber Eats orders while the platform handled about 320 million trips and deliveries. TechCrunch, The Verge and Unite.ai carry the figures; The Verge describes the four rides as the quarter's total while TechCrunch ties them to the week, so the scope is slightly unclear. CEO Dara Khosrowshahi called it "very, very early and very limited traction," noting that booking a ride in the app takes about 50 seconds and reordering a favourite meal about 90, which leaves agents little time to save. He still called agents "another opportunity to reach our platform" and a likely net positive for demand, said there are about eight major assistants today and expected more than ten. The disclosure lands amid claims that assistants will disintermediate consumer apps, and a week after Canada's big banks blocked personal agents from online banking. The quarter itself was strong: gross bookings rose 20% to $59.8 billion, trips 16% to 4.1 billion, Uber One reached 55 million members, and Khosrowshahi said about 700 fully driverless vehicles should be on Uber by year end and more than 2,000 in 2027. *(Sources: TechCrunch, The Verge, Unite.ai, Uber Q3 earnings release, November 4)*

### Voters backed restrictions in all but one of 41 local data-center ballot measures across ten states, with Ohio alone deciding 21
Tuesday's midterm elections carried 41 local measures in ten states on data-center development, tracked by Ballotpedia, and by Wednesday the results were nearly unanimous: voters either approved restrictions or rejected pro-development proposals in every contest with results except Harwood, North Dakota, where residents voted about three to one against adding review requirements for industrial projects. CNBC, Fast Company, Scientific American, 404 Media and The Hill report the pattern; Fast Company counts 38 of 41 measures as banning or pausing development and says support reached 70% or more in 28 of the 38 with reported results. Ohio, home to more than 200 data centers, accounted for 21 of the measures. Margins were lopsided: Wells, Maine passed a 180-day moratorium with more than 80%, and El Dorado, Kansas approved a ten-year ban on high-impact data centers and some battery storage 71% to 29%. 404 Media counts six cities voting to ban data centers outright and 12 capping the megawatts a building may draw. Axios adds that Democrats won governorships in Ohio, Georgia and Arizona, the three states after Texas with the most new capacity, and that Georgia's Public Service Commission race turned on a recently approved expansion for Georgia Power. Counts differ across outlets, from 40 to "more than 40 races in 13 states," and some tallies were still preliminary. *(Sources: CNBC, Fast Company, Scientific American, 404 Media, The Hill, Axios, Ballotpedia, November 4)*

### The OCC gives bank examiners their first AI framework, grading uses by consequence and autonomy with a fully autonomous payment agent at the top tier
The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency published Bulletin 2026-54 on November 4, a 22-page "Supervisory Framework on Bank Use of Super Intelligence," the term the administration now applies to AI and agents. It is addressed to examiners and the OCC states that it "does not establish supervisory expectations for banks"; it is a resource, not a rule, and banks are not told to use it to assess themselves. Examiners are instructed not to treat AI as a separate risk category or require a separate AI risk appetite, but to work within existing model-risk, governance, technology, compliance and operational-risk practice, weighing each use by its materiality, the consequences of failure, how much decision-making authority is transferred and how much human oversight remains. Coverage from CDO Magazine, CU Today and Yahoo Finance says the document sorts uses into four illustrative tiers, with a fully autonomous customer-facing payment agent in the top tier, and discusses transaction limits, independent testing, emergency shutdown mechanisms and procedures for compensating customers harmed by AI failures; the tier structure appears in secondary coverage only and the PDF could not be opened. The framework also reaches external agents that connect through bank channels, which places it squarely on the question this digest has tracked for weeks as Wells Fargo, JPMorgan, Citi, Schwab and Canada's banks blocked or restricted customer-directed agents. Model-risk principles in Bulletin 2026-13 still apply to non-generative, non-agentic models, and the OCC says it will update the framework regularly. *(Sources: OCC Bulletin 2026-54, CDO Magazine, CU Today, Yahoo Finance, PYMNTS, November 4)*

## Key Themes

**The labs are buying the plumbing around the agent.** OpenAI wants Pierre's Git-based storage so Codex owns where code lives, and Meta wants Conductor's team so it owns how engineers supervise several agents at once. GitHub's answer the same day was distribution, putting Copilot inside Apple's and JetBrains' native assistants. The agent itself is becoming the least contested layer.

**Agent demand is real in tooling and nearly invisible in consumer transactions.** Uber counted four agent-booked rides in 320 million trips in the same week that Conductor and Pierre sold for their agent infrastructure. The OCC's framework assumes autonomous payment agents are coming and ranks them as the highest-consequence use, while banks keep blocking them at the door.

**Compute access is being rationed by politics on both sides of the Pacific.** Six hundred thousand H200s wait in Hong Kong for a Beijing approval that may cover fewer than a third of them, a day after Washington pushed Oracle out of a Tencent lease. At home, voters in 40 of 41 contests said no to the buildings those chips need, while Goldman and Apollo started syndicating SpaceX's $40 billion GPU lease in $500 million slices.

**Benchmark claims need their qualifiers read.** Ripple-65B leads "US open-weight models" on one benchmark version, trails DeepSeek on the same test and Muse Spark on another, and ships with a 16K-token API behind a 1M-token claim. Images 2.6's "50% faster" and Uber's "four rides" are likewise company numbers with no outside check.
