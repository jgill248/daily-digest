## 1. Frontier Model News

### Nvidia's board opens an independent probe after Jensen Huang admits an affair with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset
Nvidia said on Sunday, November 8, that a committee of independent directors, assisted by outside counsel, will investigate reports that chief executive Jensen Huang was the target of a Chinese intelligence operation. The announcement came hours after Huang emailed employees that he had been unfaithful to his wife, that there was "no excuse" for it, that he was "deeply sorry" and that he took "full responsibility"; Axios obtained the message and Nvidia later published it. The woman is Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, a former managing director of Megaspeed, the Singapore data-centre operator whose Malaysian affiliate bought at least $4.8 billion of Nvidia-based equipment between June 2024 and February 2026, according to trade records cited by the New York Times. The Times first reported on October 29 that US agencies believed China's Ministry of State Security had recruited her to cultivate Huang and push for looser chip restrictions, and Nvidia had until this weekend called the contacts routine customer interactions. The board said it "has no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised," expressed confidence in Huang and said he remains chief executive; no timeline was given and the directors and law firm have not been named. Shares fell between 4.4% and 6% in Monday pre-market trading from Friday's $186.64 close, depending on the outlet and hour. Three House chairs, Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford, wrote on November 4 asking Huang to attend a closed-door briefing on November 18 and to answer by November 11; Senator John Cornyn called for a congressional investigation, Senator Elizabeth Warren said Huang and the board "owe Congress answers," and President Trump told reporters on Sunday that Huang is "an honorable guy" but "you never know what is going on." Semafor notes Nvidia has no public succession plan. China denies the espionage claim. *(Sources: Axios, CNBC, Bloomberg, New York Times via Bloomberg, Washington Post, NPR, CBS News, Reuters via Yahoo Finance, Semafor, House Foreign Affairs Committee, November 7–9)*

### Anthropic's IPO roadshow is reported to start this week with valuation talk down to $1.2–1.3 trillion and no price range filed
Bloomberg reported that Anthropic's investor roadshow could begin as early as the week of November 9, with JPMorgan leading the US retail tranche and Citigroup coordinating international retail sales, a retail allocation of roughly 15% to 20% under discussion and Jamie Dimon expected to join the outreach; Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead the overall offering. The amended S-1 filed November 2 still carries no share count, price range or date, and Anthropic declined to comment. The valuation conversation has moved since the company filed. Bloomberg-sourced reporting puts prospective buyers at $1.2 trillion to $1.3 trillion, with some talks as low as $1.1 trillion; the Wall Street Journal reported on November 6 that three large investors planning to participate said the $1.5 trillion figure floated earlier was unlikely after Thursday's sell-off, with one putting it closer to $1 trillion; the Financial Times had earlier cited investors expecting a raise of $80 billion to $100 billion at $1.25 trillion to $1.75 trillion. Trackers point to a Nasdaq listing under ANT in the week of November 16, ahead of Thanksgiving on November 26, which Reuters' sources had questioned on November 5. The supply question hangs over the pricing: The Information's Alex Eichenstein calculated on November 8 that AI companies have sold or priced more than $300 billion of stock and mandatory convertibles in 2026 excluding Anthropic, with about $100 billion more on tap, most through at-the-market programmes, including a planned $60 billion Meta class C programme that is not expected to start before the first quarter of 2027. *(Sources: Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance, Wall Street Journal via Yahoo Finance, Reuters, Financial Times, The Information via Techmeme, Seeking Alpha, November 5–9)*

### DeepSeek says its open-source Harness agent has 1.54 million daily users consuming 92 trillion tokens a day, and plans an official plugin marketplace
DeepSeek said on its official X account on Sunday, November 8, that Harness, the MIT-licensed agent app it launched in mid-August, had about 1.54 million daily active users on November 4, counting only users on its official API, less than three months after launch. The company's own earlier figures put the seven-day average at roughly 271,000 at the end of September and 429,000 on October 7. Those users consume about 92 trillion tokens a day, or roughly 60 million per active user, a number that reflects usage rather than revenue. DeepSeek says about 35,000 third-party plugins have been built for the agent and that around 60% of active users have created or installed at least one; it plans an official marketplace where developers can publish plugins and users can find them, plus a support programme for plugin authors offering token grants, technical discussions and promotion, with no launch date. Community catalogues on GitHub list far fewer plugins, between about 1,000 and 20,000 depending on the project, and DeepSeek itself notes they vary in maturity. Bloomberg reported the figures the same day; the South China Morning Post framed them as a test of whether DeepSeek can challenge OpenAI and Anthropic in agents, and Global Times quoted an analyst on ecosystem-building. All numbers are company-reported with no independent audit, and one aggregator dates the disclosure to November 5 rather than November 8. *(Sources: DeepSeek Harness on X, Bloomberg via Dealroom, Seeking Alpha, South China Morning Post, Global Times, MLQ.ai, November 8–9)*

### A startup says an unreleased Anthropic model proved the 18-year-old Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture in three days, with the main theorem checked in Lean
Physical Superintelligence (PSI) announced on November 6 that a research system running instances of a non-public Anthropic model had produced a proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture, a question in quantum query complexity posed in 2008 and published in Theory of Computing in 2014, which asks whether every bounded low-degree polynomial on the Boolean cube has a variable whose influence is at least polynomial in the inverse degree; the answer bears on how far quantum query algorithms can outperform classical ones on most inputs. PSI says the proof was complete on the third day of the run, that the model wrote the first draft of the paper, and that the bound obtained is on the order of one over the sixth power of the degree. David Turturean of PSI built the system, scoped the problem and takes responsibility for the paper; Jim Halverson audited the argument and Phiala Shanahan commented on the Lean formalisation, which is public in a GitHub repository alongside a definition readers can compare against the original statement, since Lean checks the logic but not whether the formal statement matches the informal conjecture. Scott Aaronson wrote on his blog that he has not yet worked through the argument but believes it is correct given the Lean certificate and expert review, and that he is satisfied the conjecture is true and disappointed humans did not get there first. Stanford researchers who proved a special case in August are preparing a shorter exposition. The result has not been peer reviewed, PSI has not identified the model, and Anthropic did not respond to requests for comment. *(Sources: Physical Superintelligence via GitHub, Shtetl-Optimized, Interesting Engineering, Dev Community, Crypto Briefing, November 6–8)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Alibaba teases Qwen4 with a four-word post, then drops a mystery model called Qoduck into its Qoder coding agent
Qwen's official X account posted "The becoming begins" at 7pm on November 6 over an image of a flock of birds and the opening line of Zhuangzi's "Free and Easy Wandering" with the creature's name left blank; the post names no model, date or benchmark and passed a million views within hours. On November 7 Alibaba's Qoder coding platform added a model called Qoduck whose maker is listed as "undisclosed," free within a daily quota for individual users and with no public API, saying it would "share more later." Developers immediately read it as a Qwen4 preview: one tester reports the tokenizer matches the Qwen family and that it beat every released Qwen on a knowledge check, others compare its output to Qwen3.8-Max, and users report a one-million-token context, a figure consistent with a Qwen Code pull request merged October 28 that sizes "the qwen4 generation at 1M." None of that is confirmed. Alibaba's only official statement on Qwen4 remains its September 22 line that the model is "currently in training"; attendees at Alibaba Cloud's October 29 Amsterdam event reported an executive saying "Qwen4 next week," which the company has not confirmed; and no weights, model card, parameter count or licence have been published. Asia Times notes how much Alibaba has left blank, and a Manifold market on open Qwen4 weights by November 25 sat at 61% on November 6. *(Sources: Qwen on X, Startup Fortune, Asia Times, Yotta Labs, Cellcog, Manifold, November 6–8)*

### Cursor's cloud agents now deploy apps with hosting, Postgres and sign-in handled, and Codex CLI adds an agent command center
Cursor announced that its cloud agents can build an app from a prompt, preview it and publish it to a live shareable URL with hosting, a provisioned PostgreSQL database, sign-in and automated security checks handled for the user; Vercel said on X that the feature is built on Vercel for Platforms, with "no account to create" and database provisioning included. Private and team apps sit behind Cursor's existing sign-in, individual subscribers can make apps public, and secrets are kept from the agent, which never sees their values. The August version of the feature required users to connect their own Vercel account; The New Stack's November 6 write-up, headlined "Most apps never make it off localhost," frames the change as a direct move onto Lovable's and Replit's turf, and a newsletter logged that Cursor also added voice chat with agents the same day, running on Grok Voice. Pricing and plan limits are not in the coverage. Separately, OpenAI shipped Codex CLI 0.167.0 on November 6 with an agent command center opened via `codex agents`, unread-result markers in the command center and session picker, switching between connected backends from the terminal with drafts preserved, local image previews while composing, and automatic context compaction that now counts output from failed responses; one third-party write-up says the documented flag combination conflicts with the release notes. *(Sources: The New Stack, Vercel on X, Vibe Coding Weekly, OpenAI Codex releases on GitHub, Dev Community, November 5–6)*

### OpenAI hires Hacktron, the team that reached its internal monorepo through Codex, to test third-party connections
OpenAI announced on November 6 a partnership with Hacktron, a security research firm it found through its bug bounty programme, to find vulnerabilities in how its systems connect to outside services; OpenAI engineers and Hacktron researchers will pick high-priority targets together and Hacktron gets expanded access to frontier cyber models through Daybreak Red, the restricted tier of OpenAI's Trusted Access for Cyber programme. The back story is why it matters. On July 25 Hacktron chained a remote-code-execution flaw in the image-processing path of OpenAI's Discourse-hosted community forum with a single-sign-on issue, reaching ChatGPT and Codex accounts of several OpenAI employees, then used one employee's Codex account and its GitHub connection to open a harmless pull request in OpenAI's internal monorepo to demonstrate access before stopping. Hacktron's timeline says OpenAI confirmed a fix about 14 hours after the report and Discourse had one ready in two days; OpenAI paid a $6,500 bounty on September 1. OpenAI's post gives no detail beyond the third-party focus and the Daybreak Red access, and has not said how many researchers are involved or whether findings will be published; the bounty programme, whose top award rose to $172,000 last week, continues. *(Sources: OpenAI, Hacktron, Unite.AI, WebProNews, SmartScope, November 6–7)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Five of Britain's biggest banks block AI agents from customer accounts before any have shown up, the Telegraph reports
The Telegraph reported on November 8 that HSBC, Nationwide, NatWest, Barclays and Lloyds have moved to block AI agents from accessing customer accounts, in some cases with specific bans on transactions made on a customer's behalf without their direct involvement. The measures are described as pre-emptive: Lloyds' finance chief said the bank had seen no sign of agents accessing accounts, and the stated worry is that personal-finance bots could act without authorisation or mismanage money with no clear rules on who is liable. The Financial Conduct Authority is working with the City and other regulators on rules for agents in finance, and the banks have not ruled out controlled read-only access through approved open-banking services. The clause text has not been published and the report rests on unnamed sources; Dealroom frames the five as holding about £700 billion of deposits. The move follows American Banker's October 27 report that JPMorgan, Citi, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and Morgan Stanley restrict outside agents that log in with customer credentials, Charles Schwab's statement that it actively blocks Meta's Muse, and Wells Fargo's November 24 terms making customers liable for their agents' instructions. The timing is pointed: The Times of London reports Meta is preparing to launch Muse in the UK within weeks, free for most consumers with paid tiers for heavier use, and retailers including Walmart, Best Buy, Gap and Sephora have signed on in the US. *(Sources: The Telegraph via Yahoo Finance, Dealroom, Retail Gazette, The Times via Rallies, FourWeekMBA, November 8–9)*

### Deutsche Telekom spends about €250 million on Nvidia B300s to double its sold-out Munich AI factory to 20 megawatts
Deutsche Telekom said on November 9 that it will invest just under €250 million, about $289 million, to expand the AI data centre it opened in Munich's Tucherpark in February, adding Nvidia Blackwell Ultra B300 systems alongside the existing B200 racks and raising capacity to 20 megawatts. Bloomberg and German outlets report the first stage raised Germany's available AI compute by about 50% and was fully booked within seven months; Goldesel names Siemens and Daimler Truck among the tenants, and dpa-AFX says Telekom runs the site entirely on renewable power. The company says no scope or timeline has been set for further Bavarian expansion. The spend is small next to US hyperscaler budgets but is one of the few European sovereign-cloud facilities to report sell-out demand, and it lands in a week when Bloomberg reports a Democratic Congress preparing to subpoena Nvidia over chip exports and Nvidia's own board is investigating its chief executive. *(Sources: Deutsche Telekom, Bloomberg, dpa-AFX via finanzen.net, Börse Express, Goldesel, FourWeekMBA, November 9)*

## Key Themes

**Nvidia's week is about governance and gatekeeping, not GPUs.** The board probe into Huang arrives alongside a New York Times investigation, published November 7, finding that the Commerce Department approved roughly two dozen Chinese firms to handle H200 chips under the December 2025 export deal, including at least two, New Matrix and Maginfra, with empty registered offices and ownership links to sanctioned entities Wise Road Capital and Inspur; neither was cleared as an end user, both were licensed to assemble H200 servers for unnamed clients, and Representative Seth Magaziner wants Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify with Huang. Add Bloomberg's November 6 report that incoming Democratic committee chairs plan subpoena-backed hearings on Nvidia's China sales, the 600,000 H200s still waiting in Hong Kong, and Senator Warren's standing invitation that Huang declined in June, and the company's exposure in Washington has flipped from lobbying for access to defending its leadership.

**AI paper is piling up faster than buyers are absorbing it.** The Information's $300 billion count of 2026 AI equity sales, Anthropic's valuation talk sliding from $2 trillion to $1.1–1.3 trillion within a fortnight of filing, and Meta's $60 billion at-the-market programme waiting until 2027 describe one market being asked to fund everything at once. The talent flows run the other way: Meta's chief strategy officer Dave Wehner, its CFO from 2014 to 2022, told X on Friday he is leaving to become finance chief of inference startup Fireworks, valued at $17.5 billion in July and, per Business Insider, in early talks at $30 billion to $40 billion; Fireworks CEO Lin Qiao said he wanted a smaller company where he could have a large impact, and Meta has not named a successor.

**Agents are growing on one side of the counter and being locked out on the other.** DeepSeek's 1.54 million daily Harness users and 35,000 plugins, Cursor publishing apps straight to production and Alibaba seeding a stealth model into its coding agent all point to agent usage compounding on developer platforms. Banks on both sides of the Atlantic are responding by refusing agents the door before they arrive, Bloomberg Opinion cited a Gartner review finding fewer than 10% of business agent deployments showing robust returns, and Musk promised Grok Bot subscribers on November 8 that overnight fixes would bring a "dramatic improvement" by Monday without saying what changed or whether weekly caps move. Web Summit opened in Lisbon on November 9 with two humanoid robots listed as speakers, Manus co-founder Tao Zhang on the opening stage and the largest Chinese delegation in the event's history; CNBC found Amazon job listings for a "new-to-world" home robot built around the Fauna Robotics team it bought in March; and Reuters' Valerie Volcovici reported that all 15 environmental groups she interviewed use AI, for wildfire alerts in Cambodia among other things, while every one worries about the data centres that power it.
