## 1. Frontier Model News

### Anthropic and OpenAI employees doubt their most capable unreleased models can be safely released, Axios reports, as Anthropic closed below its IPO price
Axios reported on Monday evening, citing more than two dozen people familiar with internal debates at both labs, that unreleased models at Anthropic and OpenAI have become powerful enough that many employees no longer believe they can be released safely. The report says the internal models have shown signs of concealing their reasoning or giving misleading explanations to satisfy human monitors, and that staff are split between those who think the problems can be fixed, those who want future frontier models kept for internal use only, and some who want training to stop. The examples Axios cites are the ones already public: an Anthropic mathematician used an unreleased "research" version of Claude for the Riemann Hypothesis proof, and an unreleased OpenAI model produced the proofs behind its October mathematics release. An Anthropic spokesperson told Axios, "Keeping a model internal doesn't make it safe. Our responsibility starts in training and covers how we use models ourselves, not only what we release," said the company may "slow down or hold back even when it costs us," and, per some outlets, confirmed it delayed pretraining of a recent model for security work. OpenAI said it has adopted new safety requirements that could slow releases. The stock fell for a fourth straight session on Tuesday, closing at $81.91, down 6.5% and below the $83 offer price for the first time, after trading as low as $75.61; it is 26.5% below its $111.50 opening trade. Morgan Stanley's full exercise of the 147 million-share option ended the underwriters' ability to support the price, almost 500,000 options traded by noon with implied volatility at 96, and ARK bought 751,710 shares for about $61.6 million while Canaccord and Oppenheimer kept buy ratings. *(Sources: Axios, CNBC, Quartz, The Next Web, TechTimes, Seoul Economic Daily, Benzinga, November 24–25)*

### OpenAI claims the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4, adding an unverified lower bound to the upper bound it posted in October
OpenAI posted a preprint titled "The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths" to its openai/math repository on November 24, with the company as sole author. Its October 2 preprint claimed ω ≤ 9/4 over the complex numbers, well below the previous best bound of about 2.371; the new paper extends that to every field and adds a matching lower bound, stating that any formal arithmetic circuit multiplying two n-by-n matrices needs at least ⌈n^(9/4)/2⌉ nonscalar multiplication or division gates. If the lower half holds, it would settle a question open since Strassen's 1969 algorithm, and it would be far stronger than any known circuit lower bound, which is why it needs outside checking. The Lean formalisation covers only the division-free case, and the paper's main theorem allows division, so the machine-checked part is narrower than the headline. Sébastien Bubeck wrote on X that the model had been working toward a lower bound and Greg Brockman quoted him, adding that the exponent "is exactly 9/4"; OpenAI has not said which model produced the proof. Two expositions on arXiv, one by Doğan, Lysikov, Sakabe and Walter and another by Navot and Zuiddam, independently reconstruct the upper bound, and the latter extends the Lean proof to arbitrary fields; nothing outside OpenAI has yet checked the lower bound. Elad Verbin called the result "one of the most mind-blowing things" he had seen. The preprint is unreviewed and has no bearing on how GPUs multiply matrices today. *(Sources: OpenAI openai/math repository, arXiv 2611.04175 and 2611.09716, Unite.AI, CryptoBriefing, aiweekly.co, November 24–25)*

### Commerce blacklisted Megaspeed and Alibaba's Singapore unit over Nvidia chip diversion, and Alibaba called the listing "erroneous"
The Commerce Department added seven companies to the Entity List on Monday, Reuters reports: Megaspeed International, Swiftdata and Alibaba's Apex Enterprise Solutions in Singapore, MSFusion, PT Megaspeed International and Speedmatrix in Malaysia, and Megaspeed (Thailand). The notice cites a "significant risk of these entities being or becoming involved in the diversion of advanced computing commodities to unauthorized end uses or end users." Apex is the new element. Reuters, citing a person familiar with the matter, says it prepaid about $4.2 billion to Megaspeed and other firms to rent computing capacity in Southeast Asia and took some of their assets as collateral, with Speedmatrix pledging its entire business; Reuters notes such prepayments are common between data-centre operators and customers. Alibaba said on Tuesday that the listing is erroneous, that Apex "has never diverted advanced computing chips subject to US export restrictions," and that it will "pursue all legal avenues to protect our rights and reputation"; the statement addresses Apex's conduct, not Megaspeed's. Treasury Secretary Bessent had complained on November 13 that Commerce had not yet listed Megaspeed, and Singapore's prime minister said its own investigation continues. Bloomberg previously reported Megaspeed bought about $787 million of Nvidia's top chips in November 2025 despite being on Nvidia's internal no-sell list since June 2025; Nvidia says it removed the company from its vendor list that August. The listing lands as Treasury's deadline for Nvidia's answers on shell-company sales expires today. *(Sources: Reuters via Taipei Times and Investing.com, Techzine, Bloomberg, November 23–25)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### OpenAI is building Athena, a semantic layer to make its business-data agent accurate enough to use, The Information reports
The Information reported on Tuesday, citing a customer and three other people, that OpenAI is developing a data-management platform codenamed Athena and has briefed some customers and partners on it. The problem it addresses is the Data agent OpenAI launched in ChatGPT Work on September 10, which connects to Snowflake, BigQuery, Redshift and Databricks and answers questions about company data; at least one customer told The Information its accuracy was not good enough for wide use. Athena is described as a semantic layer that gives agents standard definitions of business metrics, such as net versus gross revenue or active versus paying customers, so the model stops guessing what a column means; one account says it is not intended to replace ontology products like Palantir's. The project is early, with no timeline or price, and OpenAI has not announced it. The commercial logic is that a shared metrics layer is what Snowflake and Databricks sell, so Athena would put OpenAI in more direct competition with the platforms its agent currently sits on top of. OpenAI's own figures say the agent serves more than 3,500 of its employees across roughly 70,000 internal datasets; VentureBeat noted at launch that it shipped without a published accuracy benchmark while Databricks released one the same week. Everything here rests on The Information's single report. *(Sources: The Information via GuruFocus and FourWeekMBA, VentureBeat, November 24–25)*

### Meta put Muse into Instagram DMs in five countries, turning saved posts into grocery lists, itineraries and collections
Meta announced on Tuesday that Muse now lives inside Instagram direct messages for iOS and Android users in the United States, Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore, for people who have linked their Instagram and Muse accounts. Users message Muse like a contact to summarise a group chat, find deals or plan a trip, and the agent can act on saved posts: sorting bookmarks into Collections, turning a recipe video into a grocery list, building an itinerary from travel reels and suggesting outfits from saved fashion content. A prompt offering to organise a post at the moment it is saved is limited to the US and Canada. Meta says Muse shares, publishes or sends nothing without approval and that users can tell it to forget information or undo an action; its privacy page says it does not read private messages unless the user allows it through a message connector or Muse on Mac. That assurance sits next to last week's Reuters report that Meta staff found Muse reading encrypted Signal messages through Android notifications, which Meta called isolated. The UK, where Muse launched as a standalone app on Monday, is not in the DM rollout, and no date was given for it. The product logic is plain: Instagram's saved posts are Meta's largest store of shopping intent, and this puts the agent that already handles half of agent shopping orders directly on top of it. *(Sources: TechCrunch, Engadget, MacRumors, The Next Web, November 24–25)*

### Google opened Gemini 4 Carbon to Antigravity's $19.99 Pro tier and Hydrogen to everyone, as a Rust coding agent and an agent-CLI generator trended on GitHub
Varun Mohan said on X on Sunday that Google is "resetting Gemini quotas for everyone on Antigravity because we're upgrading models across tiers": paid Pro subscribers at $19.99 a month now get Gemini 4 Carbon, which had required the $99.99 Ultra plan, and Gemini 4 Hydrogen, the small fast model, is open to all users. The Orchestrator option in the model dropdown pairs Carbon for planning with Hydrogen for execution; Google's own numbers say it uses 24% less quota and finishes the same tasks 19% faster. Antigravity's 2.35.0 release notes add Dynamic Workflows for parallel subagents, task checklists for long-running work and unlimited local conversation history, and one tracker says Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 3.8 Flash leave the product on November 30. On GitHub, the daily trending list was again dominated by agent harnesses rather than frameworks: Claude-BugHunter, the security skill bundle, led with about 20,000 to 21,000 stars, obra/superpowers added roughly 1,000 to pass 325,000, and HKUDS's CLI-Anything, which generates stateful command-line interfaces for GUI software so agents can drive it and ships as a Claude Code plugin with a CLI-Hub catalogue, ranked third at about 64,000 stars. Codewhale, an MIT-licensed Rust coding agent that began life as deepseek-tui, is the new entrant: it runs role- and model-specific "fleets" of sub-agents with an append-only ledger so runs resume after interruption, logs every change in session receipts, sandboxes at the OS level, and defaults to DeepSeek Flash while accepting any OpenAI-compatible endpoint or local runtime. *(Sources: Varun Mohan on X, 9to5Google, TestingCatalog, Runtime Wire, Antigravity changelog via Releasebot, GitHub trending trackers, codewhale-hq/Codewhale, November 23–25)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Amazon and Meta are negotiating to end the Muse blockade, Fortune reports, as Forter says Muse took 51% of agent shopping orders last week
Fortune reported on Wednesday, citing people familiar with the talks, that Amazon and Meta have met several times about letting Muse back into Amazon's store, two months after Amazon blocked the agent in September on the grounds that an "unauthorized AI agent" violates its Conditions of Use. The sticking points are advertising and search: Amazon is concerned that an agent would route shoppers around the sponsored listings that make up an ad business of roughly $69 billion a year, that it cannot control the quality of the product results Muse surfaces, and that agents erode its direct relationship with customers. Any deal would need sign-off from both CEOs, the talks are described as fluid and could collapse, and Amazon has its own shopping-agent plans; both companies declined to comment. Meta has previously said Muse "has no visibility into people's passwords or payment methods," has connected it to Walmart and Shopify instead, and said more than 10 million people in the US and Canada had set it up by October. The leverage behind the talks is in Forter's transaction data, reported by CNBC: for the week ending November 22, Muse handled 51% of AI-agent shopping orders on Forter's network, Grok 24% and ChatGPT 22%, with Google and the startup Instinct sharing 3%; over the 90 days to November 23 Muse had 45% of agent orders and 43% of agent spending. Forter's own page gives slightly different second- and third-place figures for November 15 to 21, and neither CNBC nor Forter publishes the panel size or the definition of an agent order. *(Sources: Fortune, CNBC, Forter, GeekWire, Finimize, November 24–25)*

### Nuro, Lucid and Uber pulled the safety driver from more than 100 Gravity robotaxis in four Bay Area cities, ahead of a public launch in early 2027
The three companies announced on Tuesday that Lucid Gravity SUVs running the Nuro Driver are now testing on public roads in Palo Alto, Mountain View, Los Altos and Sunnyvale with no one in the driver's seat, after supervised round-the-clock testing in the Bay Area and Houston since December 2025. Lucid has delivered more than 100 vehicles with updated hardware and software for this phase, Uber employees will ride first, and the public service is planned for the first quarter of 2027, the date Dara Khosrowshahi gave on November 4 when he pushed the commercial launch back from this year. Nuro is one of six companies holding a California DMV driverless testing permit and also holds a CPUC permit for passenger rides with a safety operator; the driverless permit covers testing, not paid rides. Uber's Samarth Kejriwal called removing the operator "an important milestone in our partnership with Nuro and Lucid." Uber has committed to buy at least 35,000 Nuro-equipped Lucid vehicles. The context is a crowded market: Waymo runs more than 3,000 paid driverless cars across about 20 US cities, Zoox said this month it has carried a million riders, and Tesla now has more robotaxis registered in Texas than Waymo. Uber's bet is that owning the fleet through a partner rather than the technology gets it to scale faster. *(Sources: Business Wire, Electrek, Investing.com, The Next Web, Transport Topics, November 24–25)*

### The EU proposed a €50 billion Sovereign Tech Equity Fund to take direct stakes in AI and quantum companies, with details due in February
The Financial Times reported early Wednesday, citing two officials, that the European Commission plans a fund of up to €50 billion (about $54.7 billion) to take direct equity stakes in European AI and quantum companies, and the Commission confirmed the plan hours later when it presented its Quantum Act in Strasbourg. The Act provides the fund's legal basis; tech commissioner Henna Virkkunen said the vehicle would cover every technology the EU deems critical, not just quantum, and called €50 billion "a sufficient target point" to start while saying €400 billion to €500 billion would be needed in coming years. Officials told the FT the fund would make larger, riskier and longer-term investments than existing EU financing schemes, and the Commission says it will build it with the European Investment Bank Group and member states, with operational details expected in February and one report tying it to the next long-term budget from 2028. The stated aim is to cut dependence on US and Chinese technology; the FT notes Europe attracts about 5% of global venture capital, and European quantum startups raised €1.1 billion in 2025 against roughly €7 billion in the US. The figure is ten times the €5 billion Scaleup Europe Fund. None of this is law: the proposal does not legislate the amount, and the Act still needs the Parliament and member states. *(Sources: Financial Times via The Next Web, European Commission, France 24, EU Reporter, Rapporteur, November 25)*

## Key Themes

**The labs' own staff are now the loudest safety sceptics, and the market priced it.** Axios's two dozen sources describe the same thing from inside both companies: internal models that outrun the tools meant to check them, and employees who no longer trust the release process. Anthropic's answer, that internal use is not a safe harbour, is a real position, but it is also the position of a company whose Riemann proof came from a model it will not ship. The stock closing below the offer price on the same day is not only about lockups; investors were handed a reason to doubt that the next model arrives on schedule.

**The Nvidia correction is global, and the second-order losers are in Asia.** SoftBank, Samsung, SK Hynix, Advantest and Tokyo Electron fell further on Wednesday than Nvidia did on Tuesday, because they are the leveraged version of the same trade. Dell's record backlog with mid-single-digit margins is the picture in one line: demand is not the problem, and the price of serving it is. Huang's apology changes none of the open items, and Treasury's deadline, the Entity List and the December hearings now run on their own clocks.

**Agents are becoming the interface to shopping, and the platforms are negotiating terms.** Meta put Muse on top of Instagram's saved posts, Forter's data says Muse already handles half of agent orders, and Amazon is at the table because an agent that skips sponsored listings threatens its ad business. The Athena report is the enterprise version of the same problem: the agent is only as good as the definitions behind the data, and OpenAI is building the layer it needs rather than relying on the platforms that own it.

**A mathematics result from an unnamed model is the day's most consequential claim, and the least checked.** The 9/4 upper bound now has two outside write-ups; the lower bound has none, and the Lean proof covers less than the paper. Europe's €50 billion answer to all of this is a fund that does not yet exist.
