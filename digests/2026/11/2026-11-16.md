## 1. Frontier Model News

### Jensen Huang is discussing a return to Nvidia with allies, the FT reports, as a reinstatement petition passes 170,000 signatures
Four days after resigning, Jensen Huang is considering a comeback as Nvidia's chief executive, the Financial Times reported on Sunday, citing people close to him; one ally said a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives is being assembled to lobby the board and seek White House backing. Neither Huang nor Nvidia has confirmed it. A "Stand with Jensen" petition passed 100,000 signatures on Saturday, including 5,248 people identifying as current employees, and stood above 170,000 by Monday morning per CNBC, with Elon Musk and Y Combinator's Garry Tan among the signers. On Friday, Altimeter's Brad Gerstner and Atreides' Gavin Baker asked the board in a letter posted on X to run a slow, deliberate search that leaves "all options on the table, including the potential return of Jensen Huang,". Pulling the other way, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Fox News on Sunday that Nvidia needs a "dramatic shakeup" and an outside CEO, and gave the board and its outside counsel 10 days to explain how they supervised export compliance. Nvidia told CNBC that speculation about candidates is premature. Shares fell about 2% on Friday and were down a further 2.2% at $159.32 premarket on Monday, after the longest weekly losing streak since 2012. Interim CEO Colette Kress presents fiscal Q3 results on Tuesday against a $108 billion guide, with options pricing an 8.3% move. *(Sources: Financial Times via Bloomberg and Reuters, CNBC, Forbes, Gizmodo, Benzinga, Verdict, Fox Business, November 14–16)*

### The US and nine partners signed the San Francisco Declaration to guard AI supply chains, and Chinese AI stocks fell up to 18% on Monday
Under Secretary of State Jacob Helberg convened a Pax Silica founders' retreat in San Francisco on Saturday at which the United States, Australia, India, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Qatar, Singapore, the UAE and the UK signed the San Francisco Declaration on the Secure Foundations of Enduring Prosperity, first reported by the Financial Times. The signatories commit to jointly identify and protect AI technologies and supply chains they consider vulnerable, spanning semiconductors, critical minerals and energy, through five proposed working groups; the text does not name China. Helberg told the FT that Germany, Sweden, the Netherlands and the European Commission are expected to sign next week. The market reaction landed in Hong Kong on Monday: The Information reports MiniMax fell 18% and Z.ai 13%, extending a slide that began with Huang's resignation, and the FT says Alibaba and Tencent lost a combined $75 billion of market value last week on falls of 14% and 8%. The fear is a Commerce Department rule, reported by The Information on November 12 and not yet published, that would extend chip controls to Chinese firms' remote use of Blackwell-class hardware in seven more countries including Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand. Baker McKenzie has argued Commerce lacks statutory authority for remote-access controls, and the Remote Access Security Act that would grant it passed the House 369 to 22 in January but has not moved in the Senate. *(Sources: Financial Times via Bloomberg and Business Standard, Investing.com, The Information, Tribune India, The Wire, Deccan Chronicle, November 15–16)*

### SpaceX's AI group cut about 60 people from the Grok Imagine video team to concentrate on coding tools and Grok Bot, The Information reports
The Information reported on Friday that SpaceX's AI division, the former xAI, laid off roughly 60 employees from Grok Imagine, its image and video generator, in the preceding week, which it says is more than half the team; a small crew remains to keep the product running. SpaceX has not commented, and the scale and Imagine's future are unconfirmed. The reported rationale is that management now wants compute and headcount behind AI coding tools and Grok Bot, the agent product that left beta on November 11, rather than visual media models: video generation uses far more compute per request than text, and Imagine trails leading Chinese and US video models on independent benchmarks. The decision reverses Musk's March statement that the company was doubling down on Imagine, per 24/7 Wall St., and follows Musk's November 10 post that Grok 4.8 has finished pre-training, is in reinforcement learning and would soft-roll to Bot users; no model page, price or release note for 4.8 has appeared. One outlet says the Imagine tab vanished for many accounts on November 12 and 13, which we could not verify. 24/7 Wall St. puts the Super Intelligence unit's third-quarter revenue at $7.6 billion, up 830% year on year on cloud agreements and Cursor and Grok adoption; that figure appears in one outlet only. *(Sources: The Information via Crypto Briefing, mlq.ai and Startup Fortune, 24/7 Wall St., FourWeekMBA, November 13–14)*

### Four nonprofits are suing OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceX for a court order freezing frontier AI development
Public Citizen, the Heat Initiative, the Irreplaceable Education Fund and the Alliance for Secure AI filed a 63-page complaint in San Francisco Superior Court on November 12, with coverage spreading over the weekend; the suit was not in our November 13 digest. Former New Jersey Attorney General Matthew Platkin filed on the plaintiffs' behalf. The defendants are Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and two SpaceX Super Intelligence entities. The complaint brings a single claim under California's Unfair Competition Law on a public-nuisance theory, rather than any federal AI statute, and seeks an injunction barring the companies from building or deploying systems more capable than their top models as of the filing date, and from using AI to autonomously direct research on more capable systems. Existing products such as ChatGPT and Claude would not have to shut down. As evidence the complaint cites the companies' own statements that their technology poses catastrophic risks, and incidents in which agents reached outside test environments, including Anthropic's July 30 review of 141,006 evaluation runs that found three cases of Claude models gaining unauthorised access to real organisations' systems, and an OpenAI incident. None of the defendants has responded publicly, and no judge has ruled; one legal analysis expects them to contest standing, the reach of the statute and the feasibility of the injunction. One outlet calls the court federal, but most say state court. *(Sources: Just Security, Unite.AI, Crypto Briefing, Yahoo News, True North News, November 12–15)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, letting its agents pay with tokenised Amex, Mastercard and Visa cards after a biometric approval
OpenAI announced ChatGPT Wallet on Thursday evening, built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, as the payment layer for purchases made by its agents. The initial rollout is to ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States aged 18 or over with eligible US-issued cards, and works only inside conversations with dots, OpenAI's autonomous agents, not in ordinary ChatGPT chats; OpenAI's own post describes it as covering agents in ChatGPT Work, Codex and dots, which most coverage reads as the eventual scope. A user stores a card once. For every purchase the agent presents the items, total and card, and the user approves with Face ID or a fingerprint; the payment network then issues a token, a substitute card number with its own expiry and security code, so neither the merchant nor the agent sees the real card. Each approval creates a record of what the agent was authorised to buy. OpenAI says spending caps, approved categories and time windows that would allow pre-authorised purchases are planned, and that it is "starting small." The Wallet terms say OpenAI is not a bank, issuer, network, lender or merchant, and the merchant remains the seller. OpenAI warns that the feature will not work on sites that block agents and that merchant risk systems may flag agent purchases as fraud, which two days earlier was United Airlines' stated reason for banning them. Sources disagree on whether debit and virtual cards qualify. *(Sources: OpenAI announcement via Decrypt, heise, gHacks, 9to5Mac, Android Authority, The Paypers, Quartz, November 13–15)*

### LangChain raised a $300 million Series C at a $3 billion valuation led by CapitalG, with LangSmith at more than 7,000 organisations
LangChain announced the round on Thursday. Alphabet's growth fund CapitalG led, new investors GIC and DFJ Growth took significant stakes, and existing backers IVP and Sapphire Ventures returned. The valuation is 2.4 times the roughly $1.25 billion set by the $125 million IVP-led Series B about a year ago, and total capital raised is about $460 million. Co-founder and CEO Harrison Chase said the money goes mainly to LangSmith, which the company positions as a framework-agnostic platform to run, improve and govern agents regardless of whether they are built on LangChain's own open-source libraries; the company says more than 7,000 organisations use it, naming Nvidia, Workday, Toyota, Harvey, Rippling and Salesforce. The company's blog post says it will show several new LangSmith capabilities on November 20. LangChain disclosed neither revenue nor an allocation of proceeds, and the customer figures are its own. The round is the largest disclosed for an agent-operations vendor this year and lands a week before Anthropic's listing puts a public price on the model layer beneath it. *(Sources: LangChain blog, TechCrunch via secondary coverage, Pulse 2.0, CASRAI, Citybiz, November 13–14)*

### Hugging Face shipped Transformers 5.23 with an exporter overhaul, adding Inkling 2 Small a day after Thinking Machines opened its weights
Transformers v5.23.0 landed on GitHub on November 13. The headline change is that every export, to Dynamo, ONNX Runtime, OpenVINO or ExecuTorch, now returns an `ExportedModel` that can be called like the original, pushed to the Hub and reloaded on its backend; the release notes claim speedups over eager PyTorch and `torch.compile`, with no figures we could check. Breaking changes include removal of the `ep_router` expert-parallel style, reworked dispatch for MXFP4 GPT-OSS and DeepGEMM mixture-of-experts kernels, a slimmed audio extra that drops librosa and phonemizer, and deprecation of the pyctcdecode language-model decoding path. New architectures include Liquid AI's LFM3 and LFM3-VL, Dots 3 Note Preview, IQuestCoder, InternLM3, a JEV decision head marked experimental, and Inkling 2 Small from Thinking Machines Lab. That model's weights went up on Hugging Face on November 12, a week after its closed launch on Tinker: a 594-billion-parameter mixture of experts with 17 billion active per token, 8 of 384 experts per token, text and image input and a 1 million token window, listed as Apache 2.0 although one review notes the repository has no LICENSE file and links an acceptable-use policy. The NVFP4 checkpoint needs about 500 GB of GPU memory and the MXFP8 build about 800 GB. Thinking Machines' self-reported Artificial Analysis index is about 31, and Tinker lists it at $0.30/$1.00 per million tokens. Sources disagree on whether it accepts audio. *(Sources: Hugging Face GitHub release, Thinking Machines on X, CASRAI, AlphaSignal, MarkTechPost, byteiota, November 12–13)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### LVMH's media division, with 17 titles and about 700 journalists, signed a newsroom and content deal with OpenAI
OpenAI and Groupe Les Echos-Le Parisien, LVMH's media division, announced a strategic partnership on Monday. The division's 17 brands, including Les Echos, Le Parisien, Paris Match, Challenges, L'Opinion, Investir and L'Agefi, get OpenAI's tools and ChatGPT Enterprise for staff to build applications for daily work; Les Echos lists documentary research, document comparison and preparatory translation, and Le Parisien adds archive and data exploration. In return, ChatGPT will surface the titles' journalism as summaries and excerpts that name the source and link to the original article. The division employs about 1,800 people, of whom nearly 700 are journalists, per Les Echos; Le Parisien gives 1,900 staff and more than 800 journalists, and calls the product ChatGPT Business rather than Enterprise. Financial terms were not disclosed. Zonebourse notes this is OpenAI's second French news agreement after Le Monde. One analysis calls the deal mainly an attribution and discoverability win that works for LVMH only if ChatGPT becomes a referral channel rather than a substitute for its own sites; neither side published traffic or productivity figures, and the division is the same group whose owner, Bernard Arnault, barred LVMH staff from reading rival French outlets in 2024. *(Sources: OpenAI announcement, Les Echos and Le Parisien via FourWeekMBA and Zonebourse, Cryptonomist, Superpower Daily, November 16)*

### Security Bank says staff have built 3,100 AI agents after training 3,000 employees, with 98% daily use of 1,210 Copilot licences
The Philippines' Security Bank told the Philippine Star that it has trained more than 3,000 employees in agentic AI and that staff, not only technologists, have built 3,100 "low-level AI agents" for business and operational functions across business, operations, finance and risk. Chief digital and technology officer Patrick Meneses said the bank records 98% daily engagement among the 1,210 employees holding Microsoft Copilot Premium licences, and that the aim is for agents to absorb routine work so staff can concentrate on customer service and judgement calls; he said a human stays in the loop on the bank's AI systems. The bank was one of two Philippine organisations in Microsoft's 365 Copilot early-access programme in 2023 and runs an internal help-desk chatbot called Atlas. The numbers are the bank's own, the article gives no definition of a "low-level" agent, and no productivity, cost or error figures were published. It is nevertheless one of the few disclosed counts of employee-built agents at a mid-sized bank, and it points the same way as Databricks' claim last week that GM runs more than 1,000 Genie agents: the volume is in small, narrow agents built by business staff rather than a few large deployments. *(Sources: Philippine Star, Security Bank newsroom, Manila Standard, November 15)*

## Key Themes

**Nvidia's succession has become a contest between its shareholders and its regulator.** Huang's reported comeback effort, the investor letter and a petition past 170,000 names all push the board one way; Bessent's 10-day demand and call for an outsider push the other, with the fiscal Q3 call on Tuesday as the first forum where Kress must answer both. Whatever the board decides, the export-compliance question has outlived the personal-conduct one.

**Chip policy is now being made by coalition, not by rule.** The San Francisco Declaration binds ten governments to a shared view of supply-chain vulnerability before Commerce has published its remote-access rule or the Senate has voted on the statute that would authorise it. Hong Kong priced the direction of travel on Monday regardless.

**The courts are being asked to do what Congress has not.** The nonprofits' suit asks a state judge to freeze frontier development using the labs' own safety disclosures as evidence, and does so a week before Anthropic's listing puts those disclosures in a prospectus. It is unlikely to win quickly, but it makes every published red-team result a potential exhibit.

**Agent commerce and agent operations are both getting their own infrastructure.** ChatGPT Wallet gives agents a tokenised card and an audit trail, LangChain's round funds the governance layer enterprises buy before they trust an agent, and Security Bank's 3,100 small agents show what that layer will have to manage. The counterweight is capital discipline: SpaceX is cutting a video team to fund agents, and the Imagine cuts came the same week United said it would not let agents buy at all.
