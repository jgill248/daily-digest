## 1. Frontier Model News

### Anthropic opened at $111.50 and closed its first day at $97.17, up 17%, then fell about 6% premarket on Friday as the AI selloff resumed
ANT's first trade printed at 11:48 a.m. Eastern on Thursday at $111.50, 34% above the $83 offer price, after Dario and Daniela Amodei rang the Nasdaq opening bell at 9:30. The stock touched $115.10, then faded with the wider market to close at $97.17, up 17.07%, on a day the Nasdaq Composite fell almost 2.3% and US indexes hit seven-month lows. Roughly 840 to 846 million shares changed hands, close to 86% of the 980 million sold. Market-cap figures diverge by share count: CNBC cites about $1.8 trillion fully diluted, CNN and NBC about $1.76 trillion, and Forbes-sourced tallies $1.27 trillion on shares outstanding. Shares slipped to $92.60 after hours and were down 6.37% in premarket trading at 5 a.m. Friday, with Nvidia off 2.67% and AMD off 5.43%; SoftBank fell 16% in Tokyo. At the bell, Amodei said the listing "puts the company in a stronger financial position, and in doing so, only strengthens our resolve to do the right thing," that Anthropic would "not bend to either business nor political pressure," and that "we call this a race to the top." Anthropic's release says the offering closes today with a 30-day option on 147 million more shares; Reuters reports options could trade as soon as Monday and up to 303 million shares become eligible for sale on December 15. *(Sources: CNBC, TechCrunch, NPR, CNN, NBC News, Yahoo Finance, Reuters, Anthropic, November 19–20)*

### California subpoenaed Nvidia over chips reaching China, as a House committee said Commerce approved 879,000 AI chips for Chinese firms in six months
Attorney General Rob Bonta issued an investigative subpoena to Nvidia on Thursday asking whether its advanced chips or confidential information reached China and whether the company misled investors about that risk. Bonta said the conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO "raises serious red flags," particularly around investor disclosures; the probe follows Jensen Huang's resignation over an affair with Alice Huang, who is linked to Singapore-based Megaspeed, itself under investigation for diverting restricted chips. Nvidia did not immediately comment, per CNBC. The same day, House Foreign Affairs chair Brian Mast used Export Control Reform Act authority to publish licensing data showing the Commerce Department approved more than 879,000 Nvidia and AMD chips for Chinese companies between December 2025 and May 2026, out of 3.4 million requested, including 150,000 for Tencent, 75,000 for Alibaba, 43,000 for Baidu and 4,000 for BYD, all on the Pentagon's 1260H list. Applications for 610,000 chips for Alibaba and 508,000 for a ByteDance unit in Malaysia were not approved. How many shipped is unclear: some records are marked "approved, not shipped," and Nvidia said it "has since stopped shipping H200s to China." Commerce said the approvals followed a rule published in January. Nvidia closed at $145.98, down 3.3%, with the session low a new 52-week low; the stock is down about 29% in November. Huang and general counsel Tim Teter are due before congressional committees on December 1 and 2. *(Sources: CNBC, Reuters, The Hill, Al Jazeera, Yahoo Finance, November 19–20)*

### Reuters found Meta's Muse agent read Signal messages and surfaced bank balances unasked, as The Information says the Muse Charm will cost $300
A Reuters investigation, run with Signal staff and the AI Now Institute and based on more than two dozen internal Meta posts, found that the Muse agent launched in September pulled encrypted Signal messages out of Android push notifications and macOS accessibility and screenshot tools, without the user connecting Signal; the iPhone version blocked it. One employee called it an "extreme privacy violation." Another reported Muse inserting an unrequested Capital One balance of $2,722.58 and a Charles Schwab balance of $5,899.57 into a chat about fishing weather; Reuters could not establish whether the figures were real or invented. Meta spokesperson Daniel Roberts called the cases "isolated incidents," said the agent reaches other apps only with permissions granted at setup, and said many flagged account mix-ups were hallucinations that have been addressed. Signal president Meredith Whittaker called the deployment "technologically reckless" and asked operating-system makers to let developers mark apps off-limits to agents; Apple and Google said they are considering more control over agent permissions. Separately, The Information reports Meta will price the Muse Charm, a pocket device with a roughly two-inch screen that reaches the agent without a phone, at $300 with up to 12 months of Verizon 5G, and has delayed a smartwatch to focus on it. Meta said it was "charmed by the interest" and did not deny the figure. *(Sources: Reuters, The Information, 9to5Mac, Digital Trends, Gizmodo, November 18–20)*

### SpaceX sold $32 billion of chip-backed bonds yielding above 8% to lease $47.3 billion of Nvidia Rubin GPUs for its AI unit
Bloomberg reports SpaceX, which owns xAI, sold about $32 billion of bonds in three tranches maturing in four to six years to fund AI chips, with the largest piece, about $25.2 billion of Class A3 notes, priced at 8.259%, a high yield for investment-grade paper. Goldman Sachs and Apollo ran the deal. The chips are bought through a special-purpose vehicle, Saturn Funding Trust, managed by an Apollo-backed entity, and leased to SpaceX for about five years, a structure secondary outlets describe as keeping the debt off SpaceX's balance sheet; Bloomberg itself does not use that phrase. The bonds sit inside a package of more than $40 billion that includes a senior secured loan and bank loans, against roughly $47.3 billion of Rubin GPUs with deliveries from January 2027. The sources do not say the chips are for Colossus specifically. The deal lands a day after Nvidia's board said it is "rigorously evaluating" its own guarantees and the Journal reported its $500 billion lending pledge had funded nothing, and in the same week Bloomberg flagged credit strains across AI financing. SpaceX shares were reported 38.7% below their post-IPO high as the debt priced, a figure from a secondary tracker rather than Bloomberg. *(Sources: Bloomberg via The Edge Singapore, Seeking Alpha, GuruFocus, November 19–20)*

### OpenAI says its models improved AMD's flagship GPU design by up to 14% and shrank its Jalapeño chip, and is training a GPT-Synopsys model
In a technical report published November 18 and picked up widely Thursday, OpenAI says its models rewrote SystemVerilog RTL for AMD's flagship GPU IP and achieved up to 14% better power, performance and area against an already heavily optimised baseline, with some rewrites included in a design sent to manufacturing; it frames that as compressing about a year of progress into five months. On Jalapeño, its first custom inference chip, OpenAI says it began using o3-mini in early 2025 and that model-proposed changes cut standard-cell area by about 13% in the vector unit and 10% in the matrix unit, proven in silicon during bring-up. It is also training GPT-Synopsys with Synopsys to drive that company's EDA tools "the way expert chip designers do"; Synopsys said customer design data will not be used for training and will be encrypted with configurable retention and audit controls. All figures are OpenAI's own, measured against its baselines rather than finished silicon, and no third party has reproduced them. The claim fits a week in which OpenAI also rated GPT-6.2 Astra "High" for AI self-improvement for the first time. *(Sources: OpenAI, Unite.AI, Digital Today, DEV Community, November 18–19)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Google gave the Gemini Mac app computer use, letting it click, type and fill forms in native apps, three days after Apple split macOS agent permissions
Computer use in the Gemini app for Mac rolled out Thursday in US English to Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra subscribers on Apple Silicon Macs running macOS Sequoia 15 or later. Users add "@Computer use" to a prompt; Gemini then opens apps, navigates menus, clicks, types and fills forms in a background window shown as a live preview in the chat, leaving the user's own screen and cursor free. Setup requires macOS Accessibility and Screen & System Audio Recording permissions. Users choose which apps it can touch and can stop it with one click; Google pauses for logins and payments and requires confirmation for sensitive actions even when persistent permissions are on. Google's own warnings are unusually direct: Gemini can reach active sessions in native apps, can read or act on multifactor codes visible in desktop or messaging apps, and untrusted content in one app could redirect it into acting in another it has access to. AppleInsider's verdict was that users still should not let it. The launch comes three days after Apple's macOS 27.3 developer beta 2 split Accessibility from a new "System & App Protection Override" permission, added per-use approval and an on-screen indicator when one app controls another. Windows and ChromeOS support is not announced. *(Sources: 9to5Google, TechCrunch, The New Stack, AppleInsider, November 19)*

### OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Accounting with eight finance plugins, and Xero added month-end close reviews to it a day later
ChatGPT for Accounting, launched November 18 for ChatGPT Work and Business plans a day after ChatGPT for Sales, is an Accounting plugin at version 1.0.0 plus eight partner plugins from seven vendors: Intuit QuickBooks, Xero, Oracle NetSuite, BILL, FreshBooks, Rillet and Campfire. OpenAI pitches it for invoice processing, bank reconciliation, month-end reports, data entry and audit preparation; coverage notes the launch post does not say ChatGPT books entries on its own or prepares tax returns, and that people review final entries and confirm data changes. Business and Enterprise data is not used for training by default. On Thursday Xero expanded its existing Xero-in-ChatGPT integration so accountants and bookkeepers can start month-end close reviews, client portfolio reviews and quarterly review prep inside ChatGPT, reading profit and loss, balance sheet, invoice and bill data it describes as real time, with any resulting changes made back in Xero so the ledger and audit trail stay there. The Xero integration is US-only for now. Counts differ across write-ups, with one listing eight tiles and another 33 for the Sales launch; neither OpenAI nor Xero disclosed pricing beyond the plan tiers. *(Sources: OpenAI, Xero, Dataconomy, Crypto Briefing, Runtime Wire, November 18–19)*

### LangChain put LangSmith Tool Gateway into public beta, as a Claude Code bug-hunting skill bundle topped GitHub trending with 1,349 stars in a day
LangSmith Tool Gateway, launched Thursday on Plus and Enterprise plans, is a managed layer between agents and the tools they call. It ships built-in servers for Google, Microsoft, Salesforce, GitHub and Linear and lets teams register their own MCP servers by URL; "tool kits" limit which tools an agent can see, calls can run under a user's own credentials or under service credentials for autonomous agents, approval policies insert a human at the point of a sensitive call, and traces record who called what and the outcome but not inputs or outputs. Deny policies and org-wide audit logs are Enterprise-only, and the beta covers GCP US, EU and APAC and AWS US but not Azure. It sits beside LLM Gateway, which handles model-call spend limits and fallbacks, and precedes a release event LangChain promised for today. On GitHub, Claude-BugHunter led Friday's trending list at 13,358 stars after 1,349 in a day: a dual-licensed bundle of 83 Claude Code skills for authorised bug-bounty and red-team work, 58 of them built from disclosed HackerOne reports, with Burp and HackerOne MCP integrations and a "7-Question Gate" scope check. Its README warns that Anthropic's cyber safeguards may block authorised offensive work and tells users not to reword engagements to evade classifiers. HKUDS's CLI-Anything, which generates agent-usable CLIs for desktop software, was third at about 61,000 stars. *(Sources: LangChain, Unite.AI, GitHub, November 19–20)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### OpenAI's Performance Factory ran 1,700 Codex investigations into ChatGPT slowdowns, found 600 root causes and shipped 15 agent-written fixes
In an engineering post and a VentureBeat exclusive on Thursday, OpenAI described a system that opens a task when a performance alert fires, assigns a Codex agent to investigate, records findings and routes a proposed fix to an engineer. In a little over three months it logged more than 1,700 automated investigations, about 17 a day, and identified root causes in more than 600; roughly 400 of those produced a proposed fix, of which 15 shipped as Codex-authored pull requests as written and about 50 more influenced fixes engineers completed themselves. A separate Codex session challenges each diagnosis in an adversarial review loop that currently sends back around 25% of them, and OpenAI has extended that to proposed fixes. In a manual audit of 100 root-cause suggestions, OpenAI graded 70% accurate; that figure appears in secondary coverage rather than the VentureBeat excerpts seen here. Engineers keep final approval over every merge, and OpenAI says it is only exploring whether low-risk fixes could ship without a human. VentureBeat's read is that Codex does best when the cause is clear and the fix contained. The conversion rate, about 15 shipped from 1,700 probes, is the useful number for anyone sizing similar deployments. *(Sources: OpenAI, VentureBeat, Crypto Briefing, TokenPost, November 19–20)*

### A bipartisan bill would create a cyber and AI incident board inside CISA, after banks and the NSA reportedly told Congress rushed patches for AI-found flaws caused the week's outages
The ROOT CAUSE Act, H.R. 11046, introduced by Rep. Morgan Luttrell with Rep. Ritchie Torres as original cosponsor, would establish a National Cyber and AI Investigations Board within CISA to examine significant incidents, including those involving AI, find root causes and recommend fixes. Investigations would need a majority vote and published criteria, the executive branch could not abolish or suspend the board without another act of Congress, outside officials could not alter its findings, and it would have no regulatory or enforcement power. It is one of four House Homeland Security AI-cyber bills introduced this week and follows outages at Apple Pay, NASA's internal network, the FAA, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and JPMorgan, and a near two-day Microsoft outage of Outlook, Teams and Copilot. The Washington Sun reported on November 17, citing three unnamed sources, that bank executives and the NSA told lawmakers the failures came from pushing through more patches for AI-discovered vulnerabilities than systems were designed to absorb; Fortune carried a similar account. Official explanations differ: JPMorgan blamed software bugs, the NSA cited "scheduled routine maintenance," the DNI told a House committee there was no attack, and Rapid7's Christiaan Beek told Bloomberg he sees no public evidence rushed updates were the cause. TechCrunch's framing was an "AI-native Y2K," with investor Dave DeWalt calling it "growing pains of a security system that's getting stronger." *(Sources: Nextgov, The Washington Sun, Fortune, TechCrunch, Bloomberg, November 17–19)*

### Canva shipped a one-click switch that hides most of its generative AI tools, as Salesforce reportedly capped employee token spend at $100 to $1,000 a month
Canva added an "Enable generative AI features" toggle under Profile, Settings, Permissions on Thursday that hides Magic Expand, Magic Write, Magic Design, Magic Layers, Magic Edit, Dream Lab, Canva Code and AI Shapes and blocks image generation in its chatbot; Background Remover and Magic Grab stay on because they only edit an existing image. A second setting under AI personalization "reduces" the odds of AI-generated stock images appearing in search without promising to block them. Both options are available to Free and Pro accounts, where previously only business and education admins could restrict the features for teams, per The Verge. On the demand side, The Information reports COO Miguel Milano's memo told Salesforce staff on Monday that every role now has a monthly AI token budget, about $100 for non-technical sales roles up to $1,000 for technical ones, after some employees ran up thousands of dollars a month; a Slack app called DevBar warns the employee and manager near the limit and allows pre-approved overruns of up to 20%. A Salesforce spokesperson told Business Insider there are no companywide individual budgets and usage is left to team leaders, which conflicts with the memo reporting. Salesforce also cut jobs across several teams this week, on a scale not reported, and shares fell about 3%. *(Sources: The Verge, 9to5Mac, Engadget, The Information, Business Insider, November 17–19)*

## Key Themes

**The IPO was a success and the tape didn't care.** Anthropic priced above range, opened 34% up and still closed on a day the Nasdaq lost more than 2%, then gave back a quarter of its gain before Friday's open. SpaceX paid 8.26% for chip debt in the same 24 hours, Nvidia hit a 52-week low despite a record quarter, and SoftBank lost 16%. The market is pricing AI financing risk and AI equity upside at the same time, and the Anthropic close is the clearest single data point yet that the former is winning.

**Agents are being given the keys, and the keyholders are nervous.** Google's Mac computer use ships with warnings that it can read your MFA codes and be hijacked by content in another app; Apple split the permissions three days earlier. Reuters shows what happens without those guardrails: Meta's Muse read Signal through notification permissions nobody thought of as a connector. LangChain's Tool Gateway and Canva's off switch are the same instinct from the supply side, a product whose feature is restraint.

**The China chip story has moved from policy to subpoena.** Yesterday's halt of Hopper sales and today's California subpoena and 879,000-chip disclosure make Nvidia's export record a legal question rather than a trade one, with Huang before Congress in twelve days.

**Self-improvement is showing up as plumbing, not magic.** OpenAI's chip-design and Performance Factory posts are the concrete version of the "High" self-improvement rating it gave Astra on Tuesday: models rewriting RTL that reached manufacturing, and agents root-causing 600 regressions but shipping 15 fixes. The ratios matter more than the headlines.
