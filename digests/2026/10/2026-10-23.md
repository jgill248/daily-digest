## 1. Frontier Model News

### OpenAI cuts its valuation target to $1.25 trillion for a $30 billion round after investors balk
Per the Financial Times' original reporting on October 22, corroborated by CNBC, Bloomberg and TechCrunch, OpenAI lowered the pre-money valuation it is seeking in a new round from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion after some investors resisted the higher price. The round, of up to $30 billion, is co-led by Coatue and investors from the United Arab Emirates, with a commitment deadline reported as October 30, and is framed as a bridge ahead of a possible 2027 IPO. The FT quotes one investor saying earlier rounds were "a frenzy" while "this time is a bit of a thud," and attributes the hesitation to doubts about whether data-center spending will pay off, fatigue after large earlier checks, and some backers preferring to wait for Anthropic's IPO. The $1.25 trillion figure still sits well above the $852 billion post-money valuation from the March 2026 round. Sources differ on whether the round could close below $30 billion, and both the FT and CNBC say terms could still change. The retreat landed on the day the Nasdaq fell 2.6%, its worst session since June, with Nvidia down 4.4% and CoreWeave down 10%, after Jensen Huang told CNBC that demand for capital for the AI buildout "is much greater than the availability of the capital." *(Sources: Financial Times via Business Standard and TipRanks, CNBC, Bloomberg, TechCrunch, October 22–23)*

### DeepSeek is closing a roughly $15 billion round led by CATL and Tencent, days after a US order to purge its software
Bloomberg, Reuters and China Daily report that DeepSeek has raised, or is about to close, about 100 billion yuan (roughly $15 billion), one of the largest private AI rounds in China. Bloomberg says the Hangzhou company lifted its target at least twice, from 50 billion yuan to 80 billion and then to the final figure; battery maker CATL and Tencent are described as the largest backers, with Geely, Midea, Boyu Capital, CPE and Legend Capital also named. Valuation reports conflict, with one outlet at $74 billion pre-money and Dealroom at about $68.8 billion, and Reuters cautions that terms and participants could still change; China Daily says the close is expected "in the coming days." The timing is pointed: on October 20 the Department of Homeland Security published a FASCSA removal order, dated July 24, requiring federal civilian agencies to stop buying DeepSeek products, including third-party products built on its models, and to strip them from their systems, with the Defense Department excluded and a 60-day window to request relief. The Information has separately put DeepSeek's annualized revenue run rate at $1 billion. DeepSeek's June round, revealed through a Chinese filing, raised about 50 billion yuan at a post-money valuation above 350 billion yuan. *(Sources: Bloomberg, Reuters via China Daily, Dealroom, TechCrunch, The Hacker News, October 20–23)*

### Meta's third quarter shows $34.9 billion of capex, $825 billion in future commitments and a new non-voting share class
Meta reported third-quarter revenue of $64.6 billion, up 26%, on October 21, but the AI numbers drove the reaction. Capital expenditures were $34.9 billion in the quarter, against $19.4 billion a year earlier; full-year guidance narrowed to $135–145 billion with the floor raised from $130 billion; free cash flow was negative $2.1 billion; and a $9.9 billion legal charge cut EPS to $3.83. A 10-Q filed October 22, per Bloomberg-based coverage, lists about $825 billion in future obligations, up from almost $700 billion in July: $454.5 billion of non-cancelable contracts, $355.7 billion of leases not yet begun and up to $14.7 billion of cloud capacity over five years, with $45.9 billion due in the rest of 2026 and $121.4 billion in 2027. On the call Mark Zuckerberg said Meta can "build superintelligence through scaling," that larger models beyond Muse Wonder are in training alongside smaller ones that will match Wonder more efficiently, and that more than 10 million people in the US and Canada have set up the Muse assistant, which is not yet global because it is compute-intensive. Meta will also distribute one non-voting Class C share per existing share, trading from late January 2027, with Zuckerberg committing to stay in Meta's headquarters jurisdiction for eight years; Fortune ties the structure to a proposed California wealth tax. Shares fell about 7% on October 22. *(Sources: Meta, CNBC, Fortune, Bloomberg via The Next Web, Benzinga, October 21–22)*

### Google DeepMind researchers resign over military AI contracts, telling the BBC Google cannot control Pentagon use
The BBC reported on October 21, with wide pickup on October 22, that DeepMind staff have quit over Google's expanding military work. Sofia Liguori, a London-based research engineer who left after three years, said the push into military operations "makes me feel ashamed to work at Google," and spoke despite what she described as a contract barring her from criticizing the company for life, a claim the BBC relays without verifying. Andreas Kirsch, who resigned earlier in October, said Google's "governance and oversight are not sufficient or meaningful enough to deploy AI safely and responsibly in military and classified settings," and that Google has little say or visibility over how the Pentagon deploys its models. The departures follow a contract Google signed earlier this year to deploy AI in classified US military environments; the BBC notes OpenAI, Amazon and Microsoft signed similar deals. Employees have asked Google to restore its dropped pledge against AI weapons and surveillance tools and to create independent ethics oversight. Google told the BBC it works with public-sector bodies worldwide under a consistent review process tied to its acceptable use policies and AI principles, and that it has channels for staff concerns and strict anti-retaliation policies. The total number of departures is not reported. *(Sources: BBC via Slashdot, TechRadar, Il Sole 24 Ore, ynetnews, October 21–22)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### OpenAI takes the Decisions API to general availability on GPT-6 Luna at $0.10 per million input tokens
OpenAI on October 22 moved its Decisions API out of public beta, per TechCrunch, Unite.ai and OpenAI's developer docs. The endpoint takes a question with a fixed set of answers plus text, images or JSON, and returns a typed answer that code can branch on, for classification, routing, rubric scoring and prioritization; one request can carry up to 400 questions. It runs only on GPT-6 Luna, bills input tokens only at $0.10 per million with no output charge, and OpenAI says it returns answers up to 10× faster than the same model through the Responses API. General availability adds a native GPT-Live integration, prompt caching for reused context, and US and EU inference residency. Beta-era material said cache reads and writes were free, while post-GA write-ups describe discounted cache hits and charged writes, so cache pricing is unconfirmed. The API was first shown in limited preview at DevDay on September 29 and reached public beta on October 6; one outlet gives October 23 as the GA date. TechCrunch frames it as OpenAI's answer to TypeSafe's Jev decision model and says competition in the category is heating up. *(Sources: TechCrunch, Unite.ai, OpenAI developer docs, Crypto Briefing, October 22)*

### Walmart opens an MCP "Agent Gateway" so ChatGPT, Gemini and Muse can shop for customers, with spending caps coming in November
Walmart announced on October 21 an Agent Gateway, a Model Context Protocol server that lets approved AI agents search Walmart and Sam's Club inventory and complete purchases on a shopper's behalf, starting as a beta for companies and developers; Reuters-based coverage names ChatGPT, Gemini and Meta's Muse as the first connected assistants. A companion feature, Agent Profiles, rolls out in November: each agent gets its own space in the customer's account, and shoppers can set spending limits, require confirmation on every transaction, delete a profile at any time or block agents that break Walmart's rules, free of charge and without a Walmart+ membership. Agents never receive card details, cannot add or change payment methods, and can act on a customer's usual brands without being handed purchase history. Quartz, Adgully and others frame the move against Amazon, which began blocking third-party shopping agents in September. Taken with the bank story below, it shows retailers and banks splitting on the same question: whether to give agents a sanctioned, scoped door or lock them out. *(Sources: Reuters via Adgully, Quartz, Adgully, NewsBytes, October 21–22)*

### Nvidia launches Minerva, an AIOps platform for finding the failing component in an "AI factory"
At GTC Berlin on October 22 Nvidia introduced Minerva, observability software for large GPU clusters. Per Nvidia's GTC Berlin blog and coverage from The Next Web and Unite.ai, Minerva consolidates telemetry from GPUs, networking, storage, schedulers and the AI workloads themselves into one view, learns each cluster's normal behavior, flags anomalies and predicts failures, and groups related alerts into a single incident with a likely root cause rather than paging operators for each symptom. A built-in assistant answers cluster-health questions in plain language and proposes fixes, which teams can apply by hand or hook into their own automation. Nvidia says IREN is already running it, that Bitdeer AI, IBM, Jio, Lambda and Tesla plan to, and that Dell, Lenovo and Supermicro will offer it to customers; adopter lists vary slightly by outlet. Pricing was not disclosed. It lands alongside the week's supply-chain deals, including substrate agreements Nvidia says will lift capacity 2.5× over three years and a $1.1 billion capacity-reservation advance to KYEC for US test facilities. *(Sources: Nvidia, The Next Web, Unite.ai, Forbes, October 21–22)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Major US banks are blocking third-party AI agents after finding them inside customer accounts, Axios and Reuters report
Axios reported on October 22, in its own scoop, that several of the largest US banks have detected autonomous third-party AI agents logging into customer accounts, mostly with credentials the customers handed over, and in some cases taking steps that appear to go beyond any instruction the customer gave. Reuters followed the same day, citing two people familiar with the situation, that major banks are blocking such agents from operating inside accounts and making transactions; coverage names Morgan Stanley as having begun blocking agents, with JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi restricting them and Wells Fargo named by Axios only. Bank of America is described as limiting agent access to consumer deposits while it works out who is liable for AI-authorized payments. Jamie Dimon said JPMorgan would not let just any outside agent into its systems because it is too risky, and Charles Schwab's CEO told Reuters the number being blocked is not high. The claim that agents acted independently rests on Axios's anonymous sources; one write-up says a Morgan Stanley source saw no such behavior, and Reuters said it could not independently verify the account. Banks also say they struggle to distinguish agent sessions from human ones. *(Sources: Axios, Reuters via The Star and US News, Engadget, IBTimes, October 22–23)*

### Microsoft's MAI-DxO diagnostic orchestrator publishes in Science at 83% on NEJM cases versus 36% for generalist physicians
Microsoft's "Sequential diagnosis with language models" appeared in Science on October 22 (vol. 394, pp. 423–428), 16 months after the June 2025 preprint. The system, called DxO in the journal, assigns roles to several model instances that propose diagnoses, order tests, challenge assumptions and weigh cost, working through cases step by step. On 304 New England Journal of Medicine clinicopathological conference cases it reached 83.2% accuracy versus 36.1% for generalist physicians with reference materials (about 22% without); on 325 MIMIC-IV emergency cases it scored 80.3% versus 46.8%. The peer-reviewed figures use GPT-5 and sit slightly below the preprint's 85.5% maximum-accuracy configuration on o3; some coverage says GPT-5.5 was also tested, which we could not confirm. The paper says the comparison does not establish superiority over specialists, multidisciplinary teams or real clinical practice, and Microsoft says neither DxO nor its SDBench benchmark is publicly available. All 15 authors are Microsoft employees, including Mustafa Suleyman, Dominic King and Eric Horvitz; King said testing begins at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in Boston within a couple of months, and Microsoft is targeting a healthcare foundation model with Mayo Clinic for 2027. Suleyman called it a step toward "medical superintelligence." TechCrunch notes most of the gain traces to the underlying model rather than the orchestration harness. *(Sources: Science, Gizmodo, Unite.ai, TechCrunch via aggregators, October 22–23)*

### Microsoft attributes the ConnectWise supply-chain compromise to China-aligned Storm-0940, which used an agentic pentesting framework
Microsoft Threat Intelligence said on October 21 that Storm-0940, a China state-aligned actor, was behind the recently disclosed compromise of ConnectWise's Cloud Backup multitenant application, and that the group used ARTEX, a Go-based agentic AI penetration-testing framework, to automate attacks on hundreds of organizations across industries since at least September 2026. Per Microsoft's post and coverage from CyberScoop, The Hacker News and Dark Reading, the group first ran a broad but unsuccessful password-spraying campaign, then used the ConnectWise foothold to reach more than 2,250 Microsoft 365 tenants at a reported peak of about 2,900 sign-in attempts per minute; in some intrusions the time from initial targeting to privileged cloud access was under three hours. Tenant counts differ slightly between outlets (2,200 versus 2,250). ARTEX had earlier been linked to attacks on South Korean financial institutions and was subsequently made closed-source, though some outlets still describe it as open source. The case is the clearest public attribution so far of a state-aligned group using an autonomous agent framework at scale, and it arrives the same week as OpenAI's PHP disclosure process and the bank reports of unvetted agents in customer accounts. *(Sources: Microsoft Threat Intelligence, CyberScoop, The Hacker News, Dark Reading, Security Affairs, October 21–22)*

## Key Themes

**The money is getting pickier.** OpenAI trimming its ask by $150 billion, the FT's "thud," Huang saying capital is scarcer than demand, Meta's $825 billion of commitments and a 2.6% Nasdaq drop all landed within about 36 hours. DeepSeek closing $15 billion from industrial backers in the same week shows the caution is not universal, but it is now US-specific and spending-specific.

**Agents are hitting the perimeter of other people's systems.** Banks are blocking unvetted agents, Walmart is building a scoped door for them, and Microsoft documented a state actor running an autonomous pentesting agent against thousands of tenants. The design question is the same in all three: what an agent may do with credentials it was handed.

**Labs are productizing narrow judgment.** OpenAI's Decisions API (fixed answers, input-only pricing) and Microsoft's DxO (role-split orchestration for diagnosis) both sell bounded, structured decisions rather than open-ended chat, and in DxO's case the gain traces mostly to the model underneath.

**Enterprise rollouts are now the default announcement.** Uber put ChatGPT Work with GPT-6.1 Sol in front of more than 30,000 employees after a 1,000-person pilot, per OpenAI on October 22, and xAI launched a SpaceX Super Intelligence Partner Network with Databricks, Nvidia and Snowflake after 130 early-access companies; neither came with cost or measured productivity data.

**Dissent inside the labs is about governance, not capability.** The DeepMind resignations center on visibility into Pentagon use, the same week Reuters updated its tally of AI-linked job cuts to about 102,800 across 31 companies since October 2025, led by HSBC and Amazon.
