## 1. Frontier Model News

### Claude Haiku 5.5 is out after all, at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens, ending last week's "announced but not released" status
Our October 8 digest said Haiku 5.5 was announced but unreleased, with no pricing or benchmarks. That has changed: coverage now dates the release to October 7 (one source says October 8) and lists the model ID `claude-haiku-5-5` on the Claude platform, AWS, Google Cloud and Azure. Anthropic prices prompts under 100K tokens at $0.10 input and $0.50 output per million tokens, about 75% below Haiku 4.5's $1/$5, and above 100K tokens the rates rise to $0.50/$2.50. It is the first Haiku with an adjustable effort setting, and Anthropic positions it as a subagent under Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5. Anthropic reports 72.4% on the offline subset of OSWorld 2.1 at maximum effort, versus 15.7% for Haiku 4.5; Sonnet 5.5 still leads at 83.9%. *(Sources: Let's Data Science, YourStory, AI Weekly, Computingforgeeks, October 7–12)*

The pricing and OSWorld figures are Anthropic's own, repeated by the trade outlets. Two caveats surfaced: one analysis says the new tokenizer produces about 30% more tokens for the same text, which would eat part of the headline saving, and another says the 72.4% is a partial-credit score, with only 37.1% of tasks fully passed. Cost climbs with effort, from 42.0% at about $0.07 per attempt at Low to 72.4% at about $0.61 at Max. Sources disagree on whether an independent evaluation exists: one says Epoch AI's is pending, another says none is published, and a third claims Artificial Analysis posted one on launch day, which we could not find.

### No other frontier launch from OpenAI, Anthropic or Google surfaced in the last 24 hours
Searches for October 12 turned up no new flagship model from the big three. One aggregator lists a Meta "Muse Wonder 1.0" dated October 12, but we found nothing else mentioning it. Meta's confirmed recent releases are the Muse agent (September 8) and Muse Image 1.0 (July 7). *(Sources: nocode.mba release tracker, Axios, October 12)*

Treat Muse Wonder as unconfirmed: it rests on a single aggregator listing and no Meta announcement.

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Nous Research raised $90 million at a reported $1.5 billion valuation to turn its open-source Hermes agent into an enterprise product
Per the WSJ's October 7 report as relayed by secondary outlets, Robot Ventures led the Series B, with Nvidia, Microsoft's M12, Samsung, Union Square Ventures, Y Combinator and Menlo Ventures among the backers; total funding is about $158 million. The money goes to "Hermes for Businesses," which reportedly adds SSO, audit logging, workspace controls and SLAs on top of the MIT-licensed Hermes Agent, with customers choosing their own models and deploying on their own cloud or on-premises. The WSJ figures include 22.7 million downloads and roughly $36 million in annualized revenue by mid-September, with a target above $100 million by year-end. *(Sources: WSJ via Dealroom, AI Weekly, Trending Topics, October 7–12)*

The valuation was not disclosed by the company, and the revenue, download and "widely cloned" claims are Nous's own. Early reports said at least $75 million, so the round size grew between reports. We read the WSJ only through aggregators.

### No new open-source agent framework launched this week; existing projects kept moving
A search for new frameworks found coverage of Google's ADK (October 6) and Microsoft Agent Framework, both existing projects whose announcements are months old. *(Sources: AI Agents Directory brief of October 6, Microsoft DevBlogs)*

We are not counting these as news. The only genuinely new item in this section is the Nous funding above.

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Haiku 5.5's pitch is cost at scale, but no customer has published results yet
Anthropic targets summarization, classification, database queries, live customer support and browser use, and says the model can run as a cheap subagent beside larger ones. The effort-level cost table (about $0.07 per attempt at Low versus $0.61 at Max) is the most concrete deployment signal so far. *(Sources: Let's Data Science, Metricnexus, October 7–12)*

We found no named company reporting production results with Haiku 5.5. Case-study searches returned mostly undated vendor and blog roundups (claims such as a 38% operating-cost cut or ticket deflection rising from 12% to 75%), none tied to a named, verifiable deployment from the past day, so we are not repeating them.

## Key Themes

**Cheap models are the price story.** Haiku 5.5's list price drop lines up with last week's reporting that OpenAI and Anthropic face pressure from open models, but the tokenizer and effort-level caveats show the effective saving depends on workload.

**Open-source agents are becoming enterprise businesses.** Nous's round shows investors paying for governance layers (SSO, audit, SLAs) around a free agent, the same pattern as the OpenClaw Enterprise launch we covered on October 1.

**Evidence still lags announcements.** Both the model and funding stories come from the companies or secondhand reports, and the deployment evidence is thin again today.
