## 1. Frontier Model News

### Gemini 4 Argon is now generally available, updating the October 1 announcement that limited it to cyber defenders
What changed: on October 13 Google opened Gemini 4 Argon to paying developers and enterprises through the Gemini API (identifier `gemini-4-argon`), and to AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. Earlier coverage said broader access was "rolling out soon"; this is that rollout. The $2 per million input and $10 per million output token price is introductory, and Google's footnote, as quoted by several outlets, says it doubles to $4/$20 after November 30. Sources disagree on which subscriber tier gets AI Studio and the Antigravity app and CLI (Ultra only, per most), and one outlet citing Latent Space describes the $2/$10 as a 50% discount with no announced end date. *(Sources: VentureBeat, Cryptopolitan, Pasquale Pillitteri, Developers Digest, October 13–14)*

Independent standing is unchanged from our earlier note: Argon ranked third on Artificial Analysis behind Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, so Google's own claim of leading 13 of 19 benchmarks remains company-reported. We could not open Google's blog or pricing page, so the footnote date and tier details are second-hand.

### The EU AI Office's first AI Act information requests are being reported as reaching the big labs, but recipients are not confirmed
Reports say the Commission's AI Office sent formal information requests to more than 30 general-purpose-model providers, its first use of enforcement powers under the AI Act. Questions reportedly cover cybersecurity and physical-infrastructure safeguards, access for external evaluators, post-release monitoring and training-content summaries. Several outlets name OpenAI, Anthropic and Google as likely recipients, but spokesperson Thomas Regnier said only that the dialogue with companies continues, and the Commission has not published the list. Fines for refusing or misleading responses can reach €15 million or 3% of global revenue. Some of this coverage appears to date from early October, so this may be a continuation rather than a fresh development. *(Sources: Cloud Security Alliance research note, Techstrong, Nemko Digital, Tech Jack Solutions, October 1–15)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Researchers say an AI agent fleet scraped Alibaba's Amap for a week, with no confirmed owner
Swarmchasers, an independent research group, reported preliminarily that a fleet of agents ran through Tencent Cloud in Hong Kong and queried Alibaba's Amap for entrance data on parks, museums, zoos and hospitals across China. Scans reportedly began September 28 and peaked on October 4 with 1,810 reports; one analysis says the agents generated anti-bot tokens and varied the same request about 1,100 times. 211 outputs carried a "claude" label, but the researchers did not take that at face value, and technical fingerprints pointed toward Tencent's Hunyuan and Zhipu's GLM models. The researchers say the evidence does not establish who deployed the agents, so neither Tencent, Alibaba nor any lab should be blamed. *(Sources: TechCrunch relaying Swarmchasers' preliminary report, corroborated by Crypto Briefing, Let's Data Science, Runtime Wire, October 14–15)*

## Key Themes

**A "reversal" this time is a rollout, not a retraction.** Argon moved from restricted preview to general availability, but the independent ranking and the headline price both carry caveats: Google's benchmarks are its own and the price is temporary.

**Regulators are asking before they are fining.** The EU's opening move is information requests on security and evaluator access, which fits the week's wider concern about models and agents operating beyond their owners' control.

**Attribution is the weak point of agent-era incidents.** The Amap episode shows agent traffic with misleading model labels and no identified operator, a problem for both site owners and the labs whose names get attached.
