## 1. Frontier Model News

### Mistral opened a public preview of Large 4, a 1.05-trillion-parameter model whose open weights are promised later this month
On October 6, Mistral made Large 4 available in preview through Mistral Studio and its API. Per coverage of the launch, it is a multimodal mixture-of-experts model with about 1.05 trillion total and 49 billion active parameters, and Mistral's documentation lists a one-million-token context window with structured outputs, function calling and built-in tools. Mistral calls it open-weight, but only the hosted preview exists today: reports differ on the download date (one says October 27, another says it follows roughly three weeks of safety testing, and a French outlet says a less-restricted version is being tested by security experts and authorities first). Benchmark figures are Mistral's own and unverified; one outlet reports 62% on DeepSWE 1.1 against 61% for GLM-5.3, while GLM-5.3 keeps a wide lead on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (40 versus 28). Sources also disagree on the parameter count (1.05 trillion versus 1,000 billion). It follows Reflection AI's Beam, which also promised weights later this month, so two large open-weight releases now hinge on delayed downloads. *(Sources: Let's Data Science, Just Geek, Cryptoast, October 6)*

### Claude Haiku 5.5 is announced but not released, and leaked claims about it are unsupported
Anthropic's September 22 Opus 5.5 post said Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 would follow "in the coming weeks"; Sonnet shipped September 28. A model tracker and an aggregator logged Haiku 5.5 as shipping October 7 at "75% lower cost," but a search for primary confirmation found only an announcement of arrival, with no pricing, benchmarks or date, and a placeholder date of October 22 in one database. Leak coverage claiming it beats Opus has no official backing. We are treating it as not yet released. *(Sources: Gradually.ai model page, DataLearner, Pasquale Pillitteri, aiweekly.co, October 7–8)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### ServiceNow launched AI Workflow Factory and Autonomous Engineer to find, build and keep improving AI workflows
Announced October 6 at ServiceNow's World Forum in India, per Inc42, AI Workflow Factory combines process discovery, AI-assisted development and workflow execution into one loop, so companies move from isolated AI pilots to continuous improvement. Autonomous Engineer handles development, described as "unattended coding" for planning, building and testing implementation work while developers keep control of critical decisions. InfoWorld and CIO analysts stress that the value depends on running costs and whether savings persist after a workflow reaches production. We did not reach ServiceNow's own press release, so pricing, availability and customer results are unconfirmed, and nothing here is independently measured. *(Sources: Inc42, InfoWorld, CIO, aiagentsdirectory.com, October 6–7)*

### Google DeepMind released EmbeddingGemma 2, a small open embedding model for text, code, images, video and audio
Per a single aggregator brief, the October 7 release is a 740M-parameter, Apache 2.0 model for local multimodal retrieval. We found no second source and did not open the model card, so size and licence are as relayed. *(Sources: AI Agents News Brief via aiagentsdirectory.com, October 7)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Deployment evidence this week is mostly vendor stories and trackers, with few audited outcomes
We found no new named-customer case study dated within the window. The strongest recent figures are older and vendor-reported: Cars24 said in a July OpenAI customer story that agents handle over a million conversation minutes a month, with a 50% higher support resolution rate and 80% faster turnaround. A September tracker of 57 operations deployments counted 35 in production or with results, 19 with a reported outcome and 4 halted or reversed, and a McKinsey 2026 survey puts organizations scaling agents in at least one function at 40%, up from 27%. These are context for how much is verified, not new October evidence. *(Sources: aiweekly.co operations tracker, McKinsey State of AI 2026, OpenAI customer story via search summary, October 8)*

## Key Themes

**Open weights, closed timelines.** Mistral and Reflection AI both announced huge open-weight models whose downloads are weeks away, gated by safety testing. Until weights land, benchmark claims can't be checked outside the labs.

**Vendors sell the loop, not the model.** ServiceNow's pitch is continuous discovery-to-deployment, with humans approving key decisions, the same design seen in this week's bank deployments.

**Unconfirmed releases are piling up.** Haiku 5.5 shows how aggregator logs can run ahead of company confirmation; we held it back until Anthropic ships.
