## 1. Frontier Model News

### OpenAI published 722 math manuscripts from an unreleased internal model, with Lean proofs for many
On October 6, OpenAI released a GitHub repository (github.com/openai/math, Apache 2.0) holding 722 manuscripts grouped into 372 families across pure mathematics, theoretical computer science and mathematical physics. Per coverage of OpenAI's announcement, the model was posed roughly 4,000 problems and each published result used about three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking compute; many of the proofs have also been formalized in Lean, which checks arguments mechanically. The model is unnamed and unreleased, described only as an internal frontier system still going through safety work. The scale is the new element: OpenAI's August Astra announcement covered ten problems, and this release is roughly seventy times larger. The counts, compute figures and "new result" framing are OpenAI's own as relayed by trade outlets; we did not audit the repository, and Lean formalization covers only some proofs, so the remainder rely on human referees. Mathematicians were already disputing OpenAI's attribution and presentation practices after the August release, so expect scrutiny of novelty claims. *(Sources: OpenAI announcement via Unite.AI, MLQ, FourWeekMBA, Dealroom, explainx.ai, October 6)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Anthropic folded Project Glasswing into a three-tier Cyber Verification Program
Announced October 6, the expanded program gives verified security professionals access to Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Mythos 5.1 and future models with reduced blocking classifiers. Defense Access covers incident response, malware analysis and vulnerability validation; Red Team Access adds authorized penetration testing, is limited to organizations, and reviews take "a few weeks"; Specialized Access covers critical-infrastructure testing (power grids, flight systems) and requires in-depth collaboration with the U.S. government. Anthropic says Glasswing partners found at least 129,000 verified vulnerabilities between April and July, with the true figure "at least five times higher," and that more than 33,000 were rated critical or high. On its CyScenarioBench evaluation, Defense Access blocked 46 of 50 tasks while Red Team Access blocked none and succeeded on 34 of 50. Every number here comes from Anthropic, including the safeguard test, which it designed and ran itself. Organizations apply through Anthropic's portal. *(Sources: Anthropic announcement, SiliconANGLE, Unite.AI, Cybersecurity News, Quartz, October 6)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Five big banks showed production agents at Sibos, all keeping a human on the release decision
Per a Sibos 2026 recap, BNY, BNP Paribas, Deutsche Bank, HSBC and Citi demonstrated agents working payment repair and trade exceptions. The pattern is consistent: a model proposes the fix, deterministic scheme rules (or ICC rules) validate it, and a person approves anything that moves money. Finastra's Repair Recommendations, launched September 30, follows the same design, checking suggested fixes against Swift, Fedwire, SEPA, UPI and Nexus rules with final action left to human approval. None of the described deployments lets a model release a payment unsupervised. This is a single secondary recap of conference demos rather than audited deployment data, and we found no throughput or error-rate figures. Separately, PYMNTS reports bank AI job postings are up 49% this year and "agent orchestration" mentions up 1,721% from a small base of 108, with governance skills outnumbering model-building roughly 2 to 1; that is a hiring-data inference, not direct evidence of deployed scale. *(Sources: Beri.net Sibos recap, Finastra release via Webull, PYMNTS, October 2–6)*

## Key Themes

**Scale is the new claim, verification the old problem.** OpenAI's jump from ten proofs to 722 manuscripts makes the Lean formalization share the number to watch; until outside mathematicians check the rest, it is a company-reported output.

**Access is being tiered rather than gated.** Anthropic's program pairs stronger capability with stricter vetting, putting the hardest cases under government review. That mirrors the human-in-the-loop designs banks are shipping.

**Humans keep the last click where money or infrastructure is at stake.** From payment release to power-grid testing, the common design is model proposes, rules check, person decides.
