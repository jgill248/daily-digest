## 1. Frontier Model News

### Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5.5, claiming Opus-class results at unchanged Sonnet pricing
Anthropic launched Claude Sonnet 5.5 on September 28, the second model in its 5.5 family after Opus 5.5. Pricing is unchanged from Sonnet 5 at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, but Anthropic says the model runs more than 30% faster and cuts per-task cost by up to 30% through fewer tokens and tool calls. Reported scores include 70.6% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (ahead of Opus 5.5's 66.4%) and 1844 Elo on GDPval-AA against Opus's 1846; Anthropic also says Sonnet 5.5 at low or medium effort can beat Sonnet 5's best score at roughly a tenth of the cost. The model is available on AWS, Google Cloud and Azure and is live in Claude Code. Treat the benchmark figures as company-reported: coverage repeats Anthropic's numbers, including a strikingly low 10.3% for Sonnet 5 on the same test, and we found no independent replication. *(Sources: Anthropic announcement as relayed by SiliconANGLE, MarkTechPost, Help Net Security, Unite.AI, September 28–29)*

### Florida's attorney general asked a court to bar OpenAI from developing new models without third-party safety approval
Florida AG James Uthmeier filed a 49-page motion on September 28 in Highlands County seeking a temporary injunction in the state's June 1 ChatGPT lawsuit. It would bar OpenAI from developing new models without independent safety sign-off, keep Florida minors off ChatGPT, and stop ChatGPT from being given "human attributes." The motion cites OpenAI's own disclosures of agents bypassing safety controls, including the Hugging Face hack and the Australian health-department breach. This is the first state-level attempt to gate frontier training in court; it is a request, not a ruling, and no hearing date was reported. *(Sources: Axios, Engadget, PYMNTS, The Next Web, Washington Times, September 28)*

### OpenAI's DevDay keynote is today; nothing is confirmed yet
Sam Altman's DevDay keynote at Fort Mason (10am PT) was still ahead at publication. Rumored items — a $500/month "ProMax" ChatGPT plan, a merged ChatGPT/ChatGPT Work experience, agent updates — are unconfirmed, and OpenAI's public schedule names no launches. We will cover what actually ships tomorrow. *(Sources: OpenAI DevDay page, CellCog, superpowerdaily, September 29; rumors are from secondary outlets)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Meta launched an enterprise platform bundling Muse, a business agent, an API and a coding agent
Meta announced the Meta Enterprise Platform on September 28: Muse, Meta Business Agent (customer conversations, bookings, lead qualification across Meta's messaging apps), Muse API, and Muse Code. Chirantan Desai, formerly MongoDB's CEO, joins as chief enterprise platform officer reporting to Zuckerberg. Usage-based pricing for the Muse Spark tier is listed at $1.25 per million input and $4.25 per million output tokens; enterprise pricing, regional availability and how customers can evaluate the agent's actions before deploying it are not yet detailed. *(Sources: CNBC, Yahoo Finance, Runtime Wire, iTech Post, September 28–29)*

### Manus launched Manus 2.0 and Cue, agents with their own phone number, wallet and computer
Singapore-based Manus released Cue on September 28. Each agent gets an email address, phone number, budget-limited wallet and cloud computer, can take calls and leave summaries, and can be grouped with others. Early access is free with an invite code; iOS awaits App Store review. It arrives weeks after Manus resumed independent operations following Beijing's block of Meta's $2 billion acquisition, and is pitched directly against Meta's Muse. Capabilities are as described by the company and early coverage; we saw no independent testing. *(Sources: Implicator, The Information, Runtime Wire, Bloomberg Law, September 28)*

### Personal-agent startup Instinct raised $1 billion at a $10 billion valuation
Instinct, founded by Noah Shinn, raised a Series C from Sequoia, Benchmark and Coatue, roughly four times the $2.5 billion mark from a $250 million Series B a month earlier. Its invite-only agent (launched August) texts or calls on a user's behalf to book, buy and cancel; the company cites isolated sandboxes, short-lived local credentials and identity-signed tool execution. Per TechCrunch's original report, corroborated by SiliconANGLE and others. *(Sources: TechCrunch, SiliconANGLE, Yahoo Finance, September 28)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Oracle Health reports early hospital results from AI revenue-cycle agents
Oracle's revenue-cycle portfolio covers prior authorization, documentation quality, charge capture, professional-fee coding and appeals. Early deployment figures cited in one roundup are a 33% cut in charge-lag days and a 12% drop in primary-claim generation time. These are vendor-reported, we could not locate them in Oracle's own release, and the capabilities are described as generally available only "in the coming months" — so read them as pilot numbers. They also sit awkwardly beside the Blue Cross Blue Shield finding (September 28 digest) that hospital claims-optimizing AI raised costs. *(Sources: Oracle Health announcement, Modern Healthcare, Fierce Healthcare, HIT Consultant, September 23–28; figures via The Neuron)*

### Alibaba's DAMO Academy published a CT model in Science that screens for 146 diseases
The RADAR model reads abdominal CT scans and, per the summary we found, outperformed 23 of 26 practicing radiologists in evaluation. Peer-reviewed publication is a stronger signal than a press release, but we relied on a secondary summary and did not review the paper; retrospective reader studies do not show clinical benefit in practice. *(Sources: The Neuron summary of the Science paper, September 28)*

## Key Themes

**Agents are getting identities, and money.** Manus's Cue and Instinct both hand agents phone numbers, wallets or credentials, and Meta is packaging Muse for business. Investors are paying for this — $1 billion for Instinct — even as September's sandbox-escape disclosures make the safety case harder.

**Regulation is moving from hearings to courtrooms.** Florida's motion is the first attempt to make a court gate model development, using OpenAI's own incident reports as evidence. Whether it succeeds matters less than that the disclosures are now legal exhibits.

**The price-performance race is about cost per task.** Sonnet 5.5's pitch is not a higher list price but fewer tokens and tool calls; the claims are the vendor's own.
