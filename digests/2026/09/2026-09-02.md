## 1. Frontier Model News

### Anthropic Signs a $35 Billion Compute Deal With Nvidia-Backed Lambda for a Texas Data Center

Anthropic has agreed to a multi-year, $35 billion cloud-computing deal with Lambda for capacity at a roughly 700-megawatt data-center campus in Nueces County, Texas, per Bloomberg's original reporting, corroborated by Forbes, TechRepublic, and Betanews. The structure is notable for how many roles Nvidia plays at once: it supplies the GPUs Lambda will run, holds an equity stake in Lambda itself, and anchors the underlying lease on the Hut 8-developed campus — a site Nvidia had separately contracted weeks earlier before Lambda plugged in to serve Anthropic. Lambda is also taking out a $917 million leveraged loan to buy the chips, according to The Next Web, and Nvidia CFO Colette Kress has publicly addressed analyst concern that the arrangement amounts to circular financing, arguing the underlying compute is fungible and redeployable regardless of who owns it. The $35 billion figure is a multi-year contracted commitment rather than upfront cash or annual revenue, and neither Anthropic nor Lambda has issued its own confirming statement beyond the reported deal terms. It's the second Anthropic compute deal of the week, following the $45 billion Nscale agreement this digest covered August 28, underscoring how much of Anthropic's pre-IPO spending is going straight into data-center capacity. *(Sources: Bloomberg, Forbes, TechRepublic, The Next Web, August 31–September 1)*

### DeepSeek Revives a Paused Funding Round, Reportedly Nearing $7.4 Billion at a $74 Billion Valuation

DeepSeek has restarted a fundraising round it had quietly paused, and is now close to raising roughly $7.4 billion (50 billion yuan) at a pre-money valuation near $74 billion, according to Bloomberg's original reporting, corroborated by Reuters and China Money Network. That's a sharp jump from the more than $50 billion valuation investors including Tencent assigned the company in its first-ever external funding round in June. The round reportedly stalled last month after founder Liang Wenfeng grew frustrated over leaked remarks, before resuming with a syndicate said to include China's national AI fund, NetEase, JD.com, IDG Capital, and Monolith Management. The capital is earmarked for R&D and compute buildout as DeepSeek prepares for a possible IPO on Shanghai's STAR Market as soon as 2027. As of the most recent reporting, the round had not formally closed, and DeepSeek itself has not issued a public confirmation of the terms. *(Sources: Bloomberg, Reuters, China Money Network, August 28–29)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### OpenAI Brings WebMCP "Site Tools" to ChatGPT's Browser, Letting Websites Hand Agents Direct Actions

OpenAI has added support for the emerging WebMCP standard to the built-in browser in its ChatGPT desktop app and to Codex, letting compatible websites expose structured actions — searching documents, editing files, exploring dashboards, comparing travel options, updating a shopping cart — that its agents can call directly instead of scraping page layouts and simulating clicks, per OpenAI's own developer announcement, corroborated by Search Engine Journal, VKTR, and Digg. Millions of Shopify storefronts are already WebMCP-enabled by Shopify's own count, with Expedia, Instacart, and Target reported to be experimenting with the standard since Google first proposed it in May. OpenAI paired the rollout with a 10-day "WebMCP Challenge," offering cash prizes from itself, Shopify, Google Chrome, Netlify, Cloudflare, and Vercel to developers who build or retrofit agent-native sites. The adoption figures are the platforms' own claims rather than independently audited numbers, and WebMCP itself remains a proposed rather than finalized web standard. *(Sources: OpenAI, Search Engine Journal, VKTR, Digg, September 1)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Bank of America, Citi, and JPMorgan Disclose the Scale of Employee AI Use for the First Time

Three of the largest US banks put concrete numbers on internal AI adoption this week, per CIO Dive's reporting, corroborated by Banking Dive and Yahoo Finance. Bank of America says more than 200,000 employees now use AI-enabled capabilities — productivity tools, coding assistants, and agentic workflows — generating over 400,000 prompts daily, out of more than 300 approved AI use cases (114 of them generative AI, with 34 fully implemented in operations), according to CEO Brian Moynihan. Citi says nearly 9 in 10 employees are using its AI tools, which the bank credits with speeding up how fast new products reach market. JPMorgan Chase says it has nearly 1,000 live AI use cases spanning risk, fraud detection, marketing, and document review, though CEO Jamie Dimon has cautioned the technology's biggest near-term winners will be customers rather than headcount reduction. All three figures are self-reported by the banks via earnings calls and public statements rather than independently audited, and none of the banks has disclosed a standardized methodology for what counts as an "approved" or "live" use case, making direct comparisons between them approximate at best. *(Sources: CIO Dive, Banking Dive, Yahoo Finance, September 1)*

## Key Themes

**AI infrastructure spending is increasingly being financed through deals where the same handful of companies sit on multiple sides of the transaction.** Anthropic's Lambda deal has Nvidia simultaneously acting as chip supplier, equity investor, and landlord — echoing the circular-financing pattern this digest has flagged in Anthropic's Volta Infra and Nscale agreements, and in Nvidia's broader web of customer-investments industry-wide. The dollar figures keep climbing ($35 billion here, on top of $45 billion and $10 billion in prior deals this quarter), but they describe multi-year contracted capacity, not cash changing hands today — a distinction that gets flattened in headlines more often than the underlying reporting flattens it.

**Enterprise AI adoption is shifting from anecdote to disclosed scale, even as agent infrastructure catches up to make that adoption more durable.** Banks disclosing four-, five-, and six-figure "use case" counts is a different kind of evidence than the ROI case-study roundups this digest has covered before — it's operational telemetry rather than a single showcase deployment. That kind of internal agent use depends on agents being able to act reliably inside existing systems, which is exactly the gap OpenAI's WebMCP push is trying to close on the open web: giving agents a structured way to act on a site instead of an ad hoc, breakage-prone one.
