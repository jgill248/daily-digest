## 1. Frontier Model News

### Anthropic Says Claude Now Leads 26% of Its Own AI Research and Development, Up From 1% in March

Anthropic published the first results of a new "R&D Automation Index" on September 17, reporting that Claude models "led" 26% of the company's internal AI research and engineering work as of August, up from roughly 1% in March, per Anthropic's own post, corroborated by Bloomberg's original reporting and Unite.AI and Benzinga. The index, built with an Automation Level scale developed by Epoch AI running from AL0 (no AI involvement) to AL5 (fully autonomous, no human in the loop), measures the full range of R&D tasks at Anthropic rather than any single benchmark. The company stressed that "leading" is not "autonomous": no measured task ran at AL5, and the reported figure describes work where Claude completes most of a task end-to-end from a high-level prompt while a human still supervises and can intervene. Anthropic said more than 90% of its R&D work now falls at or above the "collaborates" level, where Claude does large chunks of work under close human direction, and that roughly 30,000 agent instances were doing research and engineering work as of August. Anthropic did not disclose which specific research tasks Claude leads, how the company verifies the automation-level scores, or whether the 26% figure includes work later reverted or corrected by human researchers. *(Sources: Anthropic, Bloomberg, Unite.AI, Benzinga, September 17)*

### Unsealed Court Filings in NYT's Copyright Suit Allege OpenAI and Microsoft Knowingly Bypassed Paywalls and Stripped Copyright Notices to Train Models

Newly unsealed filings in The New York Times' copyright lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, made public Thursday, quote a January 2023 internal memo in which Microsoft's head of applied science, Brent Hecht, called industry-wide AI training scraping "an astonishing theft of unprecedented proportions" and "the largest theft of labor in human history," per The Washington Post's original reporting, corroborated by TechCrunch and Futurism. The filings allege OpenAI's mid-training datasets contain more than 91,692 copies of articles from the Times, the Daily News, and the Center for Investigative Reporting, and that a Common Crawl-derived dataset included more than 2 million documents from nytimes.com alone; the Times' brief also alleges the companies bypassed the paper's paywall undetected and deliberately stripped copyright notices from scraped text. Among the exchanges cited, an OpenAI researcher told co-founder Greg Brockman about "a hack to get around nytimes paywall," to which Brockman is quoted replying "ah nice." This builds on the Trump administration Justice Department's early-September filing backing OpenAI in the same suit; the new material comes from the Times' own legal brief characterizing internal communications, and neither OpenAI nor Microsoft has publicly responded to the specific quotes or scraping-scale figures as of publication. *(Sources: Washington Post, TechCrunch, Futurism, September 17)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Google Opens Its Home Ecosystem to Outside AI Agents Like Claude Through the Model Context Protocol

Google began early access rollout of "Home MCP" on September 16, letting third-party AI agents — including Anthropic's Claude, Google's own Antigravity, Hermes, and OpenClaw — connect to Google Home devices and event history through the open Model Context Protocol, per Google's Cloud blog announcement, corroborated by Android Authority, TheNextWeb, and Thurrott. At launch, the feature is limited to Google Home Premium Advanced subscribers in the US ($20/month or $200/year) and requires setup through a Google Cloud project; agents that connect through it can analyze footage from Nest doorbells and cameras, control Matter-compatible bulbs and other devices, and build custom natural-language dashboards. The move ends Gemini's exclusive hold on Google's smart-home ecosystem, letting users route commands and analysis through whichever MCP-compatible agent they already use elsewhere. Google has not disclosed adoption numbers, said when Home MCP will leave early access or reach non-Premium tiers, or detailed what device data third-party agents can retain outside the session. *(Sources: Google Cloud, Android Authority, TheNextWeb, Thurrott, September 16)*

### Anthropic Launches Claude for Financial Advisors, Bundling Custodian and Portfolio Connectors From Schwab and BlackRock

Anthropic launched "Claude for Financial Advisors" as a Cowork plugin for Enterprise customers, pairing connectors to nine wealth-industry platforms — including Charles Schwab, BlackRock, Addepar, Envestnet, iCapital, Orion, Wealthbox, Wealth.com, and Zocks — with workflow skills built around an advisor's daily research and client-reporting tasks, per Anthropic's own announcement, corroborated by Forbes' Jon Markman and Unite.AI. Anthropic is recommending the Enterprise plan specifically to registered investment advisers because it includes the audit logs regulators expect for recordkeeping, and the plugin appears alongside Anthropic's broader Cowork plugin browser rollout for enterprise functions. Firms that request a license before the end of September get a one-time usage credit, though Anthropic has not disclosed pricing beyond that promotion, how many advisory firms have signed on, or what compliance review, if any, the partner-platform data connections underwent before launch. *(Sources: Anthropic, Forbes, Unite.AI, September 14–16)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Three Security Researchers Used Claude Opus 5 to Turn an Image Bug Into a Working Breach of OpenAI's Internal Systems

Hacktron AI, a small security research outfit, disclosed this week that its three-person team used Anthropic's newly released Claude Opus 5 to chain a libheif image-parsing flaw in OpenAI's Discourse-based community forum into an exploit that reached OpenAI's internal GitHub environment, per Hacktron's own technical writeup, corroborated by original Wall Street Journal reporting and Fortune, VentureBeat, and Forbes. The team found that HEIC and HEIF images uploaded to the forum bypassed Discourse's standard image checks and were passed to an outdated, unpatched version of ImageMagick's libheif parser, which contained a heap-buffer overflow; an earlier attempt with Anthropic's prior model, Opus 4.8, had produced a working exploit only with memory protections disabled, but Claude Opus 5 produced a working exploit against the protected, real-world configuration within about three hours, which the team then adapted to gain remote code execution. Hacktron reported the flaw to OpenAI, which confirmed a fix roughly 14 hours later; OpenAI paid a $6,500 bug-bounty reward, and Hacktron says it stopped short of examining sensitive source code once access was confirmed. "We're just three guys with Claude and Codex subscriptions," Hacktron's Mohan Pedhapati told the Journal, adding the work took "a few days for an agent, and just a few hours of human time." OpenAI has not detailed what other systems, if any, were reachable through the same access chain, and the account of what data was or wasn't viewed rests on Hacktron's own description of its conduct rather than independent audit. *(Sources: Hacktron AI, Wall Street Journal, Fortune, VentureBeat, Forbes, September 18)*

## Key Themes

**AI models are increasingly being turned on the industry that builds them.** In the same week Anthropic disclosed that Claude now leads a quarter of its own successor's development, a three-person team used a rival Anthropic model to break into OpenAI's internal systems in a matter of hours — and Google chose to let outside agents like Claude, rather than only its own Gemini, run its home ecosystem. Capability is compounding faster than any one company's walls around it.

**Unsealed allegations of paywall-bypassing and copyright-stripping landed the same week AI agents got a deeper hold on people's finances and homes.** The contrast is stark: courts are only now surfacing internal admissions about how today's frontier models were trained, even as Claude for Financial Advisors asks wealth managers to trust connectors into client portfolios and Home MCP asks homeowners to trust an agent with camera footage and device control.

**Anthropic's own numbers illustrate how quickly the ground is shifting underneath oversight.** Claude's "leading" share of Anthropic's R&D work went from roughly 1% to 26% in six months, and it took Claude Opus 5 about three hours to solve an exploit problem that had stumped researchers using its predecessor for months. Governance and disclosure frameworks — like the incident-reporting system OpenAI rolled out this week — are being built for a pace of capability gain that keeps outrunning them.
