# Daily AI Digest — September 3, 2026

## 1. Frontier Model News

### Trump Administration's DOJ Backs OpenAI in Its Copyright Fight With The New York Times
The Justice Department filed a statement of interest in Manhattan federal court on Wednesday supporting OpenAI's fair-use defense against the Times, per Bloomberg Law's original reporting, corroborated by TheWrap, ABC News, US News, and Deadline. It's the first time the US government has weighed in on the wave of copyright suits publishers, authors, and music labels have brought against AI companies over training data. The brief argues that training models on copyrighted text can qualify as fair use, and that ruling for the Times "would be inconsistent with basic copyright law principles" and would undercut President Trump's January 2025 executive order aimed at sustaining US dominance in AI — citing national security and economic competitiveness as government interests. The Times has pushed back publicly on the administration's intervention, per Deadline's coverage, though the filing does not bind the court's eventual ruling. The DOJ's brief is legal argument, not a factual finding, and several outlets frame it as unusually direct political backing for a single company in active litigation rather than a neutral statement of policy. *(Sources: Bloomberg Law, TheWrap, ABC News, US News, Deadline, September 2)*

### Anthropic Releases Claude Fable 5.1 and a Restricted Mythos 5.1, Cutting Cache Costs 75%
Anthropic launched Fable 5.1 on September 1, per its own announcement, corroborated by Bloomberg, TechCrunch, VentureBeat, 9to5Mac, MacRumors, and AWS's release notes. The model keeps Fable 5's headline pricing of $10/$50 per million input/output tokens but cuts the cost of cache reads from $1.00 to $0.25 per million tokens — a 75% reduction Anthropic says lowers typical billed workloads by about 25% and complex agentic ones by up to 45%. On Anthropic's own benchmarks, Fable 5.1 scores 55.8% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 versus 42.0% for Fable 5, and 52.6% on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1, more than double its predecessor. It ships with a 1-million-token context window and 128,000-token max output, and is available immediately in Claude.ai, Claude Code, and Cowork, via Anthropic's API, and on AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure. Mythos 5.1 is the same underlying model without Anthropic's standard production safeguards, offered only through restricted-access programs to vetted cybersecurity and life-sciences organizations. The benchmark figures are Anthropic's own reported numbers rather than independently reproduced results. *(Sources: Anthropic, Bloomberg, TechCrunch, VentureBeat, AWS, September 1)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Google Ships Gemini 3.8 Flash, Its Fourth Flash-Tier Model in Four Months
Google released Gemini 3.8 Flash on September 2, first reported by 9to5Google and corroborated by The Register, Artificial Analysis, and DataCamp. The model holds 3.7 Flash's pricing of $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output tokens — a rate Google says stays in effect through December 31 before doubling to $1.50/$7.50 on January 1, 2027 — while winning 8 of the 14 benchmark rows in Google's own comparison table against its predecessor, including a jump to 90.8% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (from 81.6%) and 73.7% on the long-horizon coding benchmark DeepSWE v1.1. Google also released a restricted "Cyber" variant, available only through its Fairwind Program for vetted defenders, which it says exceeds a 70% real-world vulnerability-discovery rate. The Register frames the release as Google reasserting pace against Anthropic and OpenAI in a segment where it had been lagging; all benchmark comparisons come from Google's own published tables rather than third-party leaderboards. *(Sources: 9to5Google, The Register, Artificial Analysis, DataCamp, September 2)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### OpenAI Connects ChatGPT Health to Epic's Patient Records for Clinicians at Major Hospital Systems
OpenAI announced on September 1 that ChatGPT Health now has a read-only integration with Epic, the electronic health record system used for more than 325 million patients in the US, per TechCrunch's reporting, corroborated by PYMNTS, FierceHealthcare, and Becker's Hospital Review. The integration lets clinicians pull authorized appointment notes, lab results, medications, and specialist documentation into ChatGPT and ask questions across a patient's record, with the option to embed ChatGPT directly inside the EHR layout so clinicians don't leave the patient chart. OpenAI paired it with a new Healthcare Public Data plugin connecting ChatGPT to nine reference sources, including ClinicalTrials.gov, CMS Coverage, RxNorm, DailyMed, and PubMed. Access is explicitly read-only: OpenAI says ChatGPT cannot write to the record, place orders, or alter clinical documentation through the integration, and the product is built to HIPAA requirements for organizations with a signed business associate agreement. Launch partners include AdventHealth, Baylor Scott & White Health, Boston Children's Hospital, Cedars-Sinai, HCA Healthcare, Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, and UCSF Health. The safety constraints and HIPAA-compliance claims are OpenAI's own representations; none of the launch partners has published independent data yet on clinician usage or outcomes. *(Sources: TechCrunch, PYMNTS, FierceHealthcare, Becker's Hospital Review, September 1)*

## Key Themes

**Frontier labs are competing on price and iteration speed as much as on flagship capability.** Fable 5.1 and Gemini 3.8 Flash landed within a day of each other, and neither is a from-scratch flagship — both are incremental updates that hold or cut per-token pricing (Anthropic via a 75% cache-read discount, Google via a pricing freeze through year-end) while pushing coding and agentic benchmarks. Google's release is its fourth Flash-tier model in four months, a cadence that reads as a deliberate response to Anthropic and OpenAI rather than a scheduled major release.

**The copyright fight over AI training data is escalating on both sides of the table at once.** Days after Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell sued Anthropic over alleged piracy (covered here August 31), the US government filed its first-ever brief in the opposite direction — backing OpenAI's fair-use defense in the industry's oldest and highest-profile training-data suit. Together they underline that the legal question of whether training on copyrighted material is fair use remains genuinely unsettled, with billions of dollars and now the executive branch's stated policy priorities riding on the outcome.
