## 1. Frontier Model News

### Trump Publicly Rebukes Amodei, Rejects AI Guardrails as a "SICK Conspiracy"

President Trump singled out Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei by name in a Truth Social post on September 14, writing that his administration "has stopped AI 'people' from doing bad, or potentially bad, 'things,' like Dario (Anthropic!), who is now pretending to be a 'perfect little angel,'" per Axios, corroborated by CNBC, NBC News, Al Jazeera, and The Hill. Trump wrote that "the only control or 'guardrails' that AI needs is a STRONG AND SMART (High IQ!) PRESIDENT, and the U.S.A. has that, in spades," claimed the administration already holds "tremendous CRIMINAL and REGULATORY power over these companies," and described opposition to AI and data centers as a "SICK conspiracy" that benefits China. The post is a direct response to Amodei's September 12 essay "We Must Pace the Frontier," which argued frontier labs should deliberately slow capability gains — a call that drew same-day backing from Sam Altman, Elon Musk, and Satya Nadella, as this digest reported September 14. Where that day's story was a rare show of unity among lab CEOs, the new development is the administration's first direct, personal pushback on one of them, surfacing a split between the White House's deregulatory posture and the labs' own public safety pledges. The post is Trump's own statement on his own platform; neither Anthropic nor Amodei has publicly responded, and no policy or regulatory action has followed it yet. *(Sources: Axios, CNBC, NBC News, Al Jazeera, The Hill, September 14)*

### Google Breaks Its Own Rule, Gives All Engineers Access to Anthropic's Claude Opus 5

Google has opened access to Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 to engineers company-wide through its internal Antigravity development platform, ending a longstanding policy that had confined outside coding tools like Claude Code and OpenAI's Codex to a small number of DeepMind teams and high-priority projects, per Business Insider's Hugh Langley, corroborated by BigGo Finance and Cryptopolitan. Claude is available with per-employee usage quotas and is positioned as a complement rather than a replacement, with Google telling Business Insider that "Gemini remains our primary and foundational model for internal development." The move follows persistent internal complaints from engineers about Gemini's coding performance relative to Claude, and comes as Anthropic and Google remain commercial rivals in the same frontier-model market. Google has not disclosed how many engineers have taken up Claude access since the policy changed or what share of internal coding work now runs through it. *(Sources: Business Insider, BigGo Finance, Cryptopolitan, September 15)*

## 2. New AI Tools and Software Frameworks

### Perplexity Brings Its Local-First "Portable Computer" Agent to Windows RTX PCs

Perplexity extended Portable Computer, the on-device AI agent it introduced for Mac in late August, to Windows via the Microsoft Store, requiring an Nvidia GeForce RTX or RTX PRO GPU with at least 24GB of VRAM, per Perplexity's own announcement, corroborated by Tom's Hardware, The New Stack, and Heise. The agent runs its harness, orchestrator, and scheduler entirely on-device using a locally optimized Qwen 3.8 27B model, consuming zero cloud credits for tasks it can complete locally; when a task needs fresher information or a more capable model, it runs a PII classifier over the outgoing context, shows the user exactly what would leave the machine, and asks permission before calling out to Perplexity's search index or one of more than 15 partner frontier models, which Perplexity says never get access to local files or tools. The feature requires a Perplexity Pro or Max subscription and is limited to a narrow slice of high-end Nvidia hardware — cards like the RTX 3090, 4090, and 5090 qualify, but most consumer laptops and older GPUs do not. Perplexity has not published data on how many users have adopted Portable Computer or how often tasks stay fully local versus escalating to the cloud. *(Sources: Perplexity, Tom's Hardware, The New Stack, Heise, September 14–15)*

## 3. Real-World AI Implementation

### Anthropic Says Its Own Engineers Now Ship 8x More Code a Quarter, Straining Its Test Infrastructure Three Times Over

Anthropic disclosed in an engineering blog post that its continuous-integration job volume grew 25-fold over six months as engineers began shipping roughly eight times more code per quarter than the company's 2021–2025 average, with Claude now authoring about 80% of newly merged code, per Anthropic's own post, corroborated by VentureBeat and Analytics India Magazine. The surge — the same underlying shift this digest covered in May, now with updated internal figures — overwhelmed the test-impact-analysis service that decides which tests must run for each change; Anthropic says it patched the service three separate times as load grew, with each fix holding for a shorter stretch (70 days, then 29 days, then under a day) before engineers redesigned the system rather than patching it again, while the size of its test suite grew roughly tenfold with only a modest increase in headcount. The account is Anthropic's own description of its internal infrastructure and is not independently audited; the company did not disclose current headcount figures or say whether the redesigned system has itself been tested at further scale. *(Sources: Anthropic, VentureBeat, Analytics India Magazine, September 15)*

### GSA Swaps OpenAI's $1-a-Year Government Deal for Usage-Based Pricing as OPM Recommits to ChatGPT

The General Services Administration announced a new 27-month OneGov agreement with OpenAI, effective October 1, that replaces the outgoing $1-per-year flat-fee ChatGPT deal with consumption-based pricing: eligible federal, state, local, and tribal agencies pay no platform-access fee (normally $15 per user per month) and get a 50% discount on token-based usage, with no minimum spend commitment, per GSA's own release, corroborated by Nextgov/FCW and FedScoop. The Office of Personnel Management said it will continue deploying ChatGPT under the renewed terms, one of the first agencies to confirm it is staying on the new pricing structure rather than reassessing usage now that costs scale with actual consumption instead of a fixed nominal fee. GSA has not published projected total costs to agencies under the new structure or said how many agencies beyond OPM have committed to continuing service past October 1. *(Sources: GSA, Nextgov/FCW, FedScoop, September 10)*

## Key Themes

**A single essay calling for restraint produced two opposite reactions within 48 hours.** Amodei's pacing call drew immediate, unified backing from rival CEOs on September 12–13, but by September 14 the White House had turned it into a personal target — the clearest sign yet that "pace the frontier" is a corporate talking point without government buy-in, not an emerging consensus.

**Even as labs trade public blows, their tools keep crossing rival lines inside engineering orgs.** Google handing its own engineers Claude access, on the same platform where Gemini is supposed to be "primary," shows how far commercial rivalry has diverged from what individual teams will actually use to ship code — echoed by Anthropic's own admission that its infrastructure is straining to keep up with how much of that code Claude itself now writes.

**The frontier-vs-guardrails fight is playing out in Washington, but the more concrete AI story this week is procurement.** GSA's shift from a symbolic $1-a-year ChatGPT deal to real, usage-based pricing — alongside Perplexity's bet that some users will pay to keep AI processing off the cloud entirely — suggests the industry's next fight isn't about capability thresholds, but about who actually pays for inference and where it runs.
